# External ear

> The auricle is one plate of elastic cartilage (helix, Y-shaped antihelix with two crura, tragus, antitragus, concha) under thin, tightly bound skin, plus a cartilage-free lobule. Its relief is crisp and individual enough to serve as a biometric, so a generic template ear visibly weakens likeness. Harvested adult means put length at about 57–63 mm and width at about 29–33 mm (width ≈ 0.47–0.54 × length), males longer, and length keeps creeping up through adult life. The ear hangs from the skull at the ear canal, so its height is best anchored to the Frankfort horizontal (tragion ≈ orbitale level) rather than to the "brow to nose base" canon, which is only approximate; its long axis leans back roughly 10–30°, and protrusion norms disagree. In photo fitting, perspective, head pitch, the ear's outward flare and hair occlusion cause larger errors than anatomical variation does.
>
> Evidence status: 3 ✅ / 27 📄 / 50 🧠 / 21 ↔️ — 🧠 items are queued for web verification.

## Key facts

[1] and [2] are both StatPearls content, so together they count as one source (sourced, not verified). No independent verification pass has run on this note yet; nothing has been refuted.

**Structure**
- **Tissue.** One continuous elastic-cartilage plate gives the shape; the lobule is soft and fleshy with no cartilage [1][2] 📄. Front (lateral) skin is thin and adherent with almost no fat, so folds read sharply; back (cranial) skin is looser and its relief softer 🧠.
- **Helix.** Outer posterosuperior rim, curving inward toward the meatus [1][2] 📄. It begins as the crus (root) of the helix inside the concha above the canal, climbs the front edge, arcs over the top and runs down the back into the lobule. The free edge is usually rolled forward over the scapha (the groove inside the rim), and roll width varies a lot. Darwin's tubercle, a nodule on the posterosuperior rim, is common, but reported prevalence varies widely 🧠.
- **Antihelix.** Y-shaped ridge rising from the antitragus that separates concha, triangular fossa and scapha [1][2] 📄. Correction to the earlier draft: the two crura enclose the *triangular fossa*; the superior crus is broad and soft, the inferior crus sharper and also the upper wall of the cymba conchae 🧠.
- **Concha.** Hollow beside the canal, bounded by tragus, intertragal notch, antitragus, antihelix, inferior crus and helical root; the meatus opens into it [1][2] 📄. The helical crus splits it into a small upper cymba and a larger, deeper lower cavum; it is the deepest pit of the ear, very roughly 10–20 mm 🧠.
- **Tragus / antitragus.** Tragus: projection in front of the canal that partly covers it; antitragus: smaller opposing projection forming the lower conchal boundary and back wall of the intertragal notch [1][2] 📄. The mandibular condyle sits directly in front of the tragus; tragion (notch at the upper margin of the tragus) is the soft-tissue stand-in for bony porion 🧠.
- **Why ears protrude.** An under-folded antihelix and an over-deep concha are the two classic causes, often combined; prominence is commonly quoted at about 5% of people of European descent 🧠 ↔️.

**Size, sex, age**
- **Length and width.** Means about 57.6–62.9 mm (length) and 28.8–32.8 mm (width) [3][5][16]; a review puts male means at 59.5–68.5 mm [17] ↔️ (a spread across populations and methods, not one disputed value).
- **Shape ratio.** W/L ≈ 0.47–0.54 from those means (this note's arithmetic) [3][5][16] 📄.
- **Sex.** Males have longer ears in North Indian, Central Indian, Saudi paediatric and Sundanese samples [3][9][12][16] ✅. Width difference is inconsistent: significant in [9][12], not in Eastern Saudi adults or a Turkish sample [10][13] ↔️. Lobule: larger in males in [9], no difference in [10][13] ↔️. Absolute length and width discriminate sex better than indices [13] 📄.
- **Growth.** Length reaches mature size by about 14 in a Chinese sample [11] 📄, then keeps increasing slowly (Heathcote's cross-sectional estimate is 0.22 mm/year [18]), largely via lobule elongation, so a 70-year-old's ears are typically several mm to about 1 cm longer than at 25 📄 ↔️. This conflicts with the "steady until ~70" claim relayed by [14].

**Position and orientation**
- **Skull anchor.** The ear canal is fixed in the temporal bone, so ear height follows the skull: with the head in Frankfort horizontal (FH), tragion lies at about the level of the lower orbital rim, and the tragus sits near mid-height of the auricle 🧠.
- **Brow / nose-base canon.** "Top at brow level, bottom at nose base" holds roughly on average because brow-to-subnasale distance is close to ear length. The related neoclassical "nasoaural" canon (ear length = nose height nasion–subnasale) mostly failed in Farkas's tests, since ears are usually about 5–12 mm longer than the nose 🧠 ↔️. Individual offsets of 5–10 mm are normal, and lobes sit lower with age 🧠.
- **Axis tilt.** The long axis leans back roughly 10–30° from vertical (typical ~20°) in FH. "Ear axis parallel to the nasal dorsum" held in only a minority of Farkas's subjects; the ear is usually more upright 🧠 ↔️.
- **Front-back and flare.** The helical root sits roughly one ear length behind the lateral canthus (the microtia-reconstruction placement rule) 🧠. The ear plane faces forward-outward, flared very roughly 20–35° from the sagittal plane, so a true profile foreshortens ear width 🧠. Deformational plagiocephaly can shift one ear forward by several mm 🧠.
- **Methods differ** for length, protrusion, inclination and ear level (projection extremes vs superaurale–subaurale, ruler, photogrammetry, 3D), so raw numbers do not transfer between studies [8][16][17] ✅.

**Protrusion**
- Prominent if upper protrusion >21.5 mm or lower >20.0 mm (boys), or >17.5 / >15.5 mm (girls); the authors argue for sex-specific criteria [7] 📄.
- Auriculocephalic angle: normal 20–30° in one summary, <25° with ≤20 mm projection from the mastoid in another [8] ↔️.
- McDowell's otoplasty goals (helix–mastoid 10–12 / 16–18 / 20–22 mm by third; helix visible beyond the antihelix from the front; sides within 3 mm) are surgical targets, not population means 🧠; [7] calls the 3 mm criterion too strict 📄.
- Conchoscaphal angle is about 90° when well folded and opens toward 120–150° when the antihelix is unfolded 🧠.

**Identity**
- Ears are used for biometric recognition and forensic comparison [14][15] ✅. Recognition is usually >90% but falls to about 80% or lower as imaging angle changes [15] 📄; ear + face fusion reached 97.1% in one border-control study [15] 📄.
- Forensic ear comparison is about 40 years old and an early system used twelve measurements [14] 📄 (Iannarelli's system 🧠). Ear tissue is elastic and can be deliberately deformed to fool recognition [14] 📄.
- Shape classes (round, oval, rectangular, triangular) were less useful for sex estimation than metrics [12] 📄.
- Ear morphology is highly heritable and polygenic; earlobe attachment is a continuum, not a single-gene switch 🧠.
- Highest-salience likeness cues: how much ear shows from the front; whether the antihelix or the helix forms the outer edge in front view; lobule size and attachment; helix roll width; Darwin's tubercle; concha size; acquired marks (cauliflower ear, stretched piercings, keloids) 🧠.

## Applying this to photo→3D face sculpting in Blender

1. **Capture for ears on purpose.** Both true profiles, one shot per side about 20–35° *behind* profile (perpendicular to the ear plane), a back view; hair tucked back; no earrings, glasses, hats or headphones; camera at 1.5 m or more with a longer lens. Arm's-length frontal selfies shrink ears to about three quarters of nose scale and hide them behind the cheeks (Table 5).
2. **Solve the camera before measuring.** Measure length (superaurale–subaurale) and width (preaurale–postaurale) on the 3D model reprojected through the solved cameras, never with a 2D scale taken from the nose: at 1 m a profile photo inflates the near ear by about 9% relative to midline features.
3. **Level the head first.** Rotate to FH (tragion–orbitale horizontal), then compare ear top with brow and ear bottom with subnasale and read the tilt. A 10° nod moves the ear about 17 mm relative to the brow, more than any canon tolerance.
4. **Block out from the skull outward.** Place tragion first (just behind the condyle, on the FH line), then set tilt, length, width and flare with a lattice or ear cage, then detail. Sanity checks: W/L 0.45–0.58; adult length 55–70 mm (longer when old); helical root about one ear length behind the lateral canthus.
5. **Get protrusion from front and back.** Frontally, match how far the helix silhouette stands outside the head contour and whether the antihelix or helix forms the outer edge. From behind, measure helix-to-head distance at three heights against Table 2. The commonest failure is ears glued flat to the head.
6. **Sculpt by tissue.** Block the shell (C of the helix, Y of the antihelix, bowl of the concha), then cut crisp cartilage folds (Draw Sharp, Crease, Pinch along the rim), push the concha genuinely deep behind the tragus, and keep the lobule soft and ridge-free (Inflate, Smooth). The back surface stays softer than the front.
7. **Topology.** One continuous edge loop along the helix rim turning into the helical crus, separate loops around the concha and triangular fossa, and a ring around the ear root joining the head. The ear needs far more polygons per cm² than the cheek; many artists sculpt it as a separate object and bridge it in before Multires.
8. **Symmetry.** Mirror only for blockout; left-right differences in size, height, tilt and protrusion are common and identity-bearing.
9. **Traps.** Hair occlusion (mark unknown, don't guess); earring-stretched lobes; ears bent by glasses or hats; phone lens distortion; red backlit translucency blurring edges; norms that used a different length definition.
10. **Validate.** Overlay renders from every solved camera on its photo: helix contour in profile, frontal silhouette, and left vs right.

## Algorithmic implications

- **Frame.** Express ear parameters in an FH-aligned head frame anchored at tragion, never in image axes, so pitch error cannot masquerade as ear height or tilt.
- **Parameters per side:** `tragion_xyz` (skull frame), `length_mm`, `width_ratio`, `tilt_deg`, `flare_deg` (or auriculocephalic angle), derived `helix_mastoid_mm[3]`, and a low-dimensional shape code (helix roll, antihelix/crura prominence, concha depth, lobule size and attachment). Left and right stay separate, linked by soft symmetry.
- **Suggested priors (provisional, adult; replace with population tables once verified):**

| Parameter | Prior | Hard bounds | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| length_mm | N(61, 4.5) + 2 if male + 0.2·max(0, age−25) | 45–85 | [3][5][16]; sex [16]; age slope [18] 📄 |
| width_ratio | N(0.50, 0.05) | 0.38–0.65 | [3][5][16] |
| tilt_deg (top back, from FH vertical) | N(20, 7) | −5 to 45 | 🧠 |
| tragion height vs orbitale (mm) | N(0, 5) | ±20 | 🧠 |
| auriculocephalic_deg | N(25, 8); warn >35 | 5–70 | [8] ↔️ + 🧠 |
| L–R difference: length / tilt / tragion / protrusion | σ 2 mm / 4° / 3 mm / 3 mm | – | 🧠; [7] |

- **Canons are checks, not priors.** Brow and subnasale alignment and nasal-dorsum parallelism become UI warnings (e.g. beyond ±12 mm or ±15°), never energy terms: they are emergent and often violated.
- **View weighting.** Weight ear-contour residuals by `max(0, cos(camera ray, ear-plane normal))²`: full weight from the ear-normal view, about 0.7 in true profile, near zero frontally, where views contribute only silhouette and protrusion.
- **Full perspective.** Never weak-perspective or orthographic for ears; the 4–36% scale errors in Table 5 rival or exceed the population SD.
- **Occlusion and robustness.** Gate ear landmarks with a hair/accessory segmentation mask and use a robust loss (Huber or Cauchy); allow a small per-photo elastic nuisance deformation because glasses and hats bend the ear [14].
- **Rigidity.** Auricular muscles are vestigial, so ears are effectively rigid relative to the cranium across expressions; visible tragion and helix points are good extra head-pose anchors in multi-expression sets 🧠.
- **Landmarks.** Farkas set: superaurale, subaurale, preaurale, postaurale, otobasion superius and inferius, tragion; plus intertragal notch and lobule low point; optionally a dense 55-point in-the-wild ear contour scheme 🧠. Store each measurement with its definition [16][17].
- **Template and shape code.** Generic head models (e.g. FLAME) carry coarse ears and most monocular face regressors mask ears out 🧠. Fit the ear as a separate part with its own PCA or morphable model (York Ear Model [19] 📄: free for research and education only, non-commercial licence, access by signed user agreement, so it cannot go into a commercial pipeline; ear part of the Universal Head Model 🧠), blended at the root ring, after checking licences.
- **Identity metric.** Face-recognition embeddings are trained mostly on frontal crops and largely ignore ears 🧠, so add an explicit ear-contour term (Chamfer distance in ear-normal and profile views); ear + face beat either alone in [15].
- **Provenance.** Normalise to mm on ingest; keep `source_url`, `n`, `status` and `definition` on every prior row (`research/data/ear.json`).

## Disagreements, myths & open questions

- **Protrusion norm:** 20–30° vs <25° with ≤20 mm projection [8]; McDowell's 3 mm symmetry goal vs [7]'s "too strict". Encode ranges; warn, don't clamp.
- **Width and lobule by sex:** [9][12] vs [10][13]. **Length definitions:** extreme projections [17] vs superaurale–subaurale [16]; a few mm of cross-study difference can be pure method.
- **Stability vs growth:** "shape fixed by 4 months", "significant change to 8 years" and "stable until ~70" all come via one secondary source [14] (Iannarelli-era claims) and conflict with adult lengthening data [18] 📄 ↔️.
- **Myth: the classical canons are rules.** Brow-to-nose-base placement, ear length = nose length and axis parallel to the nasal dorsum are drawing conventions; Farkas found the last two hold only in a minority 🧠 ↔️.
- **Myth: "ears are unique like fingerprints".** Ears are discriminative, but uniqueness is unproven and earprint evidence has been successfully challenged in court 🧠 ↔️.
- **Myth: attached vs free earlobe is a simple Mendelian trait.** It is polygenic and continuous 🧠.
- **Suspect value:** the Sundanese women's concha width (0.42 cm) looks like a typo [16] and is excluded.
- **Dropped (no traceable URL):** molded-ear angles (cephaloauricular 47.7° vs 31.1°; scaphaconchal 132.6° vs 106.7°) and a 3D auriculocephalic angle from plane normals fitted to mastoid and auricle (worth adopting as the engine's own protrusion measure 🧠).
- **Open:** sourced norms for ear height vs brow/subnasale and for axis tilt across populations; measurement points in [7]; full-text checks of [3], [5], [16] and [6] (adolescence to mid-adulthood); availability and licences of ear morphable models and landmark datasets; Blender/Polycount ear-sculpting conventions.
