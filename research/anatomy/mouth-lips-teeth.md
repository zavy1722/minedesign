# Lips, mouth and dental support

> The mouth is the most mobile part of the face. The lip shape that carries identity must therefore be fitted from neutral, lips-closed, bare-lip views, with smiles kept as separate shape keys [1]. Likeness depends on a few measurable features: mouth width (ch–ch), upper-lip height (sn–sto) split into skin and vermilion, the Cupid's bow (peak spacing ≈ philtrum width), the tubercle and lower-lip "pillows", how far the mouth wraps back to the commissures, and profile projection. Profile projection is set by the incisors and alveolus as much as by soft tissue [14][15][16]. The lower vermilion is taller than the upper [5][6], but the popular 1.6:1 figure is an aesthetic target, drawn mainly from young white women. It is not a population mean, and lips change with age and ancestry [2][8]. Classic canons fail for most people [3], and "mouth corners under the pupils" has no direct test here (typical norms put resting corners nearer the inner edge of the iris), so a fitting engine should encode all of this as weak priors conditioned on age and population, plus QA warnings, with a latent dental arch under the lips.
>

## Key facts

**Surface anatomy**

- **Philtrum.** The philtrum is a vertical midline groove on the upper lip, bounded by two philtral columns and running from subnasale (sn) to the upper vermilion border. Measure it at rest, because expression moves these landmarks [1]. 📄
- **Column geometry.** The columns diverge downward from the columella base and end at the two peaks of the Cupid's bow (Farkas landmark *crista philtri*, cph). The bow's central dip sits at the foot of the philtral groove, directly above the tubercle. 🧠
- **The bow comes from philtral depth.** Whether a Cupid's bow shows depends more on philtral depth than on upper-lip fullness, so a deep philtrum can make a bow on a thin lip (snippet-level evidence) [1]. 📄
- **Peaks are taller than the dip.** Upper vermilion height is greater at the bow peaks than at the midline dip. The evidence is a 3D toddler study used for cleft-repair marking; the harvest's attribution of it to [1] is doubtful [1]. 📄
- **White roll.** A narrow (≈1–2 mm), slightly raised, paler ridge of skin runs along the border between vermilion and skin and is sharpest across the bow. It catches a thin specular line, is a major realism cue, and flattens with age. 🧠
- **Tubercle and pillows.** The upper lip has a central tubercle, often with two lesser lateral swellings. The lower lip has two paramedian lobules with a shallow midline groove that receives the tubercle. The lip-contact (stomion) line is therefore a shallow wave that dips under the tubercle, not a straight slit. 🧠
- **Wet–dry line.** The boundary between dry vermilion and wet mucosa lies near the contact zone when the lips are closed. It shows when the lips evert, part or pout. 🧠
- **Commissure and modiolus.** Cheilion (ch) is the lateral meeting point of the lips [4]. 📄 About 1 cm lateral to it lies the modiolus, the muscle knot that positions the corner (see `muscles-lower-face.md`). At rest the lower lip tucks slightly under the upper at the corner. 🧠
- **Planes.** The upper vermilion faces forward and down, and the lower vermilion faces forward and up. Under overhead light the upper lip reads darker, the lower lip carries the highlight, and the mentolabial sulcus casts a shadow. Part of the apparent upper border in a photo is therefore a shading edge, not the anatomical border. 🧠

**Dimensions and ratios**

- **Adult upper lip.** Philtrum width was ≈11–12 mm and "upper lip height" ≈22–23 mm in adults aged 20–59; cohort, n and sex split are unconfirmed [2]. 📄 **Correction to the earlier draft:** 22–23 mm matches sn–sto (the whole upper lip, vermilion included), not sn–ls (skin only, typically ≈12–17 mm). 🧠
- **Age.** Upper vermilion height fell from ≈8.1 mm at 20–29 y to ≈6.6 mm at 50–59 y, a loss of ≈1.5 mm (≈19%) [2]. 📄 With age the skin lip also lengthens, the vermilion inverts, the columns and bow flatten and the corners turn down. 🧠
- **Direction of the vertical ratio.** The lower vermilion is taller than the upper [5][6][8]. ✅
- **Size of the ratio.** The cited lower:upper ideal is ≈1.6:1 (range 1.6–2:1), drawn mainly from young white women [5][6]. A 2024 review says the ratio does not generalize across populations and that Black individuals tend to have larger lip volume [8]. ↔️
- **Measured vs ideal.** Caliper and photo norms for young adults of European descent put the midline ratio sto–li / ls–sto nearer ≈1.1–1.4 than 1.6, which suggests 1.6 describes a target rather than the average lip. This is an expert estimate to verify. 🧠
- **Perception.** An over-filled upper lip looked less artificial once the lower lip was raised to ≈1.6× its height [6]. Keeping the upper:lower red-lip proportion mattered more than shortening the skin of the upper lip [7]. 📄
- **Lower-third split.** sn–sto is ≈⅓ of sn–me, and sto–me is ≈⅔. 🧠
- **Mouth width.** ch–ch in young adults is typically ≈50–58 mm in men and ≈46–54 mm in women (European-descent norms). It is often wider in African-descent samples. 🧠
- **Canons.** In 1,050 young adults, mouth width exceeded 1.5 × nose width in most subjects, with a wide spread, and intercanthal width was below nose width in most [3]. 📄 Canons and norms from a single population do not generalize [3][4][8]. ✅
- **Mouth width vs pupils.** No study in the harvest directly tests "mouth width = interpupillary distance" [3][4]. Typical adult means (ch–ch ≈50–54 mm, IPD ≈60–65 mm) give ch–ch/IPD ≈0.8–0.9. That puts the resting corners between the medial limbus and mid-iris, and surgical texts commonly use the medial-limbus line. In a broad smile the corners move to or beyond the pupil lines. ↔️
- **Lateral lip height (Kenya).** One group measured 23.5 ± 2.6 mm and another 22.3 ± 2.3 mm. The group labels were unclear in the snippet [4]. 📄
- **Rest posture.** Competent lips close without strain, and a resting gap between the lips of ≈0–3 mm is common. A larger gap with mentalis strain (chin dimpling) signals lip incompetence, often over protrusive incisors. 🧠

**Teeth and lip support**

- **Drape.** The lips drape over the incisors and alveolus, so lip projection is tooth/alveolar position plus soft-tissue thickness (≈11–15 mm at the upper lip over the incisors). 🧠 How closely the lip follows the incisor varies: orthodontic studies report roughly 0.3–0.9 mm of lip change per mm of incisor change, confounded by lip thickness and strain. ↔️
- **Resting display.** A pleasing upper-incisor display with the lips at rest is ≈2–4 mm [14]. 📄 In Vig & Brundo's classic data, women show more than men, upper display falls with age and lower-incisor display rises. 🧠
- **Too little display.** Low display reads as aged [14]. It goes with poor lip support, reduced vertical dimension, a long upper lip or a retruded maxilla (case-level evidence) [16]. ✅
- **Smile display.** The esthetic reference is full display of the upper-incisor crowns with minimal gum showing [13]. 📄 Roughly 3 mm or more of gum showing reads as "gummy". Smile lines divide into low, average and high in roughly 20/70/10% proportions (Tjan et al.). 🧠
- **Incisor angle.** Upright incisors increase smile display, and flared incisors reduce it [15]. 📄
- **Occlusion and dentition.** Normal overjet and overbite are each ≈2–3 mm. A large overjet (Class II/1) traps the lower lip behind the upper incisors, everts it and deepens the mentolabial fold. Class III flattens the upper lip and brings the lower lip forward. Without teeth the mouth collapses: the vermilion inverts, the lower face shortens and the corners droop. 🧠
- **Corner over the teeth.** At rest the commissures sit lateral to the canines, roughly over the first premolars. In a smile, a dark buccal corridor between the back teeth and the corners is normal. 🧠

**Smile arc**

- **Definition.** The smile arc compares the curve of the upper incisal edges and canine tips with the curve of the lower lip in a posed smile. The orthodontic ideal is for the two to run parallel ("consonant") [11]. 📄
- **Prevalence.** Consonant arcs were 63.3% in 150 Saudi orthodontic patients [17][18] and 66.9% in Jeddah adults [19]. One cohort reported 82.8%, but its source was not pinned down. Arcs were parallel in only 43.1% of 490 Vietnamese students [20], and 58.3% of finishing-phase patients had non-consonant arcs [21]. Maulik & Nanda (cited in [19]) found straight arcs most common, at 49%. ↔️
- **Preference.** Laypeople rated flat or reverse arcs favourably, and general dentists preferred flat to mildly curved arcs [12]. ↔️
- **Skeleton.** A 2026 retrospective study of 150 patients found no association between arc type and sagittal skeletal pattern or incisor display [17][18]; [17] and [18] are the same study. 📄
- **Pose.** Head pitch changes whether observers judge the arc and lower-lip curve as consonant [22]. 📄

## Applying this to photo→3D face sculpting in Blender

1. **Capture for the mouth.**
   - A neutral view with the lips closed and relaxed.
   - Bare lips: liner, gloss and filler move the apparent border and bow.
   - A true profile, for projection, the E-line and the nasolabial angle.
   - Both three-quarter views, for the wrap and the commissure depth.
   - A natural smile, and a teeth-apart view for the dental proxy.

   Mark the philtrum and corners as low-confidence under a moustache or beard.
2. **Gate before measuring.** For identity, reject or down-weight frames with parted lips, showing teeth, raised corners or chin strain [1]. Send those frames to expression shape keys.
3. **Measure after the metric solve.** The face3d camera solve gives scale. Read off:
   - ch–ch.
   - sn–sto and sn–ls.
   - ls–sto and sto–li, at the midline and at the bow peaks.
   - cph–cph.
   - Commissure depth, from the profile and three-quarter views.
   - Lip positions relative to the E-line.

   Use Tables 1–3 only to flag outliers. Never pull the mesh toward a norm [4][8].
4. **Place the corners from the photos, not the eyes** [3]. In three-quarter views the far corner is foreshortened and often in shadow (practitioner observation, no source found), so place each corner from the view on its own side.
5. **Build a dental proxy first.**
   - Arch with an intercanine width of ≈33–37 mm.
   - Upper incisal edges set so the resting display matches the photo (default 2–4 mm [14]).
   - Upper labial tooth surface ≈11–15 mm behind ls.
   - Lower incisors 2–3 mm behind the uppers.

   If the photo shows 2–4 mm of incisor and the model shows none, the upper lip is too long or too low [14].
6. **Wrap, then add detail.** Block the lips as a band around the arch. Check the top view: the mouth curve should follow the arch, with ch ≈10–20 mm behind the lip midline. Then sculpt in this order:
   1. Philtral groove and columns.
   2. Bow peaks at the feet of the columns, with peak spacing equal to philtrum width and taller vermilion at the peaks than at the dip [1]. Build the bow from the philtrum, not from lip volume [1].
   3. The white roll: Pinch or Crease along the border with ≈0.3–1 mm relief, then soften.
   4. The tubercle and the lower pillows.
   5. The stomion line, cut as a deep crevice into a mouth bag rather than a surface groove.
   6. The corner pocket where the lower lip tucks under.
   7. The mentolabial sulcus.
7. **Check the planes under matched light.** Render with the photo's key-light direction. The upper vermilion should go darker and the lower pillows should catch highlights. A mismatch usually means the vermilion angle is wrong, not its height.
8. **Age and dentition.** An older mouth has a thinner, inverted upper vermilion (≈1.5 mm less by the 50s [2]), a longer skin lip, a flatter bow, less upper and more lower incisor display, and downturned corners. Without teeth the mouth collapses, unless dentures were worn in the photos.
9. **Smile shape keys.** Copy the subject's actual arc and do not force consonance [12][17]–[21]. Fix the head pitch first [22]. In a smile the corners move laterally and upward, and buccal corridors appear.
10. **Topology for expressions.**
    - Concentric loops, following orbicularis oris.
    - A dedicated loop on the vermilion border, so the white roll and the colour edge stay crisp.
    - At least two loops across each vermilion.
    - Loops that continue into the mouth bag.
    - No poles on the vermilion; put them out at the modiolus.
11. **Common failures:**
    - Lips fitted from parted or smiling photos.
    - The 1.6 ratio imposed on someone else [8].
    - Mouth width taken from the eyes.
    - A flat, unwrapped mouth: it reads fine frontally and wrong in three-quarter view.
    - An upper lip modelled as a forward shelf.
    - No white roll, which gives a CG look.
    - A straight slit for a mouth.
    - The lipstick edge used as the border.
    - A smile arc misread because of head pitch [22].
    - Lips sculpted without teeth underneath.

## Algorithmic implications

- **Landmark schema.**
  - sn.
  - `ls_border` (the midline vermilion border, Farkas ls).
  - `ls_prom` (the most anterior upper-lip point, which orthodontists call "labrale superius"). The engine's synthetic set uses `labrale_superius`; keep both names (see `craniometric-landmarks.md`).
  - cph_L/R.
  - sto, split into sto_sup and sto_inf when the lips part.
  - li, sl and ch_L/R.

  Store the upper border (ch–cph–dip–cph–ch), the lower border and the stomion line as polylines [1][4]. 🧠
- **Correspondence type by view.**
  - The vermilion border and ch are fixed points on the surface; fit them barycentrically.
  - In profile and steep three-quarter views, the detected outer lip contour is an *occluding contour*. Fit it with sliding silhouette correspondences, not fixed vertices.
  - Switch per point using a normal·view test, or yaw above ≈35–45°.

  🧠
- **Detector mapping.**
  - iBUG-68: 48–59 outer lip (48/54 corners, 50/52 near the bow peaks, 51 the dip, 57 the lower midline) and 60–67 inner lip.
  - MediaPipe Face Mesh: 61/291 corners, 0/17 outer midlines, 13/14 inner midlines.

  Neither scheme has philtral columns, and both follow colour edges, so lipstick and shading bias them. 🧠
- **Neutral gate.** Flag a frame as parted when the inner-lip gap divided by ch–ch exceeds ≈0.04. Flag it as an expression when ch height relative to sto deviates more than ≈2 mm from the median across frames, or when teeth pixels show. Fit identity on frames that pass; route the rest to expression [1]. 🧠
- **Weak Gaussian priors.** Add each as w·((x−μ)/σ)², with w set so that one well-observed landmark outweighs it. Multiply σ by 1.5 when ancestry or age is unknown.

| Parameter | μ | σ | Condition on | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ch–ch / IPD (rest) | 0.85 | 0.06 | sex | 🧠 |
| ch–ch / al–al | 1.55 | 0.15 | population | 🧠 |
| sn–sto / sn–me | 0.33 | 0.03 | — | 🧠 |
| ls–sto (mm) | 8.0 − 0.05·(age−25) | 1.5 | age | 📄 [2] (σ is ours) |
| sto–li / ls–sto | 1.3 (clamp 0.8–2.3) | 0.3 | population | ↔️ |
| cph–cph (mm) | 11.5 | 1.5 | — | 📄 [2] |
| Bow dip depth below peaks (mm) | 1.5 | 1.0 | age | 🧠 (low confidence) |
| Commissure depth behind ls (mm) | 15 | 4 | — | 🧠 |
| Upper / lower lip to E-line (mm) | −4 / −2 | 3 | population | 🧠 |
| Upper-incisor display at rest (mm) | 3 (range 2–4) | 1.5 | sex, age | 📄 [14] |

- **Latent dental arch.** Model the arch with these parameters:
  - Intercanine width.
  - Arch depth.
  - Position of the incisors' labial surface.
  - Incisal-edge height (which sets display).
  - Proclination.

  Make the lip surface the arch offset by a thickness field with its own prior, so that a projection error is split between tooth position and soft tissue. Export the teeth as a separate mesh [14][15][16]. 🧠
- **QA warnings, never constraints.** Warn when:
  - ch–ch/IPD falls outside 0.70–1.00.
  - sto–li/ls–sto falls outside 0.8–2.3.
  - sn–sto/sn–me falls outside 0.27–0.40.
  - Resting display falls outside −2 to 6 mm.
  - Commissure depth falls outside 6–25 mm.

  There is no mouth-width = IPD constraint [3]. 🧠
- **Pose before curvature.** Solve the head pitch, then measure the smile arc in a head-aligned frame. Report the arc class as a descriptor, not a target [11][12][22]. 📄
- **Uncertainty.** Carry each prior's badge into the solver as a confidence weight.

## Disagreements, myths & open questions

- **"Mouth corners under the pupils."** This is untested in the harvest. Typical means put resting corners inside the pupil lines, near the medial limbus; the rule roughly holds only for a broad smile. ↔️
- **"Mouth = 1.5 × nose" and "intercanthal = nose width."** Both fail for most of 1,050 young adults [3]. 📄
- **The lip ratio.**
  - 1.6 is a target derived from young white women [5][6], and a review rejects it as universal [8].
  - Measured means appear lower. 🧠
  - Heidekrueger et al. (2017, crowdsourced survey) reportedly found ≈1:1 the most preferred female ratio; the details need verification. ↔️
  - Wording problem: one snippet gave StatPearls as "upper-to-lower 1.6:1 to 2:1", which contradicts [6]. This note assumes lower > upper.
- **Smile arc.**
  - The orthodontic ideal [11] conflicts with lay preference [12].
  - Prevalence gaps likely reflect differences in classification [19].
  - On the link to display and skeleton, [17] found none, while [16] ties low display to lip support.

  ↔️
- **"Golden proportion" teeth (Levin).** Natural dentitions rarely follow it, so it is useless as a likeness prior. ↔️
- **Landmark definitions differ.** Labiale superius is defined either as the border point or as the most prominent point, and subnasale has variants too, so numbers from different studies are not interchangeable. 🧠
- **Dropped claims.** "Lip-line cant 1.6° (SD 1.0) in Asian adults" was dropped because no URL was found. A Saudi facial-thirds finding attributed to [3] was dropped because the attribution looked wrong.
- **Open questions.**
  - Full text of [2]: ethnicity, n, sex split and landmark definitions.
  - The Kenyan group labels [4].
  - The source of the 82.8% figure.
  - Numbers still to mine from [9], [10] and [23].
  - Exact Vig & Brundo values.
  - Direct tests of mouth width against IPD and the medial limbus in large multi-ethnic 3D cohorts.
  - 3D vermilion volume and tubercle projection data from 2024–2026.
  - Detector accuracy on the lips under lipstick and beards.
  - Practitioner views from Polycount, BlenderArtists and Reddit.
