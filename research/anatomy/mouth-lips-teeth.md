# Lips, mouth and dental support

> Mouth likeness must be fitted from neutral, lips-closed, bare-lip views, with smiles kept as separate shape keys [1]. Identity lives in mouth width (ch–ch), upper-lip height (sn–sto) and its skin/vermilion split, Cupid's-bow peak spacing (≈ philtrum width), the tubercle and lower-lip pillows, how far the corners wrap back, and profile projection, which the incisors and alveolus set as much as soft tissue does [14][15][16]. The lower vermilion is taller than the upper [5][6], but "1.6:1" is an aesthetic target drawn from young white women, not a population mean, and lips change with age and ancestry [2][8]. Classic canons fail for most people [3], and "mouth corners under the pupils" is untested; resting corners usually sit nearer the medial limbus. A fitting tool should encode all of this as weak, age- and population-conditioned priors and QA warnings over a latent dental arch.
>

## Key facts

**Surface anatomy**

- **Philtrum.** The philtrum is the midline groove between the two philtral columns, running from subnasale (sn) to the upper vermilion border. Measure it at rest, because expression moves these landmarks [1]. 📄
- **Columns.** The columns diverge downward and end at the two Cupid's-bow peaks (Farkas *crista philtri*, cph). The central dip of the bow sits above the tubercle. 🧠
- **Bow from depth.** A visible bow depends more on philtral depth than on lip fullness, so a deep philtrum gives a bow even on a thin lip (snippet-level) [1]. 📄
- **Peaks taller than dip.** Upper vermilion is taller at the bow peaks than at the midline (3D toddler cleft study; the harvest's attribution to [1] is doubtful) [1]. 📄
- **White roll.** A raised, paler ridge of skin about 1–2 mm wide runs along the vermilion border, sharpest across the bow. It carries a thin specular line and flattens with age. 🧠
- **Tubercle and pillows.** The upper tubercle sits in a shallow groove between two lower-lip lobules, so the line where the lips meet is a shallow wave, not a slit. The wet–dry line shows when the lips evert or part. 🧠
- **Commissure.** Cheilion (ch) is where the upper and lower lips meet laterally [4]. 📄 The modiolus lies about 1 cm lateral to it (see `muscles-lower-face.md`), and at rest the lower lip tucks under the upper at the corner. 🧠
- **Planes.** Upper vermilion faces forward-down, lower forward-up: under top light the upper lip goes dark and the lower holds the highlight, so part of a photo's "border" is a shading edge. 🧠

**Dimensions and ratios**

- **Adult upper lip.** Philtrum width is about 11–12 mm and "upper lip height" about 22–23 mm in adults aged 20–59; the cohort and sex split are unconfirmed [2]. 📄 *Correction:* 22–23 mm matches sn–sto (vermilion included), not sn–ls (skin only, about 12–17 mm) as the earlier draft labelled it. 🧠
- **Age.** The upper vermilion is about 8.1 mm at 20–29 and 6.6 mm at 50–59, a loss of about 19% [2]. 📄 With age the skin lip also lengthens, the vermilion turns inward, the bow flattens and the corners turn down. 🧠
- **Ratio direction.** The lower vermilion is taller than the upper [5][6][8]. ✅
- **Ratio size.** The cited ideal is about 1.6:1, up to 2:1, and comes mostly from young white women [5][6]. A 2024 review rejects it as universal and notes larger lip volume in Black individuals [8]. ↔️ Measured midline sto–li/ls–sto in young adults of European descent looks nearer 1.1–1.4. 🧠
- **Perception.** An over-filled upper lip looked less artificial once the lower lip was raised to about 1.6× its height [6]. Keeping the red-lip proportion mattered more than shortening the skin lip [7]. 📄
- **Canons.** In 1,050 young adults, most had a mouth wider than 1.5 × nose width and an intercanthal distance narrower than the nose [3]. 📄 Canons and norms from a single population do not generalize [3][4][8]. ✅
- **Mouth vs pupils.** No harvested study tests "mouth width = interpupillary distance (IPD)" [3][4]. Typical means (ch–ch ≈50–54 mm, IPD ≈60–65 mm) give ≈0.8–0.9, putting resting corners between the medial limbus (the line surgical texts use) and mid-iris; they reach the pupil lines only in a broad smile. ↔️
- **Rest posture.** Competent lips close without strain; a 0–3 mm resting gap is common. A larger gap with chin dimpling signals incompetence, often over protrusive incisors. 🧠

**Teeth and lip support**

- **Drape.** Lip projection is roughly tooth and alveolar position plus soft tissue about 11–15 mm thick over the upper incisors. 🧠 Reported lip change per 1 mm of incisor change ranges from 0.3 to 0.9 mm. ↔️
- **Resting display.** About 2–4 mm of upper incisor showing at rest is pleasing [14]. 📄 Women show more than men, and with age upper display falls while lower display rises (Vig & Brundo). 🧠
- **Low display.** Too little display reads as aged [14]. It goes with poor lip support, reduced vertical dimension, a long lip or a retruded maxilla [16] (case-level evidence). ✅
- **Smile display.** The reference is full display of the upper crowns with minimal gum [13]. 📄 About 3 mm or more of gum reads as "gummy". Smile lines split roughly 20/70/10% into low, average and high (Tjan et al.). 🧠
- **Incisor angle.** Upright incisors increase display, and flared incisors reduce it [15]. 📄
- **Occlusion.** Normal overjet and overbite ≈2–3 mm. Class II/1 traps and everts the lower lip and deepens the fold below it; Class III flattens the upper lip; tooth loss inverts the vermilion, shortens the lower face and droops the corners. 🧠
- **Corners over the teeth.** The corners sit lateral to the canines, roughly over the first premolars. Buccal corridors are normal in a smile. 🧠

**Smile arc**

- **Definition.** The smile arc compares the curve of the upper incisal edges and canine tips with the curve of the lower lip in a posed smile. The orthodontic ideal is that the two run parallel ("consonant") [11]. 📄
- **Prevalence.** Consonant or parallel arcs range from about 43% to 83% across cohorts, and an older study found straight arcs most common (Table 4) [17]–[21]. ↔️
- **Preference.** Laypeople favoured flat or reverse arcs; general dentists preferred flat to mild curves [12]. ↔️
- **Skeleton.** In a 2026 study of 150 patients, arc type was not associated with sagittal skeletal pattern or with incisor display [17][18] ([17] and [18] are the same study). 📄
- **Pose.** Head pitch changes whether an arc is perceived as consonant [22]. 📄

## Applying this to photo→3D face sculpting in Blender

1. **Capture:**
   - neutral, relaxed, lips closed;
   - bare lips (liner, gloss and filler all shift the border);
   - a true profile;
   - both three-quarter views;
   - a natural smile;
   - a teeth-apart view.

   Mark the philtrum and corners low-confidence under facial hair.
2. **Gate:** for identity, reject or down-weight frames with parted lips, visible teeth, raised corners or chin strain, and send them to expression keys instead [1].
3. **Measure after the metric solve:** ch–ch, sn–sto, sn–ls, ls–sto and sto–li (at the midline and at the peaks), cph–cph, corner depth and E-line distances. Use Tables 1–3 only to flag outliers, never to pull toward a norm [4][8].
4. **Corners come from the photos, not the eyes** [3]. Take each corner from the view on its own side, because the far corner is foreshortened and shadowed in three-quarter view (practitioner observation, unsourced).
5. **Build a dental proxy first:**
   - intercanine width about 33–37 mm;
   - incisal edges set to the resting display seen in the photo (default 2–4 mm [14]);
   - upper labial surface about 11–15 mm behind ls;
   - lower incisors 2–3 mm behind the uppers.

   If the photo shows incisor and the model shows none, the upper lip is too long or too low [14].
6. **Wrap, then detail.** Block the lips as a band on the arch; from the top view, ch should sit about 10–20 mm behind the midline. Then sculpt in this order:
   1. philtral columns;
   2. bow peaks at the column feet, spaced by the philtrum width, taller vermilion at the peaks, the bow made from the philtrum, not from volume [1];
   3. white roll (Pinch or Crease, about 0.3–1 mm, then soften);
   4. tubercle and pillows;
   5. lip-contact line as a crevice into a mouth bag;
   6. corner pocket;
   7. mentolabial sulcus.
7. **Matched light:** with the photo's key-light direction, the upper vermilion should go dark and the lower pillows catch light. A mismatch usually means the vermilion angle is wrong, not its height.
8. **Age:** thinner, inward-turned upper vermilion (about 1.5 mm less by the 50s [2]), a longer skin lip, a flatter bow, less upper and more lower incisor show, and downturned corners. A mouth without teeth collapses.
9. **Smile keys:** copy the subject's actual arc instead of forcing consonance [12][17]–[21], and solve pitch first [22].
10. **Topology:**
    - concentric loops;
    - one loop on the vermilion border, which keeps the white roll and colour edge crisp;
    - at least two loops across each vermilion;
    - loops continuing into the mouth bag;
    - poles at the modiolus, never on the vermilion.
11. **Common failures:** parted or smiling fit photos; an imposed 1.6 ratio [8]; width taken from the eyes; a flat, unwrapped mouth (fine frontally, wrong in three-quarter view); a shelf-like upper lip; no white roll (CG look); a straight slit; lipstick edge taken as the border; an arc misread because of pitch [22]; no teeth.

## Algorithmic implications

- **Landmark schema** 🧠
  - sn;
  - `ls_border` (Farkas ls, on the vermilion border);
  - `ls_prom` (the most anterior point; the orthodontic "labrale superius" and the engine's synthetic `labrale_superius`; see `craniometric-landmarks.md`);
  - cph_L/R;
  - sto (sto_sup/sto_inf when the lips part);
  - li, sl, ch_L/R.

  Store the upper border (ch–cph–dip–cph–ch), the lower border and the stomion line as polylines [1][4].
- **Correspondence by view.** The vermilion border and ch are fixed on the surface (barycentric). In profile and steep three-quarter views the detected outer lip line is an occluding contour, so it needs sliding silhouette correspondences. Switch per point with a normal·view test, or above about 35–45° yaw. 🧠
- **Detectors.** Neither detector marks the columns, and both track colour edges, so lipstick and shading bias them. 🧠
  - iBUG-68: 48–59 outer (48/54 corners, 50/52 near the peaks, 51 dip, 57 lower midline), 60–67 inner.
  - MediaPipe Face Mesh: 61/291 corners, 0/17 outer midline, 13/14 inner midline.
- **Neutral gate.** 🧠
  - Parted: inner gap / ch–ch > about 0.04.
  - Expression: ch height relative to sto more than about 2 mm off the cross-frame median, or teeth visible [1].
- **Weak Gaussian priors.** Each prior adds w·((x−μ)/σ)², with w set so one good landmark outweighs it. Multiply σ by 1.5 if ancestry or age is unknown.

| Parameter | μ | σ | Condition on | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ch–ch / IPD (rest) | 0.85 | 0.06 | sex | 🧠 |
| ch–ch / al–al | 1.55 | 0.15 | population | 🧠 |
| sn–sto / sn–me | 0.33 | 0.03 | — | 🧠 |
| ls–sto (mm) | 8.0 − 0.05·(age−25) | 1.5 | age | 📄 [2], σ ours |
| sto–li / ls–sto | 1.3, clamp 0.8–2.3 | 0.3 | population | ↔️ |
| cph–cph (mm) | 11.5 | 1.5 | — | 📄 [2] |
| Bow dip below peaks (mm) | 1.5 | 1.0 | age | 🧠 low confidence |
| Commissure depth behind ls (mm) | 15 | 4 | — | 🧠 |
| Upper / lower lip to E-line (mm) | −4 / −2 | 3 | population | 🧠 |
| Upper-incisor display at rest (mm) | 3 (2–4) | 1.5 | sex, age | 📄 [14] |

- **Latent dental arch.** Parameters: intercanine width, arch depth, incisor labial position, incisal-edge height (which sets display) and proclination. Lips = arch + a thickness field with its own prior, so projection error is split between teeth and soft tissue [14][15][16]. Export the teeth as their own mesh. 🧠
- **QA warnings, never constraints.** Warn when any value falls outside its range, and add no mouth = IPD term [3]: 🧠
  - ch–ch/IPD: 0.70–1.00;
  - sto–li/ls–sto: 0.8–2.3;
  - sn–sto/sn–me: 0.27–0.40;
  - resting display: −2 to 6 mm;
  - corner depth: 6–25 mm.
- **Pose before curvature.** Solve pitch, measure the arc in a head-aligned frame, and report its class as a descriptor, not a target [11][12][22]. 📄 Pass each prior's badge to the solver as a confidence weight.

## Disagreements, myths & open questions

- **"Corners under the pupils."** Untested; roughly true only in a broad smile (see Key facts). ↔️
- **"Mouth = 1.5 × nose" and "intercanthal = nose width."** Both fail in most of 1,050 young adults [3]. 📄
- **Lip ratio.** Three answers compete: a 1.6 target from young white women [5][6], lower measured means 🧠, and a crowdsourced preference of about 1:1 (Heidekrueger et al. 2017, to verify) ↔️. A review rejects any universal ratio [8]. One snippet gave StatPearls as "upper-to-lower 1.6:1 to 2:1", contradicting [6]; this note assumes lower > upper.
- **Smile arc.** ↔️
  - Orthodontic ideal [11] vs lay preference [12].
  - Prevalence gaps reflect classification [19].
  - No link with display or skeleton [17], vs low display tied to lip support [16].
- **"Golden proportion" teeth (Levin).** Natural dentitions rarely follow it, so it is useless as a likeness prior. ↔️
- **Landmark definitions differ** (ls as border point vs most prominent point; subnasale variants), so cross-study numbers don't mix. 🧠
- **Dropped claims:**
  - lip-line cant 1.6° (SD 1.0) in Asian adults (no URL);
  - a Saudi facial-thirds finding misattributed to [3].
- **Open questions:** full text of [2] (ancestry, n, sex, definitions); Kenyan group labels [4]; the 82.8% source; mining [9][10][23]; exact Vig & Brundo values; direct mouth-width vs IPD / medial-limbus tests in large multi-ethnic 3D cohorts; 2024–2026 3D vermilion and tubercle data; detector accuracy under lipstick and beards; practitioner views (Polycount, BlenderArtists, Reddit).
