# Skin thickness, wrinkles, pores and microdetail

> Facial skin (epidermis plus dermis) is a 0.4–2.5 mm sheet, thinnest on the eyelids and thickest on the nasal tip, upper lip, chin and glabella–forehead; its thickness sets how crisply bone and cartilage read through and the scale at which it folds. Wrinkles are dynamic, static, compression (sleep) or atrophic (crepey); expression lines lie perpendicular to their muscle's pull, along relaxed skin tension lines (RSTL), which a tool can store as a vector field on the template. Pores are pilosebaceous openings ≈0.05–0.4 mm across, largest and densest on the nose and medial cheeks and absent from the eyelids and vermilion; microrelief furrows 0.01–0.1 mm deep lie beneath them. This tertiary layer mostly signals age (identity lives only in marks such as moles, scars and distinctive lines), and pores need ≤0.03–0.05 mm/px photos, far finer than a normal portrait; with no harvest or draft for this topic, every item is expert knowledge.

## Key facts

**Layers and thickness**
- The epidermis is 0.04–0.12 mm on most of the face and 0.03–0.05 mm on the eyelid; its stratum corneum is ≈0.01–0.02 mm. The dermis is >85% of the total and sets the mechanics 🧠.
- High-frequency ultrasound (HFUS) puts most facial skin at ≈1.0–2.2 mm and the eyelids at 0.4–0.9 mm 🧠. This matches the HFUS values sourced in `anatomy/smas-ligaments.md`: mid-cheek dermis 1.58 mm (F) and 1.88 mm (M), and a median of 1.1–1.6 mm across five regions 🧠.
- Studies differ by ≈±30% with method: fixed cadaver tissue shrinks, probe frequency moves the boundary, and some include the subepidermal low-echogenic band (SLEB) ↔️.
- **Skin is not soft-tissue depth.** Forensic depth tables (10–25 mm at the cheek) include fat and muscle; reading them as skin thickness is an order-of-magnitude error 🧠.
- **Nose.** The upper third has thin, mobile skin over bone, and the soft-tissue envelope is thinnest at the rhinion (≈2–3 mm). The lower third has thick, sebaceous, adherent skin: the tip envelope is ≈3 mm, or ≥4 mm in thick-skinned noses. At the nasion it is ≈5–7 mm (Lessard & Daniel 1985) 🧠. Thin skin shows the domes; thick skin blurs the tip. This is a major likeness variable 🧠.
- **Sex and age.** Male dermis is ≈10–25% thicker, with more sebum and larger pores. Women's dermis thins faster after menopause (Brincat: ≈30% collagen loss in the first five years) 🧠. Rete ridges flatten with age, but elastosis on sun-exposed skin means measured thickness can rise or fall ↔️.
- **Thin skin "shrink-wraps"** underlying edges (eyelid, dorsum, zygomatic arch, ear, mandibular border); where skin plus fat is thick, forms soften over ≈1–2× the tissue depth 🧠 (heuristic).

**Wrinkles**
- **Classes** 🧠 (Kligman, Zheng & Lavker 1985; Lemperle 2001):
  - dynamic (expression);
  - static (present at rest);
  - compression or sleep lines;
  - atrophic or crepey lines (elastosis; they flatten when stretched);
  - gravitational folds (nasolabial, marionette, jowl): secondary forms, see `anatomy/smas-ligaments.md`;
  - constitutional creases: the lid crease, mentolabial sulcus and neck "necklace" lines.
- **Dynamic lines run perpendicular to the muscle fibres** (Table B) 🧠. **Static lines** are dynamic lines that remain at rest once the dermis loses recoil: in Glogau's scale, type II has wrinkles in motion and type III at rest 🧠.
- **Sleep lines** ignore muscles. They are vertical on the lateral forehead and oblique on the cheek and chin, and asymmetric, favouring the sleeping side 🧠.
- **Spacing scales with thickness.** A stiff sheet on a soft base buckles at λ ∝ t·(E_skin/E_sub)^⅓ (Cerda & Mahadevan 2003) 🧠. Observed spacing is 0.5–1.5 mm for eyelid crinkles, 2–5 mm for crow's feet and 6–15 mm for forehead lines ↔️. Frontalis dermal insertions also position the forehead lines ↔️.
- **Cross-section.** A wrinkle is a folded sheet, not a cut: a rounded trough with soft shoulders. Static depth is 0.05–1 mm, rarely >1.5 mm 🧠.
- **Who wrinkles where.** Women have more perioral radial lines, with thinner upper-lip dermis and fewer pilosebaceous units (Paes et al. 2009) ↔️. UV and smoking dominate. Darker-pigmented and East Asian skin are often reported to wrinkle later and more mildly ↔️.

**Tension lines**
- Three schemes exist 🧠:
  - Langer (1861): cadaver awl-puncture cleavage lines;
  - Kraissl (1951): lines perpendicular to muscle action;
  - Borges (1984), RSTL: the furrows that form when relaxed skin is pinched.
- On the face RSTL and wrinkle lines largely coincide, while Langer's lines depart from them in several areas (on the face the two sets often run at right angles) 📄 [1][2].
- Skin is pre-tensioned and stiffer along these lines. Round wounds gape into ellipses along them, and pores and microrelief elongate along them with age 🧠.

**Pores and microrelief**
- Visible "pores" are sebaceous-follicle openings. Eccrine pores (≈0.02–0.06 mm) are invisible at portrait scale 🧠.
- Face and scalp have the body's highest sebaceous-gland density, often quoted as ≈400–900/cm² against <100/cm² on the limbs. Visible pore counts are lower and depend on the detection threshold ↔️.
- Pores enlarge with age, sebum and sagging. Cheek pores become teardrop-shaped and line up along RSTL (Sugiyama-Nakagiri et al. 2009; Flament et al. 2015) ↔️.
- **Microrelief** 🧠:
  - primary furrows are 0.02–0.1 mm deep and bound polygons 0.15–0.8 mm across;
  - secondary furrows are 0.005–0.04 mm deep;
  - pores and hairs sit at the intersections.
- **Vermilion:** no pores and no hair, but vertical "lip-print" grooves 0.3–1.5 mm apart (Suzuki & Tsuchihashi 1970) 🧠.
- **Eyelids:** crepe lines parallel to the margin, and vessels show through 🧠.
- **Ear:** anterior skin is thin and fixed to cartilage. A diagonal earlobe crease (Frank 1973) is a common adult mark 🧠.
- **Identity versus age.** Tertiary detail mainly signals age and realism (`anatomy/face-perception-likeness.md`). The exceptions are moles, scars, distinctive or asymmetric lines and a coarse-pored nose, and these must be placed to ≈1 mm 🧠.

## Applying this to photo→3D face sculpting in Blender

1. **Sort marks from texture.** List the identity marks: moles (raised or flat), scars, any asymmetric line, an earlobe crease, the nose's pore texture. Place each from ≥2 views to ≈1 mm. Generic pores come last 🧠.
2. **Check what each photo can carry.** Take mm/px from the solved camera and read Table E. Coarser than ≈0.05 mm/px, synthesize pores from Table C rather than copying them. Phone beauty, portrait and noise-reduction modes erase pores and fine lines, so turn them off, shoot RAW, and take close-up patches for microdetail 🧠.
3. **Separate static from dynamic lines.** A line at the same skin location in every neutral photo taken on different days is static and goes in Basis. Lines that appear only with raised brows, a squint or a smile go in AU shape keys or wrinkle maps (`anatomy/facs-expressions.md`). Dynamic lines baked into neutral make the sitter look tense and older 🧠.
4. **Sculpt folds, not cuts.** Trace static lines as surface-snapped curves. Then, at Multires L4–L5:
   - use Crease or Draw Sharp with radius ≈½ the line width and low strength;
   - raise soft shoulders with Layer or Inflate;
   - check depth under raking light against the photo's shadow widths.
   A V-shaped knife cut is the commonest CG tell 🧠.
5. **Scale lines to skin thickness.** Forehead and glabella: few, deep, wide-spaced lines. Lids and lateral canthus: many fine, close lines. Lips: short vertical lines 🧠.
6. **Work at real scale.** Use metres with unit scale 1.0, and check pore spacing with the Measure tool: ≈0.5 mm on the nose, ≈1 mm on the lateral cheek. Pore depth ≥0.3 mm, or cheek spacing ≥2 mm, reads as orange peel 🧠.
7. **Follow the flow.** Rotate alphas to the RSTL field and never tile uniformly. Mask the lids, the vermilion (vertical grooves instead) and the brows. Never mirror detail (`anatomy/facial-asymmetry.md`) 🧠.
8. **Let thickness control crispness.** Keep the lid crease, dorsum, alar groove and ear sharp; keep a thick-skinned nasal tip, the upper lip and the chin pad soft 🧠.
9. **Procedural fallback.** With Object coordinates in metres, Voronoi Distance to Edge at Scale ≈ 1/cell size (0.4 mm → ≈2500) makes microrelief, and Smooth F1 at 1/pore spacing (0.7 mm → ≈1430) makes pores; drive both with region masks 🧠.
10. **Bake and render.** Bake Multires displacement to a 32-bit EXR. Use Cycles Displacement and Bump with adaptive subdivision (Experimental status in 5.x unverified), or bump only in EEVEE. A face-only 8K UDIM gives ≈0.03 mm texels; a whole head on 4K (≈0.12 mm) aliases pores 🧠.
11. **Do not displace from albedo blindly.** "Dark is deep" turns freckles, vessels, stubble and flat moles into dents. Shading darkens all channels together, whereas pigment shifts chroma 🧠.
12. **Validate at photo resolution.** Render from the solved cameras and compare high-pass images for line positions. If detail is invisible at the photo's resolution, stop: extra line depth adds apparent years 🧠.

## Algorithmic implications

- **Band split.** Fit shape (landmarks, template warp, 3DMM) only to content with wavelength ≥≈5 mm, after low-passing the targets at 2–3 mm. Write the residual to a UV displacement layer in physical units, never to shape coefficients 🧠.
- **Template layers**, authored once because topology is fixed 🧠:
  - region masks;
  - an RSTL tangent field (Table F, σ 10–20°);
  - a thickness map (Table A, σ_log 0.2, with sex and age multipliers), which also feeds SSS thickness;
  - a pore-spacing map (Table C, σ_log ≈ 0.3).
- **Wrinkle primitive.** A surface curve with depth A, half-width σ, length, taper and shoulder factor c, with profile z(s) = −A(1 − c·s²/σ²)·exp(−s²/2σ²). c = 0 gives a plain trough; c = 1 gives a Ricker trough whose shoulders rise ≈0.45 A. Defaults: c = 0.3–0.6 for static lines, σ set per side, and the last 15–25% of the length tapered with a smoothstep 🧠.
- **Spacing and depth.** Spacing follows λ ≈ k·t, with k calibrated at ≈1.5–2.5 on the eyelid and ≈5–8 on the forehead. Depth comes from the photos; any prior on it should be weak (σ_log ≈ 0.7, set by age class) ↔️.
- **Pore stamping** in numpy, in UV space 🧠:
  - Poisson-disk sampling with minimum distance 0.75 × spacing, measured in mm through the per-face mm-per-UV Jacobian so pores keep their physical size through UV stretch;
  - log-normal radius: median 0.12 mm on the nose, 0.08 medial cheek, 0.07 forehead and chin, 0.05 lateral cheek; σ_log 0.35;
  - funnel profile z = −A(1 − r/R)^p with p 1.5–2.5 and A = 0.4–1.0 R, capped at 0.15 mm;
  - ellipse aligned to RSTL, with aspect from Table C;
  - zero density on lids, vermilion, brows, and within 1 mm of the lid margins and vermilion border.
- **Microrelief.** An anisotropic Voronoi edge network: cells 0.15–0.6 mm, grooves 0.01–0.04 mm, pores snapped to the nodes, stretch along RSTL from 1.0 (young) to 1.5–2.5 (old) 🧠.
- **Resolution gate.** Compute mm/px for each view from the solved intrinsics and depth, and enable each extraction stage only where Table E allows it. Below the gate, synthesize detail from priors and label it "synthetic" in the UI 🧠.
- **Line detection.** Use a Frangi-type Hessian valley filter at 0.3–2 mm scales, projected to pixels, on chroma-normalized luminance. Keep a line only if it lands within ±1 mm on the surface in ≥2 views. It is static if present in ≥N−1 of N neutral shots; otherwise it is dynamic and goes to an AU map 🧠.
- **Expression.** Blur microdetail across the stretch direction and sharpen it under compression (Nagano et al. 2015). Blend wrinkle maps by AU weight 🧠.
- **Learned detail.** DECA-style single-image displacement looks plausible but is not metric. Use it only as a prior below the resolution gate; the best method here is time-sensitive ↔️.

## Disagreements, myths & open questions

- **Langer vs Kraissl vs RSTL** differ on parts of the face; tools should use RSTL ↔️.
- **Thickest facial skin:** nasal tip, upper lip, glabella or chin, depending on modality and whether sebaceous and fibrofatty tissue is counted ↔️.
- **Dermal thinning with age:** HFUS results are mixed, because elastosis and the SLEB can raise the measured value ↔️.
- **Myths** 🧠:
  - "Pores open and close with heat": they have no muscle, and warmth only changes visibility.
  - "Visible pores are sweat pores": they are follicles.
  - "Wrinkles are just muscle": UV, dermal and volume loss, compression and genetics all contribute.
  - "More detail means more likeness": detail adds age and realism, not identity.
- **Ancestry multipliers** for wrinkle onset and pore size vary between studies; do not hard-code them ↔️.
- **Open:**
  - normative 3D pore size and density by region, age and sex;
  - a mm atlas of wrinkle depth;
  - facial microrelief parameters by region;
  - whether in-vivo wrinkle spacing follows buckling scaling;
  - how much tertiary detail familiar viewers use.
