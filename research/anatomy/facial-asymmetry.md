# Natural facial asymmetry

> Every normal adult face is measurably asymmetric. Whole-face surface asymmetry, measured as the RMS distance between a face and its mirror image, averages about 0.8 mm in healthy people. Individual bilateral landmarks differ far more: up to about 4.6 mm, mostly in the lower and lateral face [6][7][2]. Observers start to see asymmetry at roughly 2–4 mm, depending on the feature (eyelids are the most sensitive), on who is looking and on viewing time [9][10][11][12]. So a face whose average asymmetry is invisible can still carry visible, identity-bearing left–right differences at specific features. Mirroring a sculpt (symmetry on, Symmetrize, Mirror modifier) removes exactly those differences and copies one half's features onto the other. Use mirroring for block-out only and switch it off for the likeness pass.

## Key facts

- **Asymmetry is universal in normal faces.** No published threshold separates "normal" from "increased" asymmetry [1]. Slight asymmetry is always present [8]. Asymmetry rises with age [7]. ✅ (qualitative)
- **Global magnitude is about 0.8 mm RMSD.** Sample: 191 people aged 0.3–88 y, mean 0.8 ± 0.2 mm [7]. Healthy volunteers scanned on 3dMD photogrammetry: 0.80 ± 0.24 mm, face vs its reflection about the plane of maximum symmetry [6]. The attribution of [6] to that URL was inferred from search excerpts and is not confirmed. ⚠️ (two concordant reports, one attribution unconfirmed)
- **By facial third:** upper 0.5 mm, middle 0.6 mm, lower 0.6 mm (n = 191) [7]. ⚠️
- **The lower and lateral face is more asymmetric than the upper face.** Landmark right–left differences are larger at lower and lateral landmarks [2]. Lower-face midline landmarks drift to the opposite side from the upper face in children [1]. The upper third is the lowest by RMSD [7]. ✅ (direction of effect; magnitudes differ by metric)
- **Bilateral landmark differences are much larger than the global RMSD.** In 48 adults with normal occlusion (CBCT soft tissue, 9 bilateral pairs), right–left differences ran 0.6–4.6 mm [2]. ⚠️
- **2D skeletal measurements give inflated "normal" ranges.** Posteroanterior cephalograms give a normal range of 0–12 mm between right and left skeletal landmarks [3]. This is method-inflated and not comparable to 3D soft-tissue values. ⚠️ / ↔️ against [2][7]
- **Child midline offsets are about 0.25 mm** on average in a birth cohort followed to 6 y. This figure comes from a search excerpt, not the full text [1]. ⚠️
- **The 3 mm clinical cut-off is a planning convention, not a biological normal limit.** A 2025 3D/AI study uses 3 mm as its treatment threshold and states that asymmetry under about 3 mm is generally undetectable [4]. ⚠️
- **Perception thresholds are feature-specific and lie between 2 and 4 mm.** ↔️
  - Eyelid position at rest is the most sensitive feature, at about 2 mm [10]. ⚠️
  - Naive observers detected mouth-corner or brow asymmetry at about 3 mm or more [9]. ⚠️
  - Laypersons and general dentists put the threshold of abnormality at 4 mm; orthodontists and oral surgeons put it at 2 mm [11]. ⚠️
  - Differences under about 3–4 mm or 3–4° of canting are generally not recognised [12]. The URL attribution for this claim is unconfirmed. ⚠️
- **Viewing time matters.** Short exposures raise the detection threshold; longer viewing lets observers see smaller asymmetries [9]. ⚠️ A render shown for seconds is judged more strictly than a face glimpsed in passing.
- **Lower-face deviation is detected in degrees by experts only.** Orthodontists detected a 6° lower-face deviation; dentists and laypeople did not [11]. ⚠️
- **Thresholds from different studies are not interchangeable.** Studies use different methods (2D photos, 3D models, paralysis simulation) and different metrics (RMSD, midline deviation, index) [10][8]. ✅
- **Reflecting one half-face distorts features.** Early symmetry studies that duplicated one half made some features abnormally large or small. Later studies blended each face with its mirror image instead [15]. ⚠️
- **People prefer symmetric faces, but that finding is about attractiveness, not likeness.** The preference held in the UK and among Hadza hunter-gatherers (stronger in Hadza) [13] and in Chinese and Japanese samples [14]. None of these studies measures likeness or identity. ✅

## Applying this to photo→3D face sculpting in Blender

1. **Block-out with symmetry, refine without it.** Use Sculpt Mode's X mirror and Mesh ▸ Symmetrize only for the primary forms: skull, jaw mass, orbit placement. Then turn X symmetry off before the likeness pass. Never leave a Mirror modifier on a head intended as a likeness. Global asymmetry (~0.8 mm) is invisible [6][7], but landmark-level differences up to ~4.6 mm [2] exceed the 2–4 mm perception range [9][10][11]. Mirroring deletes the part of the asymmetry people can actually see. *(This is an inference from the combined numbers; no study in this library tested mirrored 3D heads for likeness directly.)*
2. **Keep the asymmetry in a separate layer so it is reversible.** Store the symmetric sculpt as the Basis shape key and the asymmetric likeness pass as a shape key, or as a higher multires level. Sliding that key from 0 to 1 shows exactly what the asymmetry contributes, and you can re-mirror the base without losing it.
3. **Check features in order of perceptual sensitivity:**
   - eyelid heights and palpebral openings (~2 mm [10]);
   - mouth corners and brows (~3 mm [9]);
   - lower-face, chin and nasal-tip deviation, plus cant angles (3–6° [11][12]).

   Put matching empties on bilateral landmarks and read the left–right Z/Y differences in the N-panel. Measure the angle of the bipupillary line and the commissure line against the head's horizontal.
4. **Expect most of the asymmetry low and lateral.** The lower and lateral face carries more than the brow/forehead region [2][7][1]. Spend asymmetry-matching effort on the jaw, chin, mouth and cheeks before the forehead.
5. **Sanity-check the size of the asymmetry you reconstruct.** Registering a normal adult head to its mirror should give a whole-face RMS on the order of 1 mm [6][7], with a larger value expected in older subjects [7].
   - If the photo-derived mesh shows several mm of global RMS asymmetry, suspect head yaw or roll in the photos, lens perspective, or a misplaced midline plane before you believe it is anatomy.
   - If the RMS is near 0, the likeness has probably been symmetrised away.
6. **Don't confuse the mesh's X = 0 plane with the face's midline.** Real midline landmarks deviate, and lower-face landmarks can drift opposite to upper ones [1]. Fit the "plane of maximum symmetry" [6] first, then sculpt asymmetry relative to it. Do not force nasion, pronasale and pogonion onto x = 0.
7. **If you have to mirror** (for example, one side is occluded in every photo), do not mirror a raw half-face. Hemiface duplication distorts feature sizes [15]. Blend the face with its mirror instead (average the two), then put back the asymmetries you can see in the photos.
8. **Judge renders the way they will be viewed.** Short glimpses hide asymmetries that long inspection reveals [9]. Do the likeness review with side-by-side, flicker or overlay comparison against the photos at matched camera settings.

## Algorithmic implications

- **Shape decomposition.** Represent each fitted shape as X = S + A, with S symmetric and A antisymmetric. Compute S by reflecting X (x → −x), swapping left and right landmark labels, and aligning the mirrored copy back to X with a proper-rotation similarity transform (`face3d/geometry/procrustes.py::umeyama`). Then set S = ½(X + aligned mirror) and A = X − S. This also yields the plane of maximum symmetry [6] as a by-product.
- **Asymmetry prior: shrink, never zero.** Put a zero-mean Gaussian prior on A, scaled so the expected whole-face RMS of 2A (face vs mirror) is about 0.8 mm [6][7]. Make it region-anisotropic, tighter in the upper third (0.5 mm) than the middle and lower thirds (0.6 mm) [7]. Let per-landmark variance at lower and lateral bilateral pairs allow differences up to about 4–5 mm [2]. The per-region σ values are a design inference from these summary statistics, not published priors. Hard symmetry constraints should be an opt-in block-out mode only.
- **Age covariate.** Increase the asymmetry prior scale with age [7]. The slope has not been sourced; leave it as a tunable parameter.
- **Evidence gating.** Accept a non-zero A component only where it is consistent across two or more views, or survives re-estimation after pose and lens refinement. Head yaw and roll, and perspective, create apparent left–right differences in single photos. Down-weight A from a single image.
- **Perception-aware reporting.** Output four numbers for the user:
  - the global RMSD against the mirror (mm);
  - the RMSD per facial third;
  - the left–right difference per bilateral pair (mm);
  - the bipupillary, commissural and chin-deviation angles (degrees).

  Flag eyelid differences over 2 mm and mouth-corner or brow differences over 3 mm as "visible", using [10][9]. Flag cants of 3–6° for review [11][12]. Flagged items should go to the user for confirmation against the photos rather than being auto-removed.
- **Defaults.**
  - Symmetry mode "assist" (symmetrise during the coarse fit) for the coarse stage.
  - Asymmetric output by default.
  - Store A as a separate Blender shape key so it can be toggled.
  - Never apply Symmetrize after the asymmetric fit.
- **Tests.** Inject a known A into synthetic landmarks (`face3d/synthetic.py`). Check that S and A are recovered within tolerance under camera noise, and that the solver does not invent asymmetry when A = 0.

## Disagreements, myths & refuted claims

- **Different metrics give different numbers.** Child midline offsets are ~0.25 mm [1]. Adult bilateral landmark differences are 0.6–4.6 mm [2]. Whole-surface RMSD is ~0.8 mm [6][7]. 2D skeletal ranges are 0–12 mm [3]. These measure different things in different populations and must not be mixed. Note too that the per-third RMSDs (0.5–0.6 mm) are below the whole-face figure (0.8 mm) [7]. That is probably because each third is aligned on its own, but this is unconfirmed.
- **Perception thresholds disagree.**
  - Eyelid: 2 mm [10].
  - Mouth corner and brow: ~3 mm [9].
  - Lay vs specialist observers: 4 vs 2 mm [11].
  - General: 3–4 mm / 3–4° [12].
  - Lower-face deviation: 6°, seen only by orthodontists [11].

  The working range is 2–4 mm. Treat [4]'s "below 3 mm is undetectable" as a clinical convention: it conflicts with the 2 mm eyelid and specialist thresholds.
- **Myth: "symmetric = attractive = better likeness."** The symmetry-preference studies [13][14] concern attractiveness, and use blended faces rather than raw hemiface mirrors [15]. None of them tests identity or likeness.
- **Dropped as unsourced (not refuted; no URL was found):**
  - a Raine Study dense-correspondence result (>50% of young-adult faces with more than 2 mm asymmetry; midline deviating to the right);
  - 2024 perception thresholds at asymmetry-index values of ≈1.1 and ≈3.1;
  - "directional asymmetry does not affect symmetry or attractiveness judgements, fluctuating asymmetry does";
  - "attractiveness correlates only weakly with 3D asymmetry indices".
- **Refuted:** none. No independent searches could be run in this pass.

## Open questions

- Is there direct evidence that mirroring a 3D or photographic face reduces identity recognition or likeness ratings? None was found in this run.
- Is there a consistent directional asymmetry (one side systematically larger, or the midline deviating to one side) in adults? The Raine claim is unsourced.
- What is the surface-level asymmetry by region beyond facial thirds: nose, chin, orbits, ears?
- What is the age slope of asymmetry, and does it differ by sex? Only the direction of the age effect is sourced [7].
- How much apparent asymmetry do head yaw/roll and lens focal length introduce in single-photo reconstruction? This is needed to set the evidence-gating thresholds.
- Expression (dynamic) asymmetry for blendshape rigs is not covered here.
- Re-verify against full texts: the 0.80 ± 0.24 mm attribution [6], the 3–4 mm / 3–4° statement [12], and the 0.25 mm child offset [1].
