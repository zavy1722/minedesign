# Cranial vault and back-of-head shape under hair

> Hair hides the vault. A photo-only pipeline therefore sees an upper bound (the hair envelope) plus a few true scalp observations: hairline, parting, nape margin and the skin around the ears. Everything else must come from measurements and priors. Adult vault size is tightly distributed (CV ≈ 3–4 % for g–op and eu–eu, ≈ 3 % for circumference), but vault shape varies between populations (mean cephalic index ≈ 72–88) and within them, through deformational flattening acquired in infancy that persists in a minority of adults. Face shape predicts vault shape only weakly, so a face-conditioned cranium is a mean with nearly the full prior spread. The cheap, strong fixes are a controlled-hair capture (wet, slicked or capped hair) and three tape measurements, which the engine encodes as one-sided silhouette constraints and arc-length equalities. ## Key facts

**Normative size and shape**
- **Head length** g–op on living adults (instrument compresses the hair) is ≈ 192–200 mm in men and 182–190 mm in women, SD 6–7 mm (CV ≈ 3.5 %) 🧠. **Head breadth** eu–eu is ≈ 150–157 / 143–149 mm, SD ≈ 5–6 🧠. **Circumference** (occipitofrontal, maximum) is ≈ 565–580 / 545–560 mm, SD ≈ 15 🧠. These are consistent with (lib: anatomy/anthropometric-norms.md).
- **US Army surveys** (ANSUR 1988 and ANSUR II 2012; the public ANSUR II release holds 4,082 men and 1,986 women, lib: anatomy/anthropometric-norms.md) measure head length, head breadth, head circumference and tragion-to-top-of-head. Their means sit slightly above the Farkas North American White (NAW) values, consistent with a mixed-ancestry sample and hair under the instrument ↔️. Exact ANSUR II means and SDs were not available to this step 🧠.
- **The cephalic index** CI = 100·eu–eu/g–op shifts with population: West African and African-American samples run ≈ 72–78, Northwest European ≈ 75–80, Central European and Balkan ≈ 80–86, East Asian ≈ 80–88. The within-population SD is ≈ 3–4 points ↔️. Martin–Saller bands: dolicho < 76, meso 76–80.9, brachy 81–85.4, hyperbrachy ≥ 85.5 (lib: anatomy/population-variation.md) 🧠.
- **Chinese vs Caucasian heads.** SizeChina-vs-CAESAR comparisons (Ball et al. 2010) found Chinese heads rounder, with a flatter back and forehead 🧠. Back-of-head flatness is therefore partly a population trait, not always a deformity.
- **Plasticity.** CI changes across generations and with infant care. 20th-century secular change in vault shape and Boas's immigrant study are real, but their size is disputed (Sparks & Jantz 2002 vs Gravlee et al. 2003) ↔️. Cradle-boarding flattens the occiput; how much of the "planoccipital" Armenian and Dinaric form it explains is debated ↔️.
- **Top view.** The vault is an egg, not a ball. The widest point lies behind the ear canal, at the parietal eminences, about 55–70 % of g–op from glabella. Minimum frontal width is ≈ 0.70–0.76 of eu–eu 🧠.
- **Profile, in the Frankfort horizontal (FH) frame:**
  - The g–op chord runs within ≈ ±10° of FH 🧠.
  - Glabella is ≈ 30–45 mm above FH, and opisthocranion ≈ 20–50 mm above it 🧠.
  - Inion (the external occipital protuberance, palpable) sits about −5 to +20 mm from FH, 2–5 cm below opisthocranion ↔️.
  - Vertex usually lies 0–25 mm behind the tragion vertical ↔️.
  - Tragion lies at ≈ 0.47–0.55 of the g′–op′ projection from glabella, leaving ≈ 90–105 mm of head behind the tragus in men 🧠.
  - Auricular height, tragion to vertex, is ≈ 125–140 mm (M) and 120–133 mm (F) ↔️.
- **The profile square.** Pronasale to the back of the head ≈ vertex to chin (within ≈ 5 %); breadth ≈ 0.67 × total head height 🧠.
- **Bone vs skin.** Dry-skull maximum length is ≈ 178–195 mm (M) and 170–185 (F); maximum breadth ≈ 132–145 / 128–140 🧠. Living head length exceeds skull length by ≈ 9–13 mm (glabella ≈ 5–6 mm of tissue plus occiput ≈ 5–8). Living breadth exceeds skull breadth by ≈ 10–16 mm, since temporalis and fascia add bulk at the level of euryon 🧠. Vault scalp (skin, galea, loose areolar tissue, pericranium) is ≈ 4–7 mm, thicker at the inion and nuchal region ≈ 6–10 🧠.

**Deformational flattening**
- **Infants.** In a New Zealand prospective cohort (Hutchison et al. 2004), plagiocephaly or brachycephaly affected ≈ 16 % at 6 weeks, ≈ 20 % at 4 months, ≈ 9 % at 8 months, ≈ 7 % at 12 months and ≈ 3 % at 24 months 🧠. A Canadian cohort (Mawji et al. 2013) reported ≈ 47 % at 7–12 weeks, mostly mild and more often right-sided 🧠.
- **Adults.** I know of no reliable population prevalence. Clearly visible residual asymmetry is plausibly in the low single-digit percent, and subtle asymmetry (cranial vault asymmetry index CVAI ≈ 2–4 %) is common ↔️. The 1992–94 "back to sleep" campaigns raised infant occipital flattening, and those cohorts are now ≈ 30–34 years old. The effect on adult CI is plausible but unquantified ↔️.
- **Three shapes to recognise:**
  - Deformational plagiocephaly, seen from above, is a **parallelogram**: one posterior quadrant flat, the ear on the same side displaced forward, the forehead on that side bossed forward, and the opposite occiput bulging. Mild facial asymmetry often accompanies it 🧠.
  - Positional brachycephaly is a **symmetric flat back**: high CI, a nearly vertical occiput in profile, a widened posterior parietal region and often a taller back vault 🧠.
  - Lambdoid synostosis (rare) is a **trapezoid**: the ear on the same side sits *posterior* and lower, with a mastoid bulge 🧠.
- **Scaphocephaly** (sagittal synostosis) gives a long, narrow head (CI ≈ 65–72) with a keel and a bulleted occiput. Craniosynostosis affects ≈ 1 in 2,000–2,500 births, and sagittal is the commonest type 🧠. A very long, narrow head may simply be real.
- **Clinical metrics** carry over to adult meshes. CVA is the difference of the two transcranial diagonals at ±30° from the midline. CVAI = CVA / longer diagonal × 100. On the Children's Healthcare of Atlanta (CHOA) scale, CVAI ≥ 3.5 % is plagiocephaly and the grade boundaries fall at 6.25, 8.75 and 11 % 🧠. Infant brachycephaly thresholds range from CI > 90 to > 95 by author ↔️.

**Scalp landmarks visible through hair**
- **Hairline landmarks** are trichion, the frontotemporal recess apices, the temporal peaks, the sideburn bottom and the midline nape hairline (usually near earlobe level to 2–3 cm below, beneath the inion) 🧠. They are skin observations of position, never of scale (lib: anatomy/forehead-brow-temple-hairline.md).
- **The parting line** is a 1D curve of true scalp. **The crown whorl** sits several cm behind the vertex and 0–3 cm off the midline. Most whorls are clockwise, and a minority of people have two ↔️.
- **The ears** constrain the side of the head when hair is tucked behind them: the skin of the side of the head at superaurale, the otobasion points and the mastoid 🧠.

**Hair over the scalp**
- **Strands** are ≈ 50–120 µm thick, at ≈ 100k–150k hairs (lib: blender/hair-blender.md). Flattened hair 10 cm long packs to roughly 1–2 mm (density × length × diameter ÷ packing), and long hair builds 3–8 mm where it accumulates. Dry hair adds 5–150 mm depending on class (Table 3) 🧠.
- **A tape over hair** reads long by ΔC ≈ θ_hair · t_c, where t_c is the compressed hair thickness and θ_hair ≈ 3.3–3.8 rad is the turning angle of the haired arc from temple to temple around the back. Short straight hair adds ≈ 3–10 mm; dense coily hair adds 10–25 mm, which approaches one population SD 🧠 (derived geometry).
- **Visual hull error.** A visual hull from n evenly spaced views around the head overshoots a circular section of radius R by up to R·(sec(π/2n) − 1). For R ≈ 95 mm that is ≈ 8 mm with 4 views, ≈ 2 mm with 8 and ≈ 1 mm with 12. A top view is needed for the vertex 🧠 (derived geometry).

**Face → cranium**
- **Correlation.** Within a population, bizygomatic width predicts head breadth moderately (r ≈ 0.4–0.6). Face height and face width predict head length weakly (r ≈ 0.1–0.35). Facial size predicts circumference at r ≈ 0.4–0.6. Face shape after size removal explains little vault shape (R² ≈ 0.05–0.2) ↔️.
- **Why.** Integration runs through the cranial base (Lieberman et al. 2000; Enlow's dolicho→lepto / brachy→eury counterpart principle). It is clear across species and populations but weak for predicting an individual ↔️. Even at r = 0.5 the residual SD is 87 % of the prior SD 🧠.
- **Sex and ancestry**, inferred from the face, shift the cranial *mean* (CI, size) more than any direct shape regression does 🧠.

**Statistical head models**
- **LYHM** (Dai, Pears, Smith & Duncan, ICCV 2017; IJCV 2020) is built from Headspace: 3dMD full-head scans of ≈ 1,500 UK subjects in tight caps, of which ≈ 1,200 are in the model. Those counts are confirmed in (lib: reconstruction/face-scan-datasets.md) against the York project page. The exclusions are reportedly poor scans such as hair bulging under the cap ↔️. It provides global and age/sex-specific models; the cranium is completed by fitting the PCA to the observed face only and reading off the covariance-implied vault 🧠.
- **UHM** (Ploumpis et al., CVPR 2019; TPAMI 2021) fuses LSFM's ≈ 9.7k-person face space with LYHM's head space, by regressing from face to head and by combining covariances. Its vault variability is therefore still the ≈ 1,200 Headspace subjects 🧠. HIFI3D++ also folds in Headspace ↔️.
- **No open, commercially usable *statistical* vault prior is known to me.** LYHM, UHM and Headspace are academic and non-commercial (lib: reconstruction/3dmm-overview.md); time-sensitive ↔️. FLAME 2023 Open (CC BY 4.0, lib: reconstruction/flame-model.md) has a scalp, but its identity scans (CAESAR) were not hair-controlled, so its vault is a smoothed scalp-plus-hair surface ↔️. MakeHuman/MPFB head targets are artist-made, not statistical 🧠.

## Applying this to photo→3D face sculpting in Blender

1. **Capture a shape set for the vault**, with consent and separate from the groom set (lib: blender/hair-blender.md).
   - Hair: wet and combed flat, or under a nylon wig cap topped by a silicone swim cap. Long hair goes in a low, flat braid pinned to the nape, never a crown bun.
   - Views: 8 at eye level, every 45°; 4 elevated 30–45°; 1 straight down; all at ≥ 1.5 m.
   - Tangent props: one profile with a ruler held vertically against the back of the head (the tangent point marks op′), and one with a level board resting on the head (marks v).
2. **Take three tapes and two boards** (≈ 5 minutes).
   - Maximum OFC, snug, just above the brows and over the occipital bulge: take the largest of three readings.
   - Sagittal arc from nasion over the vertex to the palpated inion.
   - Bitragion arc over the vertex.
   - Head length: back of the head against a wall, a book flat on the glabella parallel to the wall, measure wall to book. Head breadth: two books above the ears.
   - Log the hair state for each reading, so the tape correction from Table 3 can be applied.
3. **Set up a frame.** Keep 1 BU = 1 m with millimetre display. Create an FH frame empty from tragion and soft orbitale. Place empties `LM.g`, `LM.op`, `LM.eu.L/R`, `LM.v`, `LM.t.L/R`, `LM.i`, `LM.tr`, `LM.nape` and `LM.whorl` (lib: anatomy/craniometric-landmarks.md).
4. **Block the vault.**
   - Fit the face first. Then scale only the cranium (vertex group fading out at the brows and in front of the ears) with a 3×3×3 Lattice aligned to FH, to the target L, B and t–v.
   - Then adjust shape keys: occipital fullness, parietal-eminence width, vertex AP position, symmetric flattening, and plagiocephaly L/R (parallelogram shear plus a flat posterior patch).
5. **Check the loop.** Slice a copy with Bisect through `LM.g` and `LM.op`, or use a script operator, and read the loop length. It should be within ±5 mm of the tape minus θ_hair·t_c. Then check, in order:
   - Top view: egg-shaped, widest behind the ears.
   - Profile square holds.
   - Ear at ≈ half the depth.
   - Occiput overhanging the nape, with op′ above the inion.
   - ≈ 90–105 mm of head behind the tragus (M).
6. **Hair-on photos only.** Build the vault from the prior, then add a hair shell (Solidify or Displace by class, Table 3) and compare silhouettes through every solved camera.
   - Scalp outside the hair outline means the vault is too big. Fix it, because it is impossible.
   - A hair shell too thin everywhere is ambiguous (bigger vault or thicker hair): keep the prior and flag it.
   - A shell too thick only behind means the back of the head is too short.
7. **Asymmetry.** Do not mirror the vault blindly. A parallelogram (frontal photo shows one ear further forward; top view skewed) goes into the plagiocephaly shape key. It matters for bald or cropped subjects and for the hair silhouette, and hardly at all under voluminous hair.
8. **Bald or buzzed subjects** show vault topography. Sculpt the temporal crest and plane break, parietal eminences, the occasional sagittal keel or post-bregmatic dip, lambdoid flattening, inion bump, nuchal folds and superficial temporal vessels. Keep the scalp a thin, even shell (≈ 4–7 mm) over the bone.
9. **What goes wrong:**
   - The back of the head is too short (the commonest blocking error).
   - The skull is drawn as a sphere, or as a "lightbulb" widest at the temples.
   - The vertex is too far forward.
   - The neck attaches at the back of the skull, with no occipital overhang.
   - The vault is sculpted to the hair silhouette.
   - The tape is used uncorrected over coily hair.
   - The occiput is flattened by a reference shot against a wall or headrest.
   - Trichion is used for scale.
   - A wide selfie lens is used: perspective shrinks the cranium relative to the face.

## Algorithmic implications

- **Parameterise the vault separately from the face.** Use L = g′–op′, B = eu′–eu′, H = t–v in FH, `vertex_ap_mm`, `op_height_fh_mm`, `occ_fullness`, `parietal_width_ratio`, `frontal_width_ratio`, `flat_sym_mm` and `plagio = (shear_deg, flat_mm, side)`. Drive a template through RBF/thin-plate anchors at the face landmarks plus g′, v, op′, eu′, t, i′ and nape, with cotangent-Laplacian smoothing; never stretch the face 🧠.
- **Log-normal size priors.** For a matched population and sex use σ_log(L) = 0.035, σ_log(B) = 0.037, σ_log(C) = 0.028 and σ_log(H) = 0.045. With unknown ancestry widen L to 0.05 and B to 0.065, because CI varies between populations 🧠. Within-population correlations: r(L,B) ≈ 0.3, r(L,H) ≈ 0.3, r(B,H) ≈ 0.2; C is derived from the mesh, not sampled 🧠.
- **Shape priors.**
  - `vertex_ap_mm` ~ N(10, 10) behind tragion; `op_height_fh_mm` ~ N(35, 10) 🧠.
  - `flat_sym_mm` ~ N(0, 4), with a heavier right tail for cohorts born after ≈ 1992 ↔️.
  - CVAI ~ mixture: 0.9·HalfNormal(σ = 2 %) + 0.1·N(6 %, 2 %) ↔️.
- **Face → vault.** With a licensed joint model (LYHM, UHM), condition the Gaussian on the face and use the conditional covariance Σ_cc − Σ_cf Σ_ff⁻¹ Σ_fc. Otherwise regress the scalars with ρ(B|zy–zy) = 0.5, ρ(L|face) = 0.25 and ρ(H|face) = 0.3, and set σ_post = σ·√(1 − ρ²) 🧠. Report this residual to the user.
- **Observation terms:**
  - Hair silhouette: one-sided hinge max(0, d_out) on the projected scalp per view, weight ≥ 10× the skin terms. Add a soft term toward (hair outline − t_class) with σ = half the class range, using Huber or Tukey loss ↔️.
  - Skin points: parting, hairline, nape margin, superaurale and the forehead at σ ≈ 1–2 mm.
  - Controlled-hair silhouettes (Table 3 wet or capped rows): two-sided at σ ≈ 1.5–3 mm, after subtracting the class mean.
  - Tape: OFC loop length = C_tape − θ_hair·t_c, with σ² = 4² + (θ_hair·σ_t)² mm². Arcs: midsagittal and coronal plane-slice lengths, σ ≈ 5 mm.
  - Boards: L or B chords at σ ≈ 2–3 mm, plus the compressed hair thickness.
  - Tangent props: op′ and v as half-plane constraints.
- **Bone.** The skull mesh stays inside the scalp with an offset of 4–7 mm on the vault and 10–20 mm at the temporal fossa (log-normal, σ_log ≈ 0.25) 🧠.
- **Output.** Store a per-vertex `cranium_confidence` attribute (0 = prior only, 1 = measured) for the sculptor, and expose every parameter in mm as an N-panel slider. Fail loudly when |z| > 3 on L, B or C; it usually means a scale or focal-length error 🧠.
- **Tests.** Generate synthetic heads with known L, B and CVAI, add procedural hair shells from Table 3, render 4, 8 and 12 views, and require L and B within ±3 mm with controlled hair (±6 mm dry) 🧠.

## Disagreements, myths & open questions

- **Myth: "the cranium is a ball."** Loomis's sphere is a drawing scaffold. Real vaults are ovoid, widest behind the ears, and the sphere shortens the back of the head 🧠.
- **Myth: CI is a fixed racial marker.** It is heritable but plastic (infant posture, cradling, secular trend); the size of the Boas effect is disputed ↔️.
- **Survey offsets.** ANSUR and Farkas differ by method, hair compression and population; the hair term (≈ 1–3 mm on chords) is unmeasured ↔️.
- **Vertex definition.** "Vertex" in FH and vertex in natural head position differ by several mm ↔️.
- **Integration vs prediction.** Face–vault integration is strong in evolutionary studies but weak for individuals. Published cranium-completion errors for UHM/LYHM were not available to this step ↔️.
- **Open questions:**
  - adult plagiocephaly prevalence;
  - the back-to-sleep cohort effect on adult CI;
  - measured hair-envelope thickness by hair type (Table 3 is practitioner estimate);
  - Headspace cap bias at the crown;
  - LYHM/UHM licences and availability in late 2026;
  - whether any commercially usable full-head statistical model with hair-free vaults now exists ↔️.
