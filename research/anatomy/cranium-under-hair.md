# Cranial vault and back-of-head shape under hair

> Hair hides the vault. A photo-only pipeline sees an upper bound (the hair envelope) plus a few true scalp observations: hairline, parting, nape margin and the skin around the ears. Everything else comes from measurements and priors. Adult vault *size* is tightly distributed (CV ≈ 3–4 % for g–op and eu–eu, ≈ 3 % for circumference). Vault *shape* varies between populations (mean cephalic index ≈ 72–88) and within them, through deformational flattening acquired in infancy. Face shape predicts the vault only weakly, so the cheap, strong fixes are a controlled-hair capture (wet, slicked or capped hair) and three tape measurements, which the engine encodes as one-sided silhouette constraints and arc-length equalities. ## Key facts

**Normative size and shape**
- **Size** (living adults; the instrument compresses hair). Head length g–op ≈ 192–200 mm (M) and 182–190 (F), SD 6–7. Head breadth eu–eu ≈ 150–157 / 143–149, SD 5–6. Maximum occipitofrontal circumference (OFC) ≈ 565–580 / 545–560, SD ≈ 15. These are consistent with lib: anatomy/anthropometric-norms.md 🧠.
- **ANSUR II** (US Army 2012; public release 4,082 M / 1,986 F, lib: anthropometric-norms) reports head length, breadth, circumference and tragion–top of head. Its means sit slightly above Farkas North American White (NAW) values (mixed ancestry, hair under the instrument) ↔️; exact values not available here 🧠.
- **Cephalic index** (CI = 100·eu–eu/g–op), population means:
  - West African and African-American ≈ 72–78;
  - Northwest European ≈ 75–80;
  - Central European and Balkan ≈ 80–86;
  - East Asian ≈ 80–88.

  The SD within a population is ≈ 3–4 points ↔️. Martin–Saller bands: dolicho < 76, meso 76–80.9, brachy 81–85.4, hyperbrachy ≥ 85.5 🧠.
- **Flat backs can be normal.** SizeChina-vs-CAESAR comparison (Ball et al. 2010) found Chinese heads rounder, with flatter backs and foreheads, than Caucasian heads 🧠. A flat back can be population-typical, not a deformity.
- **Plasticity.** Infant posture, cradle-boarding and secular trend all change CI. The size of the Boas immigrant effect is disputed (Sparks & Jantz 2002 vs Gravlee et al. 2003) ↔️, and so is how much of the "planoccipital" Armenian and Dinaric form cradling explains ↔️.
- **Top view: an egg, not a ball.** The widest point is behind the ear canal, at the parietal eminences, about 55–70 % of g–op from glabella. Minimum frontal width (ft–ft) ≈ 0.70–0.76 × eu–eu 🧠.
- **Profile, in the Frankfort horizontal (FH) frame:**
  - The g–op chord runs within ±10° of FH. Glabella sits ≈ 30–45 mm above FH and opisthocranion ≈ 20–50 mm above it 🧠.
  - Inion (palpable) sits −5 to +20 mm from FH, 2–5 cm below op ↔️.
  - The vertex is 0–25 mm behind the tragion vertical ↔️.
  - Tragion sits at ≈ 0.47–0.55 of g′–op′ from glabella, leaving ≈ 90–105 mm of head behind it in men 🧠.
- **Profile square.** Pronasale→occiput ≈ vertex→chin (±5 %); breadth ≈ 0.67 × total head height 🧠.
- **Bone vs skin.** Dry-skull maximum length is ≈ 178–195 mm (M) and 170–185 (F); breadth ≈ 132–145 / 128–140. Living length exceeds skull length by ≈ 9–13 mm; living breadth exceeds skull breadth by ≈ 10–16, because temporalis adds bulk (Table 2) 🧠.

**Deformational flattening**
- **Infants** (Table 4). Prevalence peaks around 2–4 months (≈ 20–47 % by cohort and threshold) and falls to ≈ 3 % by 24 months; most cases are mild and right-sided cases are more common 🧠.
- **Adults.** I know of no reliable adult prevalence. Visible residual asymmetry is plausibly in the low single-digit percent; subtle asymmetry (CVAI ≈ 2–4 %) is common ↔️. "Back to sleep" (1992–94) raised infant occipital flattening, and those cohorts are now ≈ 30–34 years old. An effect on adult CI is plausible but unquantified ↔️.
- **Shapes from above:**
  - Deformational plagiocephaly is a **parallelogram**. One posterior quadrant is flat, the ear and forehead on that side sit forward, and the opposite occiput bulges; mild facial asymmetry is common 🧠.
  - Positional brachycephaly is a **symmetric flat back**: high CI, a near-vertical occiput in profile, a wide posterior parietal region and often a taller back vault 🧠.
  - Lambdoid synostosis (rare) is a **trapezoid**: the ear on the flat side sits *back* and low, with a mastoid bulge 🧠.
  - Scaphocephaly (sagittal synostosis) is long and narrow (CI ≈ 65–72), with a bulleted occiput. Craniosynostosis affects ≈ 1 in 2,000–2,500 births, and sagittal is the commonest type, so a very long, narrow head may simply be real 🧠.
- **Metrics.** CVA is the difference of the transcranial diagonals at ±30° from the midline; CVAI = CVA / longer diagonal × 100. On the Children's Healthcare of Atlanta (CHOA) scale, CVAI ≥ 3.5 % is plagiocephaly 🧠. Infant brachycephaly thresholds run from CI > 90 to > 95 by author ↔️.

**Scalp observations through hair**
- **Hairline points:** trichion, frontotemporal recess apices, temporal peaks, sideburn bottom and the nape hairline midpoint (near earlobe level to 2–3 cm below, beneath the inion). They give position, never scale 🧠 (lib: forehead-brow-temple-hairline).
- **Parting and whorl.** The parting line is a 1D curve of true scalp. The crown whorl sits several cm behind the vertex and 0–3 cm off the midline; it is usually clockwise, and some people have two ↔️.
- **Ears.** With hair tucked behind the ears, the skin around them shows the side of the head at superaurale, otobasion and mastoid 🧠.

**Hair geometry**
- **Flattened hair.** Strands are 50–120 µm across (lib: blender/hair-blender.md). Ten-centimetre hair flattened packs to ≈ 1–2 mm (density × length × diameter ÷ packing), and long hair builds 3–8 mm where it accumulates 🧠.
- **Tape over hair** reads long by ΔC ≈ θ_hair·t_c, where t_c is the compressed hair thickness and θ_hair ≈ 3.3–3.8 rad is the turning angle of the haired arc from temple round the back to temple. Short straight hair adds ≈ 2–8 mm, long straight ≈ 3–11, and dense coily hair 10–23 mm, close to one population SD 🧠 (derived geometry).
- **Visual hull from n evenly spaced views** overshoots a section of radius R by up to R·(sec(π/2n) − 1): ≈ 8 mm with 4 views, ≈ 2 mm with 8 and ≈ 1 mm with 12, for R = 95 mm. Add a top view for the vertex 🧠 (derived geometry).

**Face → cranium**
- **Correlations within a population:**
  - bizygomatic width → head breadth: r ≈ 0.4–0.6;
  - face size → head length: r ≈ 0.1–0.35 (a Swedish adult sample found head breadth–bizygomatic r ≈ 0.51 M / 0.77 F, with no significant head length–breadth correlation [4]; source text garbled, so treat as indicative);
  - face size → circumference: r ≈ 0.4–0.6;
  - face shape after size removal → vault shape: R² ≈ 0.05–0.2 ↔️.
- **Integration runs through the cranial base** (Lieberman et al. 2000; Enlow's dolicho→lepto, brachy→eury counterpart principle). It is strong across species and populations but weak for predicting an individual ↔️. At r = 0.5 the residual SD is still 87 % of the prior 🧠.
- **Sex and ancestry** read from the face shift the vault *mean* more than any shape regression does 🧠.

**Statistical head models**
- **LYHM** (Dai, Pears, Smith & Duncan, ICCV 2017; IJCV 2020) comes from Headspace: 3dMD full-head scans in tight caps, ≈ 1,500 UK subjects, ≈ 1,200 in the model (counts checked in lib: reconstruction/face-scan-datasets.md). Reportedly, poor scans such as hair bulging under the cap were excluded ↔️.
  - It offers global and age/sex models.
  - It completes the cranium by fitting the PCA to the visible face and reading the vault off the covariance 🧠.
- **UHM** (Ploumpis et al., CVPR 2019; TPAMI 2021) fuses LSFM's ≈ 9.7k-person face space with LYHM by face→head regression and covariance combination. Its vault variability is therefore still the ≈ 1,200 Headspace subjects 🧠. HIFI3D++ also includes Headspace ↔️.
- **No open, commercially usable statistical vault prior is known to me.**
  - LYHM and Headspace are academic and non-commercial per the University of York user agreements [2][3]; UHM terms were not found, and the status is time-sensitive ↔️.
  - FLAME 2023 Open (CC BY 4.0) has a scalp, but CAESAR scans were not hair-controlled, so its vault is a smoothed scalp-plus-hair surface ↔️.
  - MPFB/MakeHuman head targets are artist-made 🧠.

## Applying this to photo→3D face sculpting in Blender

1. **Shape-set capture**, with consent and separate from the groom set (lib: blender/hair-blender.md).
   - Hair: wet and combed flat, or under a nylon wig cap plus a silicone swim cap. Long hair goes in a low, flat braid pinned at the nape, never in a crown bun.
   - Views: 8 at eye level every 45°, 4 at 30–45° elevation and 1 overhead, all from ≥ 1.5 m.
   - Tangent props: one profile with a ruler held vertically against the back of the head (marks op′), one with a level board on top (marks v).
2. **Tapes and boards, about five minutes.**
   - Maximum OFC, snug: the largest of three readings.
   - Nasion→vertex→palpated inion arc.
   - Bitragion arc over the vertex.
   - Head length: back of the head against a wall and a book on the glabella; measure wall to book. Breadth: two books above the ears.
   - Log the hair state for every reading.
3. **Frame.** Keep 1 BU = 1 m with mm display. Build an FH empty from tragion and soft orbitale. Add empties `LM.g`, `LM.op`, `LM.eu.L/R`, `LM.v`, `LM.t.L/R`, `LM.i`, `LM.tr`, `LM.nape`, `LM.whorl` (lib: anatomy/craniometric-landmarks.md).
4. **Block the vault.**
   - Fit the face first.
   - Scale only the cranium to L, B and t–v with an FH-aligned 3×3×3 Lattice, weighted by a vertex group that fades out at the brows and in front of the ears.
   - Then adjust shape keys: occipital fullness, parietal width, vertex AP, symmetric flattening, plagiocephaly L/R (shear plus flat patch).
5. **Verify.** Bisect a copy through g′ and op′; the loop length must equal tape − θ_hair·t_c within ±5 mm. Then check:
   - Top view: egg-shaped, widest behind the ears.
   - Profile square.
   - Ear at about half depth.
   - Occiput overhanging the nape, with op′ above the inion.
   - 90–105 mm behind the tragus (M).
6. **Hair-on photos only.**
   - Build the vault from the prior.
   - Add a hair shell (Solidify or Displace by Table 3 class) and compare silhouettes through every solved camera.
   - Scalp outside the hair outline is impossible: shrink.
   - Shell too thin everywhere is ambiguous: keep the prior and flag it.
   - Shell too thick only at the back: the occiput is too short.
7. **Asymmetry.** Don't mirror blindly. One ear further forward in the frontal photo, or a skewed top view, goes into the plagiocephaly key. That matters for bald or cropped subjects, little under voluminous hair.
8. **Bald or buzzed subjects** show topography: the temporal crest, parietal eminences, the occasional sagittal keel or post-bregmatic dip, lambdoid flattening, the inion bump, nuchal folds and the temporal vessels. The scalp stays a thin, even 4–7 mm shell over bone.
9. **What goes wrong:** a short occiput (the commonest error); a sphere or "lightbulb" vault; the vertex too far forward; no occipital overhang above the neck; the vault sculpted to the hair silhouette; an uncorrected tape over coily hair; a reference back flattened by a headrest; trichion used for scale; wide-angle selfies, which shrink the cranium relative to the face.

## Algorithmic implications

- **Parameters.** Model the vault separately from the face: L = g′–op′, B = eu′–eu′, H = t–v (FH), `vertex_ap_mm`, `op_height_fh_mm`, `occ_fullness`, `parietal_width_ratio`, `frontal_width_ratio`, `flat_sym_mm` and `plagio = (shear_deg, flat_mm, side)`. Warp the template with RBF/thin-plate anchors at the face landmarks plus g′, v, op′, eu′, t, i′ and nape, with cotangent-Laplacian smoothing; the face is never stretched 🧠.
- **Size priors (log-normal).** For a matched population: σ_log L = 0.035, B = 0.037, C = 0.028, H = 0.045. With unknown ancestry, L = 0.05 and B = 0.065. Correlations r(L,B) ≈ 0.3, r(L,H) ≈ 0.3, r(B,H) ≈ 0.2. C is derived from the mesh 🧠.
- **Shape priors:**
  - `vertex_ap_mm` ~ N(10, 10); `op_height_fh_mm` ~ N(35, 10) 🧠.
  - `flat_sym_mm` ~ N(0, 4), with a heavier tail for cohorts born after 1992 ↔️.
  - CVAI ~ 0.9·HalfNormal(2 %) + 0.1·N(6 %, 2 %) ↔️.
- **Face → vault.** With a licensed joint model, condition the Gaussian on the face: Σ_cc − Σ_cf Σ_ff⁻¹ Σ_fc. Otherwise regress the scalars with ρ(B|zy) = 0.5, ρ(L|face) = 0.25 and ρ(H|face) = 0.3, and set σ_post = σ√(1 − ρ²). Show the residual to the user 🧠.
- **Observation terms:**
  - Hair silhouette: one-sided hinge max(0, d_out) per view, weighted ≥ 10× the skin terms, plus a soft pull to (outline − t_class) with σ = half the class range under Huber loss ↔️.
  - Skin points (parting, hairline, nape, superaurale): σ 1–2 mm.
  - Controlled-hair silhouettes: two-sided, σ 1.5–3 mm, after subtracting the class mean.
  - OFC loop = C_tape − θ_hair·t_c, with σ² = 4² + (θ_hair·σ_t)²; arcs as plane-slice lengths, σ 5 mm.
  - Board chords: σ 2–3 mm.
  - Tangent props: half-plane constraints.
- **Skull.** Keep it inside the scalp: vault 4–7 mm, temporal fossa 10–20 mm (σ_log ≈ 0.25) 🧠.
- **Output and gates.** Store a per-vertex `cranium_confidence` attribute (0 = prior, 1 = measured) and expose mm sliders in the N-panel. Fail loudly at |z| > 3 on L, B or C: that is usually a scale or focal-length error 🧠.
- **Tests.** Use synthetic heads with known L, B and CVAI, add Table 3 hair shells and render 4, 8 and 12 views. Require ±3 mm on L and B with controlled hair and ±6 mm dry 🧠.

## Disagreements, myths & open questions

- **Myth: "the cranium is a ball."** Loomis's sphere is a drawing scaffold; real vaults are ovoid and widest behind the ears, and the sphere shortens the back 🧠.
- **Myth: CI is a fixed racial marker.** It is heritable but plastic ↔️.
- **ANSUR vs Farkas** differ by method, hair compression and population; the hair term (≈ 1–3 mm on chords) is unmeasured ↔️.
- **Vertex in FH vs natural head position** differs by several mm ↔️.
- **Face–vault integration** is strong in evolutionary data and weak for individuals; UHM/LYHM completion errors were not available here ↔️.
- **Open questions:** adult plagiocephaly prevalence; the back-to-sleep effect on adult CI; measured hair-envelope thickness by type (Table 3 is practitioner estimate); Headspace cap bias at the crown; the 2026 licence and availability of LYHM/UHM; whether a commercially usable full-head model with hair-free vaults now exists ↔️.
