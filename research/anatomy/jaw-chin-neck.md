# Mandible, chin and neck

> The lower-face and neck silhouette stacks four layers. Bone sets ramus height, gonial angle, bigonial width and the chin; the masseter sits on top; then fat and gland (jowl, submental, submandibular); then the neck frame of hyoid, larynx, sternocleidomastoid (SCM) and platysma. On average men have a larger, taller, squarer and more everted mandible, a wider chin and a visible laryngeal prominence, and women a smaller, more tapered jaw and a narrower, rounder chin, but every metric overlaps between the sexes. Chin projection, gonial angle and cervicomental angle are nearly invisible frontally and shift with head pitch, mouth opening, beard and lighting, so a fitter must read them from camera-solved profile and 3/4 views and model head–neck articulation explicitly. No harvest or draft existed for this topic, so all values are expert knowledge. Sibling notes (`skull-osteology`, `muscles-mastication-jaw`, `muscles-lower-face`, `craniometric-landmarks`) hold sourced versions of some overlapping values.

## Key facts

**Mandible**
- **Surface parts.** The symphysis and triangular mental protuberance, with paired mental tubercles at its lower corners. The inferior border is the jawline. The antegonial notch lies just in front of the angle. Gonion is the angle. The posterior ramus rises to the condyle, about 10–15 mm in front of the tragus. 🧠
- **Gonial angle** (ramus tangent to border tangent). The adult mean is about 120–125° and the typical range about 110–135°. It is about 150–160° at birth. ✅ for the adult mean [1][2] (cephalometric means 121–125°; CBCT samples run higher, about 125–129° [7]); range and newborn value remain 🧠
- **Sex.** Verified samples disagree: men about 1.6° smaller [1], no difference [2], or men 3–3.5° smaller in a CBCT sample [7]. Treat the difference as 0–4° with heavy overlap. ↔️ The angle flares outward (eversion) more often in men and with strong masseters. 🧠
- **The vertical pattern is the main shape axis after size.**
  - High-angle ("long") faces: obtuse gonial angle, short ramus, steep border, long lower face, retrusive chin, obtuse cervicomental angle.
  - Low-angle faces: the reverse, with a square lower face, prominent chin and deep mentolabial sulcus. 🧠
- **Bigonial width.** Bone width is M ≈ 90–105 mm and F ≈ 84–97 mm, about 0.70–0.80 × bizygomatic width. A photo silhouette adds masseter, fat and parotid on each side. 🧠 ↔️
- **Ramus flexure.** It was proposed as a male trait (Loth & Henneberg), but replications found much lower accuracy. ↔️
- **Aging.** Ramus height, body height and body length fall, and the angle opens a few degrees, mostly after tooth loss. Whether it opens in fully dentate adults is debated. ↔️ The prejowl sulcus deepens as bone resorbs in front of the mandibular ligament. 🧠

**Chin**
- **Soft-tissue cover.** About 10–12 mm over pogonion and 6–9 mm over menton. The soft chin therefore tracks bony projection but blurs bony shape. Soft tissue follows a bony advancement at about 0.75–0.9 : 1. 🧠 (sourced in skull-osteology)
- **Sex.** Male chins are taller, wider and squarer. The two tubercles make a broad front plane with "corners", sometimes with a midline dip or cleft. Female chins are narrower and rounded or pointed. Forensic standards score the mental eminence 1–5 (Buikstra & Ubelaker; Walker). 🧠
- **Chin-width ideals.** Surgical convention puts the edges of a female chin below the alae or medial canthi, and a male chin wider, toward the canines. These are surgical ideals, not population norms. ↔️
- **Projection references** (Table 2): the Ricketts E-line, the Gonzalez-Ulloa zero meridian, the Holdaway H-angle, Legan–Burstone convexity and the Merrifield Z-angle. All are mainly white-adult norms. Lips sit nearer to, or ahead of, the E-line in African and East-Asian norms and in youth. ↔️
- **Midline.** A chin deviation of up to about 2–4 mm is normal. 🧠

**Jaw–neck junction**
- **The jawline** is a plane break between the lateral face plane and the under-jaw plane. Its sharpness depends on the border angle, submental and jowl fat, platysma tone and skin laxity far more than on masseter size. 🧠
- **Submental layers**, from the surface: preplatysmal fat; subplatysmal fat; the anterior digastrics; the submandibular glands, which bulge laterally and drop with age; and the hyoid. A "double chin" can come from any layer, from retrognathia or microgenia, from a low hyoid, or from neck flexion. 🧠
- **Cervicomental angle (CMA)**, between the submental tangent (Me′→C) and the anterior-neck tangent. The youthful ideal (Ellenbogen–Karlin) is 105–120°. Measured adults are often 115–135° or more, opening with BMI and age. ↔️
- **Ellenbogen–Karlin youthful neck.** A distinct border with no jowl, a subhyoid depression, a visible thyroid bulge, a visible anterior SCM border, and a CMA of 105–120°. 🧠
- **Dedo classes.** I minimal, II skin laxity, III fat, IV platysmal bands, V retrognathia, VI low hyoid. 🧠
- **Hyoid.** It sits at C3–C4, with the cervical point C over it. A high, posterior hyoid makes a crisp angle. A low, anterior one makes an obtuse angle that removing fat cannot fix. 🧠
- **Head pitch.** Flexion and extension at the craniocervical junction change the CMA roughly degree for degree, and flexion rolls submental tissue. A 10° chin-down tilt can make a 115° neck read as about 105° with a fold. 🧠 (geometric)

**Neck frame**
- **SCM.** The sternal head arises from the manubrium and the clavicular head from the medial clavicle. It inserts on the mastoid and the lateral superior nuchal line. It shows as an oblique band from behind the lobe to the sternoclavicular joint; the sternal tendons frame the jugular notch, and the lesser supraclavicular fossa lies between the heads. Turning the head makes the opposite SCM pop. 🧠
- **Retromandibular hollow.** The SCM passes behind the angle. The hollow between the ramus, lobe and mastoid is filled by parotid and is a key 3/4 shading cue. 🧠
- **Thyroid cartilage.** The laminae meet at about 90° in men and about 120° in women. The prominence (C4–C5) is therefore far more visible in men; lean women may show a little. It rises about 1.5–3 cm on swallowing. The notch lies about 1–2 cm below the hyoid. 🧠
- **Neck form.** A cylinder tilted about 10–20° forward, with oblique ends. At the back it joins the occiput near ear-lobe or nose-base height and drops to C7, which sits higher than the jugular notch in front. At the front it joins the face at C. 🧠 (artist convention)
- **Size.** Circumference is about 37–42 cm in men and 31–36 cm in women. In muscular men, frontal neck width approaches jaw-silhouette width. In most women the neck reads clearly narrower than the jaw. 🧠
- **Platysma** runs from the chest fascia to the border, lower lip and modiolus. Its medial bands appear with age, and decussation types are contested (see muscles-lower-face). 🧠
- **Jaw motion.** For the first 20–25 mm of incisal opening the jaw rotates about the condyles, then translates forward. Normal maximum opening is about 40–55 mm. Freeway space at rest is about 2–4 mm, so "teeth apart" lengthens the lower face. 🧠

## Applying this to photo→3D face sculpting in Blender

1. **Capture.**
   - Both true profiles in natural head position: FH level, eyes on the horizon, teeth lightly together, lips relaxed.
   - Keep the neck neutral. Neither tucked nor jutted forward: the "turtle" portrait trick tightens the submental line.
   - Add a frontal, 3/4 views at 30–50° yaw, and one low 3/4 from about 15° below eye level for the under-jaw plane.
   - Collar off, hair behind the ears, beard noted. Shoot from 1.5 m or more with an 85–135 mm equivalent lens.
   - Optional: a swallow frame and a head-turn frame for the SCM.
2. **Solve cameras and head pose first.** Log each photo's pitch relative to FH. Chin height, CMA and apparent jaw width all move with pitch.
3. **Profile landmarks.**
   - Place empties at soft nasion, subnasale, stomion, Pog′ and Me′.
   - Add C (the deepest point of the submental–neck angle), the thyroid prominence, the tragus, and soft gonion as the intersection of the two tangents.
   - Measure convexity, the E-line, CMA, the lower-face–throat angle and Sn–Gn′ : C–Gn′ against Tables 2–3. Any outlier means rechecking pose, expression and posture.
4. **Bone, in a fixed order.**
   - Profile: ramus, gonial angle, border slope (FMA ≈ 25°) and chin.
   - 3/4: the border path and the antegonial notch.
   - Frontal last: split the width into bone, masseter and fat (see muscles-mastication-jaw).
5. **Chin box.** Under a high front key, a typical male chin highlight is a wide trapezoid and a typical female one a narrower oval. The chin underside is a separate plane meeting the front at menton. With FH level, soft gonion usually sits about 0.5–2 cm below the stomion line, and higher in high-angle faces. 🧠 (derived, uncertain)
6. **Neck.**
   - Block a forward-tilted cylinder with oblique ends.
   - Run the SCM bands to the jugular notch, passing behind the angle, with the retromandibular hollow under the lobe.
   - Place the larynx about 1.5–2.5 cm below C: a keel in men, a soft swell or nothing in women.
   - Vertex groups: `submental_fat`, `jowl_L/R`, `submandibular_gland_L/R`, `scm_L/R`, `larynx`, `platysma_band_L/R`.
7. **Rig.**
   - Put the jaw pivot at the condyles (10–15 mm in front of the tragus, at about tragus height), never at gonion. Add forward translation beyond about 20 mm of opening.
   - Put the head-pitch pivot deep at the upper cervical spine, about level with the mastoid tips.
   - Add yaw-driven SCM correctives and a `swallow` key.
8. **Light check.** Under a grazing top key, the mandibular border is the terminator on the jaw. The sharper cast-shadow edge lies lower, on the neck.

**What goes wrong:**
- Reading the neck cast shadow, or a beard line trimmed 1–2 cm below the border, as the jaw border. The jaw ends up too low or too sharp.
- Chin-down photos bake a double chin into identity. Chin-up photos lengthen the lower third and over-sharpen the jaw.
- Parted lips make the lower face 2–5 mm too long.
- Wide-angle selfies make the jaw and neck too narrow.
- A real chin deviation gets symmetrised.
- Male or white norms are applied to everyone.
- The neck becomes a vertical tube, or too thin under a heavy jaw. The Adam's apple ends up at jaw level. The SCM inserts in front of the ear.

## Algorithmic implications

- **Parameters.**
  - Bone: θ_go, ramus height R, bony bigonial width B, mandibular-plane angle MP, chin projection P (relative to the N′-perpendicular), chin height and width, squareness s_c ∈ [0, 1], midline deviation d.
  - Soft tissue: t_pog, t_me, submental fat f_sm, jowl, gland ptosis.
  - Neck: hyoid height and AP position, circumference, SCM prominence, larynx prominence L.
  - Per photo: head–neck pitch, yaw and roll; jaw rotation and protrusion.
- **Latent vertical pattern.** Draw z_v ~ N(0, 1) and couple: θ_go += 5z_v, MP += 5z_v, P −= 3z_v mm, lower-face height += 3.5z_v mm, CMA += 4z_v°. Add residual noise (σ_θ ≈ 4°). The coefficients are heuristics to tune. 🧠
- **Priors** (`data/jaw-chin-neck.json`). All 🧠:
  - θ_go: M N(120, 6²), F N(124, 6²), unknown N(122, 7²); bounds 105–145°.
  - B: M N(97, 6²), F N(90, 5²) mm.
  - R: M N(60, 5²), F N(54, 4²) mm.
  - t_pog: log-normal, median 11.5 mm, σ_log 0.20.
  - t_me: log-normal, median 7.5 mm, σ_log 0.25.
  - Convexity: N(12, 5²)°.
  - Lower lip to E-line: N(−2, 2.5²) mm (σ 3 if ancestry is unknown).
  - CMA in canonical pose: N(118, 12²), bounds 90–160°, +0.8° per BMI unit above 25 (heuristic).
  - Lower-face–throat angle: N(100, 8²)°.
  - f_sm: log-normal, median 5 mm, σ_log 0.5.
  - L: M Beta(4, 2), F Beta(1.5, 6); placed 15–25 mm below C.
  - Neck circumference: M N(39.5, 2.5²), F N(33.5, 2.5²) cm.
  - Head–neck pitch per photo: N(0, 10²)°.
  - Rest-frame jaw opening: half-normal, σ = 2°.
  - d: Laplace(0, 1.5 mm); confirm anything over 4 mm with the user.
- **Observations.**
  - Jawline, chin and neck front are occluding contours. Use silhouette residuals with correspondences recomputed every iteration.
  - Detector points (dlib 0–16, MediaPipe face oval and point 152) only initialise. No standard detector gives C, larynx, SCM or gonion.
  - In an FH-aligned profile, Pog′ is the most anterior chin point, Me′ the lowest, and C the curvature maximum between Me′ and the neck. Gonion is the tangent intersection.
  - Weight the frontal jaw contour low for bone and the profile high for P, θ_go and CMA.
- **Masks.** A beard mask down-weights everything below the lip line. Collar and hair masks remove the neck base and angle. Fit shading only above the jaw terminator, never the cast shadow.
- **Pose disentanglement.** Fit neck pitch jointly. Compute CMA and f_sm only after un-posing to canonical pose. A flexed frame must not update f_sm without a flexion corrective.
- **Identifiability.**
  - A frontal photo alone leaves P, θ_go and CMA unidentified. Return the prior and show the uncertainty.
  - One good profile should pin P to about 1–2 mm and θ_go and CMA to about 2–4°. 🧠
  - 3/4 views pin the border path and the silhouette width.
- **Warping.**
  - Tag feature curves: mandibular border, posterior ramus, chin-plane edges, submental line and SCM borders.
  - Stiffen the bone-anchored chin pad and border; let the submental and neck regions deform more.
  - Skin the neck as a separate articulated part.
  - FLAME-type models have separate neck and jaw joints. Face-only models and the MediaPipe mesh stop at the jaw. 🧠
- **Sex is a covariate, not a switch.** Use overlapping means and a user override. Never hard-code larynx or chin squareness by sex.

## Disagreements, myths & open questions

- **Ideal CMA.** Popular sources say 90°, Ellenbogen–Karlin 105–120°, and measured means run higher. Definitions differ (tangent lines or points; neck line through the thyroid or along the SCM), and posture dominates. ↔️
- **Gonial angle.** Its dimorphism is weak, and the age effect is confounded by tooth loss. ↔️
- **E-line, Z-angle, zero meridian.** These are clinical ideals from mostly white samples, and the E-line moves with nose size. They are not population means. ↔️
- **"Mewing" reshapes the adult jaw.** There is no good evidence of adult skeletal change. Tongue-up posture and neck extension do tighten the submental line at the moment of capture, which makes them a photo confound. 🧠
- **Myths.**
  - "A strong jawline means big masseters." It means bone plus leanness, and a big masseter can blunt the border.
  - "An Adam's apple means male." The dimorphism is in lamina angle and size, and individuals overlap.
  - "A double chin is fat." It has five possible causes (see Key facts).
- **Naming traps.** "Cervicomental" and "mentocervical" name different angles in different papers. Soft gnathion is sometimes put at the throat junction, which is properly C. Anthropometric gn is cephalometric menton (see craniometric-landmarks).
- **Open questions.**
  - 3D statistical neck and submental models by BMI, age and ancestry.
  - Inferring hyoid position from the surface.
  - Reliability of CMA measured from photos.
  - Norms for submental fat and chin width.
