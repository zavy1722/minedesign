# Muscles of the mid face

> The mid-face elevators fan out from bone to the upper lip and mouth corner. From medial to lateral they are levator labii superioris alaeque nasi (LLSAN), levator labii superioris (LLS), zygomaticus minor (ZMn) and zygomaticus major (ZMj), with levator anguli oris (LAO) and buccinator deep and risorius variable. Their dermal insertions along the cheek fat's medial edge make the nasolabial fold (NLF), a fixed hinge line present at every age that smiling deepens but does not create. ZMj drives the smile (AU12). The other elevators set lip, tooth and gum show, buccinator and risorius pull the corner back or sideways, and nasalis moves only the nostril. Attachments vary between people and imaging cannot resolve them in vivo, so a fitter should encode them as lines of action and expression priors, not identity geometry; most numbers here await verification.
Evidence status: 15 ✅ / 33 📄 / 44 🧠 / 26 ↔️ — 🧠 items are queued for web verification.

## Key facts

**Zygomaticus major (ZMj)**
- Arises from the lateral zygomatic bone near the zygomaticotemporal suture and inserts at the modiolus, blending with LAO and orbicularis oris (OO) [4][20] ✅. Some sources split the insertion above and below the commissure ↔️.
- Runs inferomedially and forward at about 45–65° from horizontal in frontal view (dimensions in Table 4) 🧠. Freilinger layer 2: deep to the nasolabial and medial-cheek fat, superficial to the buccal fat pad, LAO and buccinator, with the facial artery and vein deep to it 🧠.
- Zygomatic and buccal branches of CN VII supply both zygomatici [9] 📄.
- Principal smile muscle (AU12) [23][24][25] ✅. Device-free smiles drew about 40 % more ZMj activity than smiles made holding a Smile Stick or chopsticks (orbicularis oculi about 15 % more) [23] 📄. In 33 adults (2026), maximum sEMG-based contraction in smiling was 9.8 ± 3.1 % with force 0.93 ± 0.48 N [24] 📄; whether "contraction" means strain or %MVC is unclear ↔️. ZMj crosstalk dominates high-density facial EMG [25] 📄.
- A bifid or double ZMj appears in about 34 % of specimens, per a 2025 review citing a 1998 paper [10][12] 📄. These are not independent; the primary paper [12] reports 17 of 50 cadaver hemifaces (34 %) (confirmed in verification). The review proposes five morphological types [10] 📄. An inferior slip with a dermal attachment is the explanation the 1998 paper suggests for cheek dimples, an interpretation of cadaver findings, not a demonstrated mechanism [12] 📄 ↔️ (confirmed in verification).
- A 2025 review argues that ZMj's two layers age differently and links weakening to bone resorption (Wolff's law), without new data [27] 📄 ↔️.

**Zygomaticus minor (ZMn)**
- Arises from the zygomatic bone superomedial to ZMj, just behind the zygomaticomaxillary suture 🧠, and inserts into the upper lip lateral to LLS [3][5] ✅. Its insertion pattern is poorly described [3] 📄: angle of the mouth or upper-lip muscles [3][5] ↔️.
- The most superficial elevator (Freilinger layer 1), often fused with orbicularis oculi or LLS; FACS AU11, the nasolabial deepener 🧠.
- Often missing: in 50 hemifaces the commonest pattern (44 %) was LLSAN + LLS + ZMj [29] 📄, implying no separate ZMn there (inference from the pattern name). No absence rate was found ↔️.

**Levator labii superioris (LLS)**
- From the infraorbital margin above the foramen, deep to orbicularis oculi, to the upper lip between LLSAN and ZMn. It raises and slightly everts the lip and deepens the upper-middle NLF (AU10). The infraorbital bundle emerges between LLS and the deeper LAO 🧠.
- HA injected at the cutaneous insertions of LLS and the zygomatici was followed for a mean of about 4 months, without controls [28] 📄. A facial-exercise device thickened LLS and ZMj on ultrasound, with mixed results [40] 📄.

**Levator labii superioris alaeque nasi (LLSAN)**
- Origin: frontal process of the maxilla [6] or medial orbital wall [9] ↔️; the frontal process is standard 🧠. Inserts into the ala, pulling it superolaterally, and into the upper lip [6][7] ✅, with two bellies merging with LLS [7] 📄.
- Lifts the lip and flares the nostril; mimetic, quiet in breathing, strongly active in complex expressions [7][8] ✅. With AU10 it makes the AU9 sneer 🧠. Its connections to depressor supercilii and inferior orbicularis oculi [8] 📄 may explain why AU9 also lowers the medial brow 🧠.
- Zygomatic and superior buccal branches, facial and infraorbital arteries [7] 📄 (attribution unconfirmed). Surgeons release it for alar-base retraction and infraorbital dimples [6][7] 📄, so it tethers skin.

**Levator anguli oris (LAO, caninus)**
- From the canine fossa below the infraorbital foramen to the modiolus [38][13] ✅. It lifts the corners with ZMj; one-sided paralysis makes the smile asymmetric [38] 📄. The deepest elevator (layer 4), with a near-vertical pull that bares the canine (AU13) 🧠.

**Risorius**
- Pulls the commissure laterally in a closed-lip grin and is absent in some people [36][37] 📄 (both StatPearls, so not independent). Variable origin from parotid or masseteric fascia, platysma or SMAS. AU20, with platysma, is a fear or stretch expression, not a smile 🧠.

**Nasalis**
- Flares the nostrils and makes "bunny lines" in a snarl; not a smile muscle [36][39] ✅. The transverse part (compressor) joins the procerus aponeurosis; the alar part (dilator) runs from above the lateral incisor to the alar cartilage; AU39/38 🧠.

**Buccinator**
- From the maxillary and mandibular alveolar processes and the pterygomandibular raphe; anterior fibres reach the modiolus and blend with OO [20][21] ✅. Insertion: all fibres into OO [34] or anterior fibres ending at the modiolus [20] ↔️. A 2026 study adds an attachment at the pterygoid hamulus [22] 📄.
- Central fibres cross at the modiolus. The parotid duct pierces it opposite the upper second molar, and it lies deep to the buccal fat pad and masseter's anterior border. It holds the cheek to the teeth, makes the AU14 dimpler and forms the deepest plane of the lower cheek 🧠.

**Modiolus**
- Fibromuscular convergence at the mouth angle [13][14] ✅. Its core is OO, buccinator, ZMj, LAO and DAO, with OO and ZMj fibres interweaving [13] 📄. Discrete structure [13][14] or a cluster of convergence sites (fetal histology [15]) ↔️. MRI and CT of body donors cannot separate the atlas muscles [16] 📄. It sits about 10–15 mm lateral to cheilion and descends with age; see `anatomy/muscles-lower-face.md` 🧠.

**Nasolabial fold**
- A 3D curvature at the medial edge of the malar fat, made by the cutaneous insertions of the upper-lip elevators and ZMj [17] 📄 (56 specimens from 34 embalmed Korean adults; year 2024 per harvest, ≈ 2020 per PMC ID ↔️).
- Dominant muscles: LLS group + ZMn [18] or ZMj + the elevators [17] ↔️. The fold differs from nasolabial wrinkles and muscle ridges [19] 📄.
- Present from infancy: a thin, tethered lip side under a thick, mobile cheek-fat overhang 🧠. Working split: upper paranasal third (LLSAN/LLS, AU9/10), middle (ZMn, AU11), lower toward the modiolus (ZMj/LAO, AU12/13) 🧠.

**Smile**
- ZMj pulls the corner up, out and back around the dental arch; LLS, ZMn and LLSAN lift the lip; LAO adds vertical pull; buccinator and risorius widen. In a Duchenne smile AU6 (orbicularis oculi pars orbitalis) lifts the cheek and narrows the eye, and cheek fat bunches against the NLF 🧠.
- Rubin (1974): commissure (ZMj) smiles about two-thirds, canine smiles about one-third, full-denture about 2 % 🧠 ↔️.
- The Duchenne marker also appears in posed smiles, so it is a contested sign of genuine emotion [35][25] ↔️.
- Models: a finite-element face driven by ZMj [30], a face-head-neck model with 26 FACS-driven muscle pairs [31], EMG-informed reconstruction [32], facemotion.js [33] 📄.

## Applying this to photo→3D face sculpting in Blender

1. **Capture an expression set** with the identity cameras and light (≥ 1.5 m, ≈ 85 mm equivalent): relaxed neutral, closed smile (AU12), full smile (AU6+12+25), lip raise (AU10), sneer (AU9), lip stretch (AU20). Expression frames locate the fixed lines (NLF, dimples) but never set neutral volume 🧠.
2. **Gate the neutral.** A smile shows as corners above the stomion line, a wider mouth, a deeper and more curved NLF, a fuller "apple", a shorter upper lip and narrowed eyes [23][25]. Such a frame cannot set cheek fullness 🧠.
3. **Trace the NLF** per view as a polyline (alar-crease end, middle, commissure end) on the camera-matched images, then project it to the mesh. A real fold stays put across views and deepens in the smile; a shadow edge moves with the light 🧠.
4. **Sculpt an overhang, not a groove:** a convex cheek mass over a flat lip plane, with the crease at the bottom of the overhang [17] 🧠. Take rest depth from age (`anatomy/fat-compartments.md`) and keep the upper-lip skin tight to the teeth.
5. **Use muscles as guides, not form.** Place skull-parented empties at the ZMj origin, canine fossa and infraorbital margin and a modiolus empty vertex-parented to the lip, then join each pair with a hooked curve. Each expression key should move its point along that line 🧠.
6. **Topology:** concentric oral loops ending in a pole at each modiolus, 2–3 parallel loops along the NLF so the crease sharpens in shape keys, a loop around the ala, and a flow from modiolus to zygoma along ZMj 🧠.
7. **Shape keys.** Sculpt AU6/9/10/11/12/13/14/20 per side on the base mesh, splitting L/R with a vertex group that blends over 10–15 mm at the philtrum. The smile key moves the corner around the dental arch (backward, not just up). It also lifts and thins the upper lip, pushes cheek mass against the fold, swings the fold's lower end outward and widens the ala slightly. Multires detail does not follow shape keys (version-sensitive) 🧠.
8. **Measure** commissure excursion and angle per side (Table 3), upper-lip shortening, incisor and gum display, alar width at rest and in smile, and the dimple site. The surgical dimple convention, where the lateral-canthus vertical meets the commissure horizontal, is not anatomy 🧠 ↔️.
9. **Failures:** cheeks from a smiling photo; a V-carved NLF; a "Joker" smile that moves only the corners; corners floating off the teeth; smiles mirrored although one-sided weakness is real [38]; dynamic dimples in neutral; lighting-deepened buccal hollows; undisclosed filler or gummy-smile toxin [28], so ask.

## Algorithmic implications

- **Landmarks.** Reuse `alare`, `cheilion`, `zygion`, `pupil` (`face3d/synthetic.py`). Add skull-anchored `zmj_origin_L/R` (≈ 5–20 mm anterior, 0–10 mm inferior to zygion, σ ≈ 6 mm), `lao_origin_L/R` and `infraorbital_foramen_L/R`. Add derived `modiolus_L/R` (12 ± 4 mm lateral, −3 ± 5 mm vertical from cheilion, as in the lower-face note) and the curve `nlf_L/R` 🧠.
- **NLF as a curve.** Fit the traced polylines to a mesh edge loop by image-space chamfer distance, not point correspondence; weight by contrast and light angle 🧠.
- **NLF priors (mm, adults; widen if ancestry unknown)** 🧠: upper end 2–6 lateral and 0–5 above the alar crease point. Lower end 11 ± 4 lateral and 0 ± 6 vertical from cheilion; it often ends higher in youth and runs toward the marionette line with age. Length 30–50; rest depth half-normal, scale 0.5–1 under 30 and 2–4 over 55.
- **Expression basis.** Per-muscle linear actuators (Waters 1987 type): skull origin o, insertion i, angular zone Ω, radial falloff starting at 0.5–0.7 of |o−i|. ZMj defaults: Ω ≈ 35°, peak commissure displacement 10–15 mm. Direction priors (head frame: +x subject-left, +y up, +z forward; angular σ 15–25°) 🧠:

| AU | Moved point | Unit direction (left side) |
|---|---|---|
| 12 | commissure | (0.70, 0.55, −0.45) |
| 13 | commissure | (0.35, 0.90, −0.25) |
| 10 | upper lip above canine | (0.15, 0.95, 0.25) |
| 11 | upper lip, lateral third | (0.45, 0.85, 0.05) |
| 9 | alar base, paranasal skin | (0.20, 0.95, 0.10) |
| 14 | commissure | (0.40, 0.10, −0.90) |
| 20 | commissure | (0.90, −0.20, −0.35) |

- **Neutral gate.** Score frames on commissure angle to the stomion line, mouth width against its multi-frame minimum, upper-lip height, NLF contrast and cheek height, plus MediaPipe-style blendshape scores (mouthSmile, cheekSquint, noseSneer, mouthUpperUp, mouthDimple, mouthStretch; version-sensitive; the 52-score count is confirmed [41] 📄, individual names unconfirmed). Fit identity to a robust multi-frame median and down-weight frames > 2σ off 🧠.
- **Identity vs expression.** Half-normal prior (σ ≈ 0.05) on AU weights in neutral frames; orthogonalize the midface identity basis against the AU9–14 subspace so identity cannot absorb a slight smile 🧠.
- **Variation switches** that never alter neutral geometry: `zmj_bifid` (p ≈ 0.3 ↔️ [10][12]), on only if smile frames show a dynamic dimple; `zmn_present` (p 0.5–0.9 ↔️ [29]) scales AU11; `risorius_present` affects AU20 only.
- **Warp stiffness (relative, to tune)** 🧠: upper lip 0.3 (as in the lower-face note), cheek fat 0.5, zygomatic body 1.5–2.0. On the NLF loop, anchor position to the traced curve but cut bending weight to 0.2–0.3 so a crease forms.
- **Asymmetry.** Fit sides independently with a soft symmetry prior on neutral NLF endpoints (σ ≈ 1.5 mm); never symmetrize smile vectors [38].

## Disagreements, myths & open questions

- **Contested anatomy:** LLSAN origin [6][9]; ZMn insertion [3][5] and presence [29]; NLF muscles [17][18]; modiolus structure or cluster [13][14][15]; buccinator insertion [20][34]; the Duchenne marker [25][35]; ageing muscles lengthening and slackening or shortening and stiffening (Le Louarn) [27] ↔️.
- **Cheek dimples:** bifid ZMj [12] is the usual explanation, buccinator or other dermal slips the alternatives 🧠 ↔️.
- **Myths** 🧠: "risorius is the smile muscle" (it gives a fearful lateral stretch, AU20; the smile is ZMj [23][36]); "the NLF is an ageing wrinkle" (a fat-boundary fold present from infancy [17][19]); "the Duchenne cheek lift is ZMj" (mostly AU6, though strong AU12 also raises the cheek); "frowning takes 43 muscles, smiling 17" (no basis).
- **Harvest notes:** the year of [17] is unconfirmed and [13] has none; [37][38] are StatPearls like [9][21][36]; [5] keeps its ":443" URL; titles of [34][35] were not captured; [27] matches PubMed 41413726 in the lower-face note. Refuted so far: none.
- **Open:** ZMn and risorius absence by ancestry; in-vivo thickness norms; quantitative NLF geometry by age, sex and ancestry (none sourced); what "contraction %" means in [24]; full texts of [10][17][24]; code and licences for [31][32].
