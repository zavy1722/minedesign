# Forehead, brow ridge, temple and hairline

> The forehead is the frontal squama under a thin cover (≈4–6 mm at mid-forehead, ≈5 mm at glabella), so its true-profile silhouette (slope, curvature, glabellar projection, the male supraglabellar break) is close to bone and is the most trustworthy depth signal in the upper face. The temple is the opposite: a thick, variable stack of fascia, fat and temporalis, framed by the temporal line, lateral orbital rim and zygomatic arch, whose fullness tracks age, body fat and clenching more than bone. The brow ridge is two structures, a medial superciliary arch and a lateral supraorbital trigone, and is among the most sexually dimorphic parts of the skull; the eyebrow hair rides on it but is not it. Hairline and eyebrows are hair features that move with recession, grooming and expression, so they belong in hair/texture layers and must never set scale or bone shape. Sourced facts come from sibling-topic harvests; most numeric norms are expert knowledge awaiting verification.

## Key facts

**Frontal bone and brow ridge**
- Palpable frontal landmarks: supraorbital margins, frontal eminences, superciliary arches [3] 📄. The supraorbital notch/foramen is ≈2 cm from the midline and carries the supraorbital nerve and vessels [1][2] ✅; other series place it 2–3 cm out, roughly above the medial limbus ↔️ 🧠.
- Glabella is the most prominent midline forehead point above the nasal root, at about the level of the supraorbital ridges [4][5] ✅. Bony g and soft g′ differ; g′ is extremal, so define it in Frankfort horizontal (FH) 🧠.
- The modern brow ridge is split: a **superciliary arch** (medial, over the frontal sinus, converging on glabella) and a flat lateral **supraorbital trigone** ending at the zygomatic process, separated by a shallow oblique groove 🧠. Male brow mass is medial; the lateral third is a flat plane turning down to the frontozygomatic (FZ) suture 🧠.
- The frontal sinus sits behind glabella and the medial arches. Its size correlates only loosely with ridge projection, and it is absent in a few percent of people (population-dependent) ↔️ 🧠.
- **Frontal eminences**: paired low bosses ≈2.5–3.5 cm above the supraorbital margin, roughly above the mid-orbits; strongest in children and women, flatter in men and with age 🧠.
- **Sex pattern** 🧠: male = projecting glabella and medial arches, rounded thick upper orbital margin, sloping forehead, and a supraglabellar concavity ("frontal break") between brow mass and upper forehead. Female = near-vertical forehead, eminences, sharp thin margin, one continuous convex profile. Forensic ordinal scores for these traits (Buikstra & Ubelaker; Walker) overlap between sexes 🧠.
- The FZ suture is palpable on the lateral orbital rim ≈1 cm above the lateral canthus 🧠.

**Temporal lines and temple**
- Temporal lines start at the zygomatic process of the frontal bone and arc up and back 🧠. The inferior line bounds the temporal fossa superiorly; the fossa is also bounded by the squamosal suture, lateral orbital rim and zygomatic arch [13] 📄. A temple-filler guide places the hollow below the superior temporal line, where temporalis originates [17] 📄; the lines converge anteriorly, so the sources agree 🧠.
- **Frontotemporale (ft)** is the most medial point of each temporal crest; ft–ft is minimal frontal breadth 🧠. The crest is the forehead "corner" where the front plane turns into the side plane 🧠.
- Temple layers (superficial → deep): skin, subcutaneous fat, temporoparietal fascia (galea/SMAS continuation, carrying the superficial temporal vessels and VII's frontal branch), loose areolar plane, deep temporal fascia splitting around the superficial temporal fat pad, temporalis, deep temporal (buccal-extension) fat, bone 🧠. Temporalis lies in a fascial envelope with deep fat between muscle and deep fascia [17] 📄. See `anatomy/smas-ligaments.md` for the temporal adhesion and septa.
- Temporal fossa volume is reportedly constant across adult ages and sexes; temple variation is mainly temporalis and fat (secondhand citation) [13] 📄.
- Temporalis thickness is protocol-sensitive ↔️: ultrasound reference values disagree between cohorts (Table 1) [15]; MRI in 360 Japanese adults aged 35–84 spans 3.69–16.90 mm, men thicker except over 70, rising with weight and BMI [16] 📄. Ultrasound measures 1 cm above and 1 cm lateral to the lateral canthus; women tended lower [14] 📄.
- Clenching bulges the anterior temple 🧠; lean, older and post-craniotomy temples hollow, exposing the temporal crest and the zygoma's frontal process 🧠.

**Forehead soft tissue and muscles**
- The cover is thin: glabella ≈5.1 mm (CBCT) [8] 📄, in line with the pooled ≈5.5 mm in `anatomy/skull-osteology.md`. Nasion is 6.78 mm (CBCT) [8] and 5.57 → 8.25 mm from lowest to highest BMI group (MRI) [10] ✅. Forehead cover is thicker in males at all ages and most regions (MRI) [9]; one compiled review reports men thicker at all sites in one study [8] ✅. Skin alone is ≈1.1–1.6 mm, including supraorbital (high-frequency ultrasound) [11] 📄.
- Depth tables are population means; individual depth is never exactly known [12] 📄.
- Layers: skin, subcutaneous fat in central and middle forehead compartments (Rohrich & Pessa), frontalis wrapped by galea, a loose areolar glide plane, periosteum; under the lateral brow, retro-orbicularis oculi fat (ROOF) pads the brow 🧠.
- **Frontalis** arises from the galea (anterior vs posterior disputed), has no bony attachment, and its insertion is reported variously as brow skin, the orbicularis oculi border, or galea [18][19][20] ↔️. It raises the brow and makes transverse forehead lines; the temporal (frontal) branch of VII supplies it [18][20] ✅.
- The two frontalis bellies usually leave a midline gap and end laterally near the temporal fusion line. Lateral to that the brow has no elevator, so the lateral brow descends first with age 🧠 (Knize).
- In a 3D skin-displacement study of an Asian cohort, 53% moved only the lower forehead during frontalis contraction, unlike an earlier study [21] 📄 ↔️.
- **Depressors**: corrugator draws the brow inferomedially and makes vertical glabellar furrows [23][24] ✅; procerus pulls the medial brow down and makes horizontal nasal-root lines [27][28] ✅; depressor supercilii inserts ≈13–14 mm above the medial canthal tendon [29] 📄. All three, plus frontalis, share temporal-branch innervation [25][26] ✅. Orbital orbicularis spreads over frontalis and temporalis [40] 📄.
- Forehead wrinkling tracks upper-lid ptosis via compensatory frontalis activation [31] 📄, so a "neutral" ptotic sitter may show raised brows 🧠.
- FACS: AU1 inner brow raiser (medial frontalis), AU4 brow lowerer (corrugator group) [37] 📄; AU2 outer brow raiser (lateral frontalis) 🧠.

**Eyebrow hair vs brow ridge**
- Male brow hair usually sits on the rim and runs flat; the female brow commonly sits above the rim laterally with an arch 🧠. Brows are plucked, tattooed and microbladed, so brow hair is not anatomy 🧠.
- Naive observers detect brow asymmetry from ≈3 mm [32] 📄; glabellar frowning was asymmetric in 62% of one small 3D study [30] 📄.

**Hairline**
- Trichion (tr) is the midline point of the anterior hairline [6][7] ✅; it moves with recession and must never set scale 🧠.
- Forehead height showed the largest inter-ethnic variation of 11 neoclassical measures (2,359 adults, 27 groups) [33] 📄, and the "equal thirds" canon failed in a Saudi caliper sample [34] 📄.
- Front-view anatomy: midline (± widow's peak), frontal arc, frontotemporal recesses (apices roughly above the lateral canthus), temporal peaks, sideburns 🧠. A 0.5–1 cm transition zone of fine single hairs makes a hairline look real; a hard edge looks fake 🧠.
- After adolescence most men's frontotemporal corners recede ≈1–2 cm to a "mature" hairline without balding (≈Norwood II) 🧠. Scales: Hamilton → Norwood I–VII plus type A (men); BASP (both sexes); Ludwig and Sinclair grades and Olsen's frontal accentuation (women) 🧠. Frontal fibrosing alopecia recedes the frontal hairline and thins eyebrows; traction alopecia thins the temples 🧠.
- Androgenetic alopecia prevalence rises roughly with age decade in white men (often quoted ≈30% by 30, ≈50% by 50) and is lower in East Asian men ↔️ 🧠.

## Applying this to photo→3D face sculpting in Blender

1. **Capture.** Hair pulled back (band or thin cap) so trichion, temples and temporal crests show; no glasses or fringe; brows slack; jaw relaxed, teeth apart, so temporalis does not bulge. True profiles are mandatory: the forehead silhouette is the best upper-face depth data 🧠. Shoot from ≥1.5 m at 85–135 mm full-frame equivalent; arm's-length selfies narrow the temples and bulge the forehead 🧠.
2. **Fix pitch before measuring slope.** Inclination is pitch-confounded 1:1 🧠. Level the profile reference to FH (tragion to the lower-lid/cheek junction) before tracing anything 🧠.
3. **Trace the profile.** Draw a curve on the profile silhouette from trichion through g′ and n′. Measure with the built-in Measure tool: nasofrontal angle, upper-forehead tangent vs vertical, g′ ahead of n′, and the supraglabellar dip (male) 🧠. Match the sculpt silhouette within ~1 mm; the cover is only ~5 mm [8] 🧠.
4. **Sculpt order** 🧠: (a) block the forehead as a box (front plane, two side planes split by the temporal crests) with Clay Strips/Flatten; (b) run the temporal crest from the FZ suture up and back, crisp only as far as the photo shows; (c) brow mass: medial superciliary arch (Layer/Clay), flat lateral trigone, glabella-to-nasion transition; (d) frontal eminences (stronger in women and children); (e) temple last, from photo shading, not bone.
5. **Sex coherence** 🧠: a male brow without a supraglabellar break reads female or juvenile; a female forehead without eminences and with a heavy medial arch reads male. Do not mix types.
6. **Brows and hairline are hair.** Model the scalp as skull plus ~5–7 mm 🧠, and put the hairline and eyebrows in hair curves or textures. Blender's curves-based hair object and its node-group hair assets (3.3/3.5 era) suit hairline density gradients 🧠 (time-sensitive: check the current release).
7. **Light-check** 🧠 with one raking light (~45° up, 60–80° to the side): check the supraglabellar break, eminences, temporal crest, temple concavity and brow shadow against the photo. Overhead light makes brows look heavier than they are.
8. **Asymmetry.** Sculpt symmetric, then break it: one brow 1–3 mm higher is common, and observers see ≥3 mm [32] 🧠.
9. **What goes wrong** 🧠: slope from a pitched reference; brow ridge built from brow-hair shadow; a young temple sculpted hollow (or the reverse); raised-brow forehead lines baked into the neutral; hairline geometry in the mesh; a fringe-hidden hairline guessed; an over-smoothed temporal crest; MediaPipe point 10 used as trichion.

## Algorithmic implications

- **Parameters** (on top of the bone/skin basis of `anatomy/skull-osteology.md`) 🧠: `forehead_inclination_deg` (FH frame), `frontal_curvature` (subtense/chord), `glabella_proj_mm` (g′ ahead of n′ along FH depth), `supraglabellar_break_mm` (0–3), `superciliary_proj_mm`, `trigone_flatness`, `eminence_amp_mm` and spacing, `temporal_crest_sharpness`, `ft_ratio` (ft–ft/zy–zy), `temple_fullness_mm` (signed offset from the surface spanning temporal crest, lateral orbital rim and arch), a brow-hair curve (head, peak, tail relative to the rim) and a hairline spline (tr height, frontotemporal recess depth and angle, temporal peak, widow's-peak amplitude, Norwood stage as a categorical prior).
- **Suggested priors** 🧠 (verify before shipping): inclination μ ≈12° M / ≈8° F, σ 5°; nasofrontal angle μ 125°, σ 7°; `glabella_proj_mm` μ ≈5, σ 2.5; `supraglabellar_break_mm` μ 1.5 M / 0.3 F, σ 1; `ft_ratio` μ 0.81, σ 0.03; `temple_fullness_mm` μ 0 (young adult), σ 3, shifted negative after ~50 or at low BMI (≈−1 mm/decade placeholder); forehead height μ from `anthropometric-norms`, σ 7 and low weight, because Farkas and 3D-stereophotogrammetry norms differ considerably [35] 📄 (3DFN summary data are openly downloadable [36] 📄). Depth: log-normal, glabella μ 5.3 mm σ_log 0.15; mid-forehead μ 5, σ_log 0.2; supraorbital μ 6.2, σ_log 0.2 (μ lowered from 7.5 after verification [44][45][46]); mid-temple μ 15, σ_log 0.35.
- **Constraints** 🧠 (hinge losses, FH frame): z(g′) ≥ z(n′) + 1 mm; g′ is the max-z midline point between n′ and tr; midline curvature above g′ ≥ 0, except a male concavity ≤ 3 mm in a band ≈10–25 mm above g′; soft supraorbital rim ahead of the corneal apex; left/right eminences and crests within ~2 mm (`sigma_sym` ≈1.5 mm upper face, matching the skull note).
- **Observation weights** 🧠: profile forehead silhouette high (σ ≈1 mm); frontal temple silhouette only where a parsing mask says skin, not hair; brow landmarks σ 3–5 mm for bone, used fully only for the brow-hair curve; hairline excluded from geometry and scale; MediaPipe forehead points σ ≥ 5 mm in-plane, depth ignored.
- **Pose coupling** 🧠: optimise pitch jointly with slope and add pitch σ (≈2–3°) to the reported inclination uncertainty.
- **Occlusion** 🧠: use hair/hat parsing masks with Tukey or Huber losses; treat a fringe-covered upper forehead as unobserved and fall back on the prior.
- **Expression gate** 🧠: if brow blendshapes (browInnerUp, browOuterUp, browDown) exceed ~0.2–0.3, drop that frame's brow and forehead residuals.
- **Feature lines** 🧠: tag temporal crest, supraorbital rim, supraglabellar crease and glabella on the template, and lower cotan-Laplacian weights across them so warps keep these plane breaks.

## Disagreements, myths & open questions

- **Equal thirds** ↔️: the canon failed in Saudi adults [34] and forehead height is the most ethnically variable measure [33]; use it as a sketch guide, not a constraint.
- **Frontalis attachments and motion** ↔️: origin and insertion disputed [18][19][20]; lower-forehead-only motion in 53% [21]; forehead ultrasound layers disputed [22].
- **Temporalis thickness** ↔️: ultrasound reference values disagree by ~30% [15]; MRI values span 3.7–16.9 mm [16]. Do not transfer values across modalities.
- **Brow ageing** ↔️ 🧠: "brows descend" vs photographic series (Lambros) showing brows often stay level or rise medially; lateral descent is better supported.
- **Ideal brow peak** ↔️ 🧠: above the lateral limbus (Westmore) vs further lateral (Gunter & Antrobus); fashion-driven, not anatomy.
- **Brow ridge = sinus?** ↔️ 🧠: ridge projection and sinus size correlate weakly; spatial, biomechanical and social-signalling (Godinho et al.) explanations compete.
- **Myths** 🧠: "the eyebrow is the brow ridge"; "the forehead is one plane"; "widow's peak is a simple dominant trait" (McDonald); "any recession is balding".
- **Open:** no dense forehead/temple depth map in this library; forehead-inclination norms by population and definition; a trichion definition for thinning hair; inferring a hairline hidden by a fringe; temple-fullness norms by age and BMI.
