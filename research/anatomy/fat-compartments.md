# Facial fat compartments

> Facial fat is not one continuous blanket: septa and retaining ligaments divide it into superficial compartments above the SMAS and deep compartments beneath the mimetic muscles, the model Rohrich and Pessa's 2007 cadaver dye study made standard. Compartment borders show as soft shading transitions in a young face and as grooves in an older one (tear trough, lid–cheek junction, midcheek groove, nasolabial fold, prejowl sulcus), because neighbouring compartments gain, lose and slide independently. With age the deep midface, periorbital and temporal fat deflates while the lower superficial compartments hold their volume or descend, and body-weight change acts mostly on superficial and lower-face fat. A photo→3D fitter should therefore model the soft tissue as region-wise thickness fields with soft borders, crease seams and ligament anchors, driven by latent adiposity, age and expression factors. ## Key facts

**Layers and retaining system**
- Five layers over most of the face (Mendelson's model) 🧠:
  1. skin;
  2. subcutaneous fat in superficial compartments, crossed by retinacula cutis;
  3. the SMAS and mimetic muscles;
  4. a loose areolar plane holding glide spaces, deep fat and the ligaments that pass through it;
  5. deep fascia or periosteum.
- Retaining ligaments pass from bone through layer 4, fan into the septa between compartments and insert in the dermis. They are 🧠:
  - the orbicularis retaining ligament (ORL), which continues medially as the tear-trough ligament;
  - the zygomatic ligaments (McGregor's patch);
  - the masseteric ligaments;
  - the mandibular ligament;
  - the platysma–auricular ligament;
  - the superior and inferior temporal septa.
- Glide spaces are not compartments. The prezygomatic, premaxillary, premasseter and buccal spaces and the deep pyriform (Ristow's) space are fat-poor planes bounded by ligaments 🧠.
- Septa carry perforating vessels, so each compartment ages and swells semi-independently 🧠; they are leaky partial barriers, not sealed walls ↔️.

**Superficial compartments (Rohrich & Pessa 2007 and follow-ups)**
- **Forehead:** a central forehead compartment and paired middle forehead compartments 📄 [1].
- **Lateral temporal–cheek:** a long strip running from the temple, down in front of the ear, to the neck, bounded by the temporal septa 📄 [1].
- **Periorbital:** superior, inferior and lateral orbital compartments, ringed by the ORL. The inferior orbital compartment is a thin layer under the lid skin, not the postseptal "eye-bag" fat 📄 [1].
- **Midface, medial to lateral** 📄 [1]:
  - nasolabial, which sits lateral to the fold and overhangs the lip;
  - medial cheek;
  - middle cheek, in front of the parotid near zygomaticus major;
  - lateral temporal–cheek.
- The medial–middle septum and the zygomatic ligaments converge below the malar eminence 🧠.
- **Jowl:** its front border is depressor anguli oris (DAO) and the mandibular-ligament region, and its lower border is the mandibular septum (Reece, Pessa & Rohrich 2008). Submandibular and submental fat lie beyond it 🧠.
- The classic **malar fat pad** (Owsley) is a triangle with its base on the nasolabial fold and its apex over the malar eminence. It spans the nasolabial, medial-cheek and part of the middle-cheek compartments: one surgical name for several compartments 🧠.

**Deep compartments**
- **ROOF** (retro-orbicularis oculi fat) lies under the orbital orbicularis beneath the lateral brow. It gives sub-brow fullness, and when it deflates or descends the lateral lid hoods 🧠.
- **SOOF** (suborbicularis oculi fat) lies under the orbicularis, below the ORL, on the zygoma. Rohrich et al. 2009 describe a medial part (medial limbus to lateral canthus) and a lateral part (lateral canthus to the temporal fat). It supports the lid–cheek junction. The two-part SOOF description 📄 [3]; the lid–cheek support role 🧠. Whether ROOF and SOOF form one continuous layer is debated ↔️.
- **Deep medial cheek fat (DMCF)** lies on the maxilla beside the pyriform aperture, deep to the nasolabial and medial-cheek compartments (Rohrich, Pessa & Ristow 2008). It provides anterior cheek projection. Losing it produces the tear trough and V-deformity, a flat ogee and a deeper upper fold 🧠. DMCF may have medial and lateral parts, and a separate deep lateral cheek compartment may exist; both are disputed ↔️.
- **Buccal fat pad (Bichat)** lies between buccinator and masseter. It has a body and buccal, pterygoid, pterygopalatine and temporal extensions (Stuzin 1990; Zhang 2002, three lobes). The buccal extension decides round cheek versus submalar hollow. It is proportionally largest in infants (the "sucking pad") 🧠.
- **Temple** fat sits in three layers 🧠:
  - subcutaneous fat;
  - the superficial (intermediate) temporal fat pad, between the layers of the deep temporal fascia;
  - the deep temporal pad (the buccal pad's temporal extension), lying on temporalis.
- **Postseptal orbital fat is a separate system** 🧠:
  - Upper lid: medial and central (preaponeurotic) pads.
  - Lower lid: medial, central and lateral pads. The inferior oblique separates medial from central, and the arcuate expansion separates central from lateral.
  - Protruding pads make bags; atrophy makes a hollow upper sulcus.
  - Prostaglandin-analogue eye drops can shrink the fat on one side 🧠.

**Surface expression**
- **Ogee curve:** in 3/4 view a young midface reads as one S: concave at the lid–cheek transition, convex over the cheek mass (SOOF, DMCF and superficial fat), then gently concave below (Little 2000). Aging breaks it into segments, the lid bag and cheek forming Hamra's "double convexity" 🧠.
- **Grooves sit on boundaries** 🧠:
  - the tear trough on the tear-trough ligament;
  - the palpebromalar groove on the lateral ORL;
  - the midcheek groove on the zygomatic-cutaneous ligament line and the medial–middle septum.
  - Together they form a Y.
- The nasolabial fold is nasolabial fat bulging over a fat-poor, muscle-tethered lip. It is present from infancy; age changes its depth and overhang 🧠.
- The marionette line and the prejowl sulcus mark the DAO/mandibular-ligament border in front of the jowl 🧠.
- Malar mounds and festoons fill the prezygomatic space between the ORL and the zygomatic ligament. They are fluid-filled and vary from day to day 🧠.
- Smiling (AU6+AU12) pushes the nasolabial and medial-cheek fat up and medially, bunches the malar soft tissue and narrows the eye, while the deep fat glides underneath. A smiling photo is never neutral volume 🧠.

**Age, weight, sex, ancestry**
- **Age:** the deep compartments (DMCF, SOOF, temporal, ROOF, preaponeurotic) deflate. The superficial compartments shift down, and the lower ones keep or gain volume. The "inverted triangle of youth" becomes a heavier lower third (CT evidence: Gierloff 2012 [2], whose abstract reports inferior migration of the midfacial compartments and deep medial cheek fat loss). How much each mechanism contributes is contested ↔️.
- **Bone:** resorption removes deep-fat support and deepens grooves (Shaw & Kahn; Mendelson & Wong). It works through maxillary retrusion, widening of the pyriform aperture and orbit, and mandibular remodelling 🧠.
- **Weight gain** goes first to the submental, jowl, buccal/lower cheek, nasolabial and temple regions. The forehead, nasal dorsum and bony rims change least. Lower and mid-face width is the main cue for perceived adiposity 🧠.
- **Rapid large weight loss**, including on GLP-1 agonists ("Ozempic face"), deflates temple, cheek and periorbital fat and leaves lax skin; evidence is mostly case reports and reviews, and time-sensitive ↔️.
- **Sex:** women average more subcutaneous fat at the lateral cheek, so men are not thicker everywhere 🧠.
- **Ancestry:** in many East Asian upper lids a low septal fusion lets preaponeurotic fat lie in front of the tarsus (full lid, low or absent crease); compartment data by ancestry are sparse 🧠.

## Applying this to photo→3D face sculpting in Blender

1. **Session hygiene.** 🧠
   - One session, truly neutral face (no AU6/AU12), upright head, level gaze; malar oedema varies day to day.
   - A chin-down pose fakes jowls and a double chin.
   - CT/MRI are supine (cheek and jowl fall back and up): never copy supine volumes onto an upright likeness.
2. **Lighting set.** A top-light frame shows the depth of the tear trough and nasolabial fold. A 3/4 frame with a side key shows the ogee and the submalar hollow. A flat frame shows only the silhouette 🧠.
3. **Read the photos.** 🧠
   - Frontal: temple concavity, cheek width at the malar level versus the mouth level, jowl breaks in the jaw line, submental fill.
   - 3/4: lid–cheek junction height relative to the lash line and rim, the midcheek groove, where the ogee breaks.
   - Profile: cheek projection relative to the cornea; a "negative vector" (Jelks) predicts a tear trough. Also the jowl below the mandibular line and the cervicomental angle.
   - Malar highlight: usually in the upper-outer quadrant formed by the alar-base→tragus line and the lateral-canthus→commissure line (Hinderer).
4. **Layers in millimetres.** 🧠
   - Build the bone-led base, then one vertex group per Table 1 region.
   - Give each region shape keys, or a Displace modifier (Normal, vertex group, strength = mm × 0.001 at Metric scale 1.0), driven by custom properties in mm.
   - Blur the weights 5–15 mm, but keep them sharp along crease seams.
   - Put volume on the low Multires levels and creases on the high ones.
5. **Sculpt by compartment.** Use Face Sets per region and build with Inflate, Clay Buildup or a masked Mesh Filter → Inflate. Never Smooth across seams, because the transitions carry likeness 🧠.
6. **Folds are overhangs, not trenches.** 🧠
   - Build the cheek side up over a flat lip.
   - Shape the tear trough as a slope off the rim, deepest medially and fading by the mid-pupil.
   - Drape the jowl over the mandibular-ligament anchor, with the prejowl sulcus in front.
7. **Check.** Matcap plus a rotating key light; overlay the 3/4 silhouette on the camera-solved photo and compare contour curvature, not only landmarks 🧠.
8. **What goes wrong:** one "malar blob"; eye bags sculpted as cheek fat; symmetric tear troughs on a young face; a carved fold; volume from a smiling photo; temples filled on a lean or older subject; global smoothing; perspective-widened cheeks read as fat 🧠.

## Algorithmic implications

- **Representation.**
  - Soft tissue = template + Σ_r w_r(v)·δ_r·n(v), where w_r are the blurred atlas weights and δ_r is the per-region normal offset in mm.
  - An optional per-region tangential descent t_r is clamped at the ligament anchors.
  - Crease seams are template polylines with depth d_s ≥ 0 and width w_s 🧠.
- **Latent drivers.** δ_r = μ_r(sex, age) + a_r·z_adip + b_r·z_age + ε_r, with z ~ N(0,1) and ε_r ~ N(0, σ_r²). Heuristic loadings in mm per SD, all untested 🧠:

  | Region | a_r (adiposity) | b_r (older) | σ_r (mm) |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | submental | +4 | +1 | 4–8 |
  | jowl | +2.5 | +1, plus inferior t_r ≈ 2–4 mm | 3–4 |
  | buccal_pad | +2.5 | −0.5 ↔️ | 3–5 |
  | nasolabial | +1.5 | +0.5, plus inferior t_r | 2–3 |
  | medial / middle cheek | +1.5 | −0.5 | 2.5–3 |
  | dmcf | +1 | −1.5 | 2.5 |
  | soof | +1 | −1 | 2 |
  | temporal | +1.5 | −1.5 | 2–3 |
  | roof / upper sulcus | +0.5 | −1 | 1.5 |
  | periorbital superficial | +0.5 | −0.5 | 1–1.5 |
  | forehead, dorsum, arch, anchors | ≈ 0 | ≈ 0 | 0.75–1 |

  Set the means of z from BMI and age when the user gives them. Tune the loadings on scan data before shipping.
- **Regularisation.** 🧠
  - Bending weight 1.0 inside a region, ≈ 0.1–0.2 across a seam (allowing a slope break) and ≈ 0.5 across a soft border.
  - Ligament anchors (McGregor's patch, the ORL line, the masseteric line, the mandibular and platysma–auricular ligaments): ARAP stiffness ×2–5, σ ≤ 1 mm.
- **Crease priors.** Seam depth d_s ~ HalfNormal, scale ≈ 0.5–1 mm under 30 and ≈ 2–4 mm over 55. The nasolabial profile is asymmetric (convex cheek side, flat lip side). The tear trough is deepest medially 🧠.
- **Observability.** A frontal view alone cannot separate DMCF, SOOF and superficial projection. Keep them at the prior unless a 3/4 or profile view, or a reliable normal estimate, is available. The buccal and jowl regions are constrained by the frontal silhouette, the submental region by the profile 🧠.
- **Expression gating.** If AU6 or AU12 exceeds a low threshold, drop the midface, lower-lid and nasolabial residuals, or first fit an expression shape carrying the superomedial fat shift of ≈ 2–6 mm 🧠.
- **Pose.** Tag each source as upright or supine and shift supine cheek and jowl data before mixing. Flag head pitch below about −10° for the jowl and submental regions 🧠.
- **Defaults.** 🧠
  - The Table 1 atlas with an 8 mm border blur.
  - Seams: tear trough, palpebromalar groove, midcheek groove, nasolabial fold, marionette line/prejowl sulcus, mandibular border, temporal septum.
  - Allow left–right asymmetry with σ_asym ≈ 0.5·σ_r.

## Disagreements, myths & open questions

- **Deflation vs descent.** Lambros, Rohrich/Pessa and Donofrio stress volume loss. Gosain's 2005 MRI volumetry and the classic lift literature stress descent and redistribution. The current view is region-specific: both happen, along with ligament laxity and bone loss ↔️.
- **Facial recurve concept** (Le Louarn): rising resting muscle tone compresses the deep fat. This is a minority view ↔️.
- **Naming.** Pessa/Rohrich compartments, Mendelson's spaces, Gierloff's CT maps and Schenck (2018) and Cotofana (2019) on imaging overlap but do not match one-to-one ↔️.
- **Buccal fat and body weight.** Older texts call buccal volume roughly constant across body habitus; clinical experience says it tracks adiposity. Cosmetic buccal-fat removal (a time-sensitive trend) may hollow the cheek prematurely ↔️.
- **Myths** 🧠: "the malar fat pad is one structure"; "eye bags are descended cheek fat"; "young faces have no nasolabial fold".
- **Open questions** 🧠:
  - There are no upright, in-vivo per-compartment volume norms by age, sex, BMI and ancestry.
  - Dissection samples are small, old and mostly of European ancestry.
  - Photos alone cannot split a groove into its fat, bone and skin contributions.
  - The facial effects of GLP-1 drugs are still being quantified.
