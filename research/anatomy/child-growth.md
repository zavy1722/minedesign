# Craniofacial growth: child and adolescent faces

> A child's head is not a scaled-down adult head. The brain case follows the fast "neural" curve and reaches ~90–95 % of adult size by age 6–7. The face follows the slower "somatic" curve, growing down and forward from under the cranium into the late teens (girls) or early twenties (boys). The younger the subject, the bigger the cranium relative to the face, the lower the eye line, the larger the eyes relative to the face, and the smaller and more upturned the nose, over a short lower face and a soft chin. A child-likeness pipeline needs age-conditioned priors, a scale anchor that stops growing early (the cornea), a short capture window and hard safeguards for minors.

## Key facts

This topic had no harvest or draft of its own. 📄 items cite URLs from sibling harvests in this library; none is independently verified yet.

**Growth pattern**
- **Two clocks.** The neurocranium follows Scammon's neural curve (~90–95 % of adult size by 6–7 y). The face follows the somatic S-curve: fast in infancy, slow in childhood, then a pubertal spurt 🧠.
- **Gradient and order.** Parts nearer the brain mature first (forehead and orbits, then midface, then nose, then mandible). Width completes first, then depth, then height (Proffit), so lower-face height and the male mandible grow into the late teens or early 20s 🧠.
- **Direction.** Displacement plus remodelling (Enlow) carries the face down and forward, out from under the cranium. Slow change continues in adults, more in men (Behrents) 🧠.

**Cranium**
- Neurocranium-to-face volume is roughly 7–8 : 1 at birth and ~2–2.5 : 1 in adults; the figures depend on definitions ↔️ 🧠.
- The posterior fontanelle closes at ~1–3 months, the metopic suture fuses at ~3–9 months, and the anterior fontanelle closes at ~12–24 months 🧠.
- The forehead is tall, vertical and often bossed, with no brow ridge. The frontal sinus appears on radiographs at ~6–8 y and grows through adolescence, especially in boys, building the brow ridge 🧠.
- Infant deformational plagiocephaly (a parallelogram head, same-side ear forward) is real asymmetry 🧠. In a normal cohort to 6 y, facial midline offsets averaged only ~0.25 mm, with no normal/abnormal threshold [7] 📄.

**Eyes**
- The globe is ~70–75 % of adult axial length at birth (~17 mm), grows fastest over the first 2–3 years and is near adult size by ~10–13 y 🧠. Adult endpoint: ~24.2 × 23.7 mm, with no sex or age-group difference [12] 📄.
- The cornea is adult-sized by ~2–3 y 🧠. The adult pooled mean is 11.84 mm (SD 0.79), proposed as a photo scale [13] 📄. Infant eyes look big only relative to the eye opening and the face 🧠.
- Short, relatively tall fissures make the eyes rounder. Intercanthal width is large relative to the face and the bridge low, so children look wide-set. Epicanthal folds are common in infants of all ancestries and recede as the bridge rises 🧠.
- The eye line sits below the head's midpoint in infants (~0.60–0.70 of vertex–menton from the top) and rises to ~0.48–0.53 in adults 🧠 ↔️.

**Nose**
- The infant nose is short, low-bridged and round-tipped, with a short columella, and upturned so the nostrils show from the front 🧠.
- Tip projection is the least mature facial dimension through childhood and jumps at puberty, when any dorsal hump appears. Nasal height matures at ~13–15 y (F) and ~15–18 y (M) 🧠.
- Nose-from-skull prediction is age-dependent: juvenile-to-adult regressions improved with sex and age [11] 📄.

**Jaw, teeth, muscle**
- The gonial angle is ~160° at birth and ~120° in adults [4] 📄. Other texts give ~140–150° at birth ↔️.
- The symphysis fuses in the first year. The mental protuberance develops mostly in adolescence, more in boys, so children's chins are soft and rounded with a shallow mentolabial sulcus 🧠.
- Lower-face height steps up with eruption: primary dentition complete ~2–3 y, permanent incisors ~6–8 y, second molars ~11–13 y. Permanent incisors arrive at adult size, so they look oversized at 7–10 y, often with a midline gap 🧠.
- Children's profiles are convex because the mandible lags; they straighten as it catches up, more in boys. Björk: forward rotators get short faces and strong chins, backward rotators long faces and weak chins 🧠.
- Masticatory muscle volume rises ~199 % from 0 to 17 y, the masseter most, with a left-side bias [8] 📄. The temporalis shows no sex effect at 5–15 y [9] 📄. Jaw-angle bulk is therefore adolescent 🧠.

**Soft tissue**
- Full infant cheeks are a relatively large buccal fat pad plus subcutaneous fat (peak late in year 1, minimum ~5–6 y): fat, not bone 🧠.
- Lips are thick and protrusive (thickness peaks in adolescence), and more upper incisor shows at rest than in adults 🧠.
- Facial soft tissue is thicker in obese 6–18-year-olds [10] 📄. Adult depth tables do not transfer to children 🧠.

**Puberty and sex**
- Before puberty, sex differences are small (boys a few % larger) 🧠. Divergence follows peak height velocity (PHV) at ~11–12 y in girls and ~13–14 y in boys, ±~2 y between individuals 🧠. Whether the facial spurt matches PHV or lags it is debated ↔️.
- Males then gain nose projection, brow ridge, lower-face length, chin, jaw angle and masseter 🧠. Ears are already longer in boys in Saudi children [6] 📄 and reach mature length by ~14 in a Chinese sample [5] 📄.

**Data resources**
- Farkas norms: direct anthropometry, 2,326 North American White adults and children [3] 📄; Kolar criticised their protocols (relayed by [1]) 📄.
- 3D Facial Norms (3DFN): 2,454 people aged 3–40 (952 M, 1,502 F), cross-sectional stereophotogrammetry, with sex-specific growth curves and a MATLAB toolbox. Summary data are open; individual data need a FaceBase request [1][2] 📄. It differs considerably from Farkas (method, secular trend, ethnic mix) [3] 📄.
- Most 3D morphable models (3DMMs) are adult-trained (FLAME; the Basel model from ~200 mostly young adults). The Large Scale Facial Model (LSFM) reportedly includes children and age-specific models 🧠 (verify the age range and licence).

## Applying this to photo→3D face sculpting in Blender

1. **Record age (years and months), sex, dental stage and pubertal stage.** Every prior below keys off them 🧠.
2. **Capture in one session within the Table 4 span.** Shoot at ≥ 1–1.5 m with a fast shutter (≥ 1/250 s) or simultaneous cameras. Phone shots at arm's length exaggerate the forehead and nose 🧠.
3. **Scale from the iris, not adult IPD.** From ~2–3 y, set corneal width to ~11.5–12 mm [13]; for infants use ~10–11.3 mm (Table 2). Cross-check IPD and ear length against age. An adult IPD of 63 mm inflates a child's head by 10–40 % 🧠.
4. **Start from an age-matched base,** not a shrunken adult. With only an adult mesh, scale it to the target head circumference, then Lattice-shrink the face relative to the cranium: lower-face height most, then nose projection, then width. Keep the eyeballs as rigid spheres sized from Table 2 🧠.
5. **Check proportions:** eye-line fraction, facial index (Table 3), iris-to-fissure ratio, intercanthal width, shallow nasion 🧠.
6. **Sculpt the age signature:** a smooth, bossed forehead without brow ridge; a short upturned nose showing columella and nostrils; a short upper lip, full lips, sharp Cupid's bow; a small round chin and soft jaw border; cheek volume low and forward (fat) 🧠.
7. **Teeth.** Match the dental stage. From 6–10 y, upper central incisors are adult width (~8.5 mm, a secondary scale check), and gaps alter lip support. Prefer closed-mouth neutral frames 🧠.
8. **Keep infant cranial asymmetry and ear shift;** do not symmetrise them [7] 🧠.
9. **What goes wrong:** the adultified child (long face, defined chin, big nose from adult 3DMMs); wrong scale from adult IPD; cheek fat sculpted as bone; photo sets mixed across months; anime eyes; a male brow ridge before puberty; adult soft-tissue depths on a child skull 🧠.

## Algorithmic implications

- **Age is a required input** (`age_months`, `sex`, optional `dental_stage`, `tanner_stage`). Never infer minor status from photos 🧠.
- **Age-conditioned priors.** μ_m(a, s) per inter-landmark distance from the 3DFN curves [1][2], else Table 1 × adult norms. σ_m² = (CV·μ_m)² + (v_m·σ_a)², with CV ≈ 0.05 adults and 0.06–0.08 children, v_m the growth-curve slope, σ_a = 0 with a known birthdate (else ≥ 0.5 y) 🧠.
- **Metric scale.** Corneal diameter prior N(11.8, 0.5–0.8²) mm from ≥ 3 y [13]; age-interpolated N(10.0–11.3, 0.6²) mm under 2 y. IPD is a weak check (σ ≈ 3 mm) 🧠.
- **Rigid eyes.** Eyeball radius from Table 2 with σ ≈ 0.5 mm; eyes are excluded from template warps 🧠.
- **Growth-gradient warp.** Soft region weights (cranium, orbit, midface width, nose, lower face, mandible) with per-axis scales (`template_warp_defaults` in the data file), blended about a sella or porion origin, then fit. For a one-parameter init, use cardioidal strain R′ = R(1 + k(1 − cos θ)), θ from the vertical about a head-centre origin, k < 0 reading younger (perceptual, not anatomical) 🧠.
- **Regularise toward the age-matched mean,** not the adult mean, which adultifies the face. With an adult-only 3DMM, add an age direction bounded by the declared age 🧠.
- **Soft priors (Table 3).** Eye-line fraction σ 0.03; facial index σ 0.04; gonial angle σ ≈ 6°; nasolabial angle σ ≈ 10°. Add a dorsal-hump penalty under ~11 y, a brow-projection penalty before puberty and a low chin-projection mean 🧠.
- **Sex weighting.** w_sex(a) = logistic((a − a_PHV) / 1.5 y), with a_PHV ≈ 11.5 (F) or 13.5 (M), multiplying the sex-specific shape terms 🧠.
- **Symmetry.** Infants: σ_sym 3–5 mm for the ears and occiput, 1–2 mm for midface points [7] 🧠.
- **Identity loss.** Down-weight face-recognition embeddings for children: they are trained mostly on adults, and NIST demographic tests reported elevated errors for children 🧠.
- **Capture check.** Read EXIF dates; warn beyond the Table 4 span, and refuse beyond twice the span for infants 🧠.
- **Minor safeguards (design requirement).** Record guardian consent plus child assent; process locally by default; limit photo retention; embed provenance metadata; irreversibly block sexualised or nude pipelines for minor-flagged projects. GDPR treats identifying face geometry as special-category data (Art. 9; Art. 8 child-consent ages 13–16). The 2025 US COPPA amendments (under 13) reportedly add biometric identifiers. Sexualised synthetic depictions of minors are illegal in many jurisdictions 🧠 (legal, time-sensitive: verify).

## Disagreements, myths & open questions

- **Cranium:face ratio** ↔️: 7–8 : 1 or lower at birth, 2–2.5 : 1 in adults; volume and projected-area definitions get mixed 🧠.
- **Gonial angle at birth** ↔️: 160° [4] against ~140–150° in other texts.
- **Spurt timing** ↔️: is mandibular peak velocity synchronous with stature PHV, or 6–12 months later? 🧠
- **Ear growth** ↔️: mature by ~14 [5], against "shape fixed by 4 months, stable to ~70" (a secondary claim in [14]) and slow adult lengthening (see `anatomy/ear.md`).
- **Norms** ↔️: Farkas and 3DFN differ considerably [3], and the Farkas protocols were criticised [1]. Both are mainly North American White, and secular trends are unquantified 🧠.
- **Myths:** "eyes are born full size" (only the cornea nearly is); "children are small adults"; "growth ends at 18" (men continue into their early 20s); "nose and ears never stop growing" (slow adult lengthening, partly soft tissue; magnitude debated) 🧠.
- **Open:** ethnically diverse longitudinal 3D growth curves; child soft-tissue depths from upright imaging; age range and licences of child-capable 3DMMs; landmark reliability in moving infants; bone versus soft-tissue share of pubertal change.
