# Eye, eyelids and orbit

> The eye region is a near-constant optical and bony frame dressed in highly variable soft tissue: a ~24 mm globe with a ~11.8 mm cornea, in an orbital aperture of roughly 40 × 35 mm, its corneal apex on average ~13–19 mm ahead of the lateral orbital rim depending on ancestry. Because adult globes and corneas vary little, the visible iris is the best metric ruler on the face, and "big", "small", "deep-set" or "prominent" eyes come from fissure height, lid position, crease and fold, epicanthus and protrusion, almost never from globe size. Every lid parameter (fissure length and height, canthal tilt, MRD1/MRD2, lid-peak position, crease height, fold overhang) shifts with gaze, expression, head pitch and population, so read it in a pose-solved head frame from a neutral, forward-gaze photo. A fitter should hold globe geometry on tight priors, place the globe with population-conditioned protrusion priors, and spend its freedom on lid contours, folds and brows. No eye-region harvest or earlier draft existed: sourced facts come from sibling-topic harvests in `research/_raw/harvests`, and most lid and orbit norms are expert knowledge awaiting verification.

## Key facts

**Globe, cornea, iris**
- Emmetropic adult globes on CT measure ~24.2 transverse × 23.7 vertical × 22.0–24.8 mm axial, with no significant sex or age difference; transverse range 21–27 mm; refractive error mainly changes axial length [1] 📄.
- The cornea is horizontally oval (~11–12 × 9–11 mm); pooled mean diameter 11.84 mm, SD 0.79 [2] 📄. The limbus is ~1–2 mm wide [3] 📄.
- Corneal radii: anterior ~7.8 mm, posterior ~6.4–6.5 mm [4][5][6][7] ✅. Steeper than the ~12 mm scleral sphere, the cornea bulges ~2.5–2.8 mm ahead of the limbal plane and ~1–1.5 mm proud of the continued scleral sphere (derived) 🧠. The bulge shows through a closed or downcast lid and drags the lid-margin peak sideways with gaze 🧠.
- The iris is near-planar, ~3 mm behind the corneal apex; the visible "iris diameter" is effectively the white-to-white corneal diameter 🧠. Shading and refraction: `blender/eyes-modeling-blender.md`.

**Orbit and globe position**
- The rim is frontal, zygomatic and maxillary bone; the infraorbital rim is maxilla medially, zygoma laterally [22][23] ✅. The infraorbital foramen sits ~6 mm (5–8.5) below the rim [23][24] ✅; the supraorbital notch ~2 cm from the midline [40][41] ✅.
- Medial walls are near-parallel; lateral walls diverge at ~90° to each other, so the orbital axes point ~20–25° outward 🧠 (Whitnall). The aperture faces forward and outward: the lateral rim is the most recessed rim, the superior rim and brow the most projecting 🧠.
- Hertel exophthalmometry (lateral rim → corneal apex) averages ~15–17 mm in white adults, more in Black adults (means ~17.5–18.5 mm), less in East Asians (~14.4 mm) (Table B) [42][43][44][45] ✅.
- The globe sits ~1–2 mm closer to the orbital roof and lateral wall than the orbital centre (Stephan & Davidson 2008, 4 cadavers, mean age 83; a 9-cadaver follow-up found only 2 of 13 near-central) [54][55] ✅. The forensic rule that a mid-supraorbital to mid-infraorbital tangent touches the cornea holds only to within a few mm, with the sign disputed ↔️.
- "Negative vector" (cornea ahead of the infraorbital rim and cheek in profile) reads as prominent eyes with inferior scleral show and ages into a deep tear trough 🧠 (Jelks & Jelks).

**Palpebral fissure and canthi**
- en and ex are the inner and outer soft-tissue commissures of the fissure [27][28] ✅. Corneal diameter and fissure length have been proposed as photo scales [2] 📄.
- The upper lid covers the top ~1–2 mm of the cornea; the lower lid rests at the inferior limbus ±1 mm 🧠. Superior scleral show means retraction, proptosis or AU5; inferior show >1–2 mm means a negative vector, laxity or proptosis 🧠.
- The upper-lid peak sits just nasal to the pupil centre and the lower-lid low point just temporal to it, giving the asymmetric almond; a lateral peak reads as lateral flare 🧠.
- Canthal tilt: ex ~2–4 mm above en [52][53] ✅ (corrected in verification). Measure it against the head frame (Frankfort horizontal, orbitale–porion [25][26] ✅), never the image: 2° of head roll is 2° of tilt 🧠.
- The lateral canthal tendon inserts on Whitnall's tubercle ~3–4 mm inside the lateral rim, ~1 cm below the frontozygomatic suture; the medial tendon straddles the lacrimal sac 🧠. At ex the upper lid overlaps the lower; at en a rounded lacrimal lake holds caruncle and plica 🧠.
- In depth, ex lies ~8–13 mm behind en 🧠 ↔️, derived from Farkas chords as √(ex–en² − ((ex–ex − en–en)/2)² − rise²), which is very sensitive to the inputs. Putting ex too far forward is the classic flat, frontal-eye error 🧠.

**Upper lid**
- Levator palpebrae superioris arises from the lesser sphenoid wing above the optic canal and inserts into the anterior superior tarsus and lid skin [8][9][10] ✅ (CN III, superior division [9] 📄). Its skin insertions form the crease 🧠.
- Orbicularis oculi: orbital part for forceful closure, palpebral part for reflex blinking [11][12] ✅. The palpebral part is preseptal, pretarsal and sometimes ciliary (Riolan) [11] 📄, running medial palpebral ligament → lateral raphe [11][13] ✅.
- Crease height is sex- and population-dependent (Table C; Western measured means ~7 mm, lower than the earlier 8–11 mm F figure ↔️ [56]); in East Asian lids it is often low, tapers nasally into an epicanthal fold, or is absent ("single eyelid") 🧠 ↔️. The usual explanation is a lower septum–aponeurosis fusion that lets preaponeurotic fat descend, plus fewer skin insertions ↔️ (Jeong et al. 1999).
- Crease ≠ fold: photos show the fold hanging over the crease. Tarsal platform show (TPS, margin→fold) and brow-fat span (BFS, fold→brow) describe the lid better than crease height; hooding drives TPS to zero, laterally first 🧠.
- Superior sulcus depth reflects the central and medial preaponeurotic fat pads, the lacrimal gland laterally and ROOF under the brow; a deep sulcus means lean, older or enophthalmic 🧠.
- The upper lid follows vertical gaze (lid–globe synergy), so a downward glance reads as hooding 🧠. Ptosis recruits frontalis and forehead lines [14] 📄, so a ptotic sitter may hold raised brows at "neutral" 🧠.

**Lower lid and tear trough**
- Lower orbital fat forms medial, central and lateral pads, with the inferior oblique between the first two 🧠. The lid–cheek junction descends with age (Table C) 🧠.
- The tear trough runs inferolaterally from ~3–6 mm below en along the medial infraorbital rim to about the mid-pupil line over the tear-trough ligament, then continues as the palpebromalar groove 🧠 (Wong, Hsieh & Mendelson 2012; `anatomy/smas-ligaments.md`). Infraorbital skin measures ~1.1–1.6 mm by ultrasound [34] 📄; lid skin is thinner (<1 mm) 🧠.
- Smiling (AU6, orbital orbicularis [39] 📄) pushes the cheek up and adds a 2–4 mm pretarsal roll 🧠: never take lower-lid identity from a smile.

**Epicanthus**
- A medial fold of upper-lid skin over the medial canthus; types supraciliaris, palpebralis, tarsalis (common East Asian form) and inversus 🧠. It is common in East Asian adults (prevalence estimates vary ↔️), in infants of all ancestries and in Down syndrome 🧠.
- It hides the caruncle and moves the *visible* en ~1–4 mm laterally, shortening the apparent fissure, inflating en–en and steepening apparent tilt 🧠. Landmark detectors label the visible corner.

**Brows**
- Male brow hair sits on the rim and runs flat; female brows sit above the rim laterally with an arch 🧠. Head above the medial canthus–ala line, peak between lateral limbus and lateral canthus (contested ↔️), tail on the ala–lateral-canthus line 🧠. Brow hair is groomed, so it is never bone evidence 🧠.
- Orbital orbicularis spreads over frontalis and temporalis [11] 📄; depressor supercilii inserts ~13–14 mm above the medial canthal tendon [33] 📄.

**Landmarks and perception**
- MediaPipe: 478 points = 468 mesh + 10 iris points at 468–477 [29][30] ✅, plus 52 blendshape scores in [0, 1] such as eyeBlinkRight [32] 📄. dlib-68 eyes are indices 36–41 and 42–47 [31] 📄: corners plus two points per lid, no iris, no crease 🧠.
- Eyelid position at rest is the most asymmetry-sensitive feature (~2 mm threshold) [15] 📄; brow asymmetry is seen from ~3 mm [16] 📄.

## Applying this to photo→3D face sculpting in Blender

1. **Capture.** Neutral face, glasses off, gaze on the lens, plus an upward-gaze frame (exposes en and caruncle), a downward frame (crease, fold, corneal bulge under the lid) and true profiles. Spectacles rescale the eyes ~1–1.5% per dioptre (minus lenses shrink, plus lenses enlarge); coloured contacts move the limbus; eyeliner and lash extensions fake lid thickness 🧠.
2. **Solve camera and head frame first** (`reconstruction/camera-pose-solving.md`); head pitch alone changes apparent tilt, MRD and lid show 🧠.
3. **Scale from the iris** only if both limbus edges are sharp: fit an ellipse, use the major axis (the vertical is lid-covered), expect ±4–7% (from the SD in [2]), and let a caliper en–en/ex–ex override 🧠.
4. **Place globes before any lid.** UV sphere r ≈ 12 mm plus a corneal cap (R 7.8, Ø 11.8) aimed along the photo gaze; pupils at the measured IPD; protrusion from the profile, checked against Table B for the sitter's ancestry. Never scale the globe to enlarge the eyes; open the fissure 🧠.
5. **Lids are shells over the globe.** Shrinkwrap (offset ~0.1–0.3 mm) or sculpt with eyes visible; a ~2 mm margin with a flat intermarginal strip, tear line and caruncle; margin wrapping the corneal bulge; peak nasal to the pupil; upper lid over the lower at ex; ex 8–13 mm deeper than en 🧠.
6. **Measure in iris units** (Table E): fissure L/H, tilt, mm of iris covered by each lid, peak offset, TPS and fold overhang (medial, central, lateral), epicanthus, brow head/peak/tail, tear-trough depth 🧠.
7. **Topology.** ≥3 concentric loops on the margin, one on the crease (so a "fold" shape key can drape skin over it), one along the rim / lid–cheek junction, one on the tear-trough line 🧠.
8. **Asymmetry layer.** Sculpt symmetric, then add a measured asymmetry shape key; lid differences ≥2 mm are visible [15] 📄.
9. **What goes wrong** 🧠: globe scaled with the fissure; zero-thickness lids ("doll eyes"); ex too far forward (frontal "fish" eye in ¾); the epicanthal corner taken for en; crease sculpted where the fold belongs; downcast or smiling frames used for lids; top light faking a tear trough or deep sulcus; wide-angle selfies shrinking eyes relative to the nose; brow hair read as brow bone.
10. **Light check** with one raking key ~45° up: lid shadow on the iris, fold shadow, sulcus and tear trough against the photo 🧠.

## Algorithmic implications

- **Globe (fixed unless strong evidence):** `globe_r` N(12.0, 0.5) mm, `cornea_d` N(11.8, 0.6) [2][46][47], `cornea_R` N(7.8, 0.27) [4][5][6][7], `iris_depth` N(3.0, 0.3); shared L/R, asymmetry σ ≈ 0.2 mm 🧠.
- **Globe position:** IPD N(63, 3.8) or sex-specific; `hertel_mm` by population: N(16, 2) white, N(18, 2) Black, N(14, 2) East Asian [42][43][44]; L–R difference σ 0.7 and vertical dystopia σ 1.0, flag either >2 mm; globe centre ~1–2 mm superolateral to the aperture centre (σ 1.5) on the bone layer [54][55] ✅.
- **Fissure and lids** as parametric margin curves on the globe+cornea offset surface: `fissure_len` N(30, 1.6), `fissure_h` N(9.5, 1.0), `tilt_deg` N(+5, 3) (East Asian N(+8, 3.5) ↔️), `MRD1` N(4.5, 0.8), `MRD2` N(5.25, 0.8), `upper_peak_dx` N(−1 nasal, 1), `lower_low_dx` N(+1 temporal, 1), `ex_depth_behind_en` N(10.5, 2.5) 🧠.
- **Crease/fold:** mixture {absent, low 3–6 mm, standard} with population weights; `crease_h` N(7.3, 1.5) F / N(6.9, 1.2) M (Western; corrected in verification [56] ↔️); `fold_overhang` 0–4 mm, rising with age and laterally; `epicanthus` ∈ [0, 1] shifting visible en up to ~4 mm and hiding the caruncle 🧠.
- **Hard constraints:** no lid–globe penetration; margin thickness 1.5–2.5 mm; margins meet only at the canthi; ex ≥3 mm inside the bony lateral rim 🧠.
- **Observations:** ex σ ≈ 0.7 mm; en σ ≈ 1.0 mm (2.5 with epicanthus or hidden caruncle); lid contours as point-to-curve residuals (σ ≈ 0.5 mm normal, free tangent); limbus edges σ ≈ 0.3 mm, horizontal extremes only; brow points σ 3–5 mm for bone, 1 mm for the hair curve 🧠. MediaPipe iris centres (468/473) drive gaze 🧠.
- **Gates:** per-frame gaze from the iris ellipse with lid–gaze coupling (upper margin follows ~0.8–1.0 of corneal vertical displacement, lower ~0.3 in downgaze), or drop frames with |gaze pitch| > 10–15°. Drop lid residuals when eyeBlink/eyeSquint/eyeWide exceed ~0.2–0.3, lower-lid and tear-trough residuals when cheekSquint does 🧠.
- **Scale fusion:** inverse-variance combination of iris (±4–7%), IPD (±6%) and any caliper value, which dominates 🧠.
- **Separate layers:** brow hair as a curve anchored to the supraorbital rim (lower-edge offset men N(0, 3), women N(+4, 3) mm); tear trough as a 0–3 mm groove on a bone-anchored path 🧠.

## Disagreements, myths & open questions

- **Exophthalmometry norms** ↔️ differ by ancestry, sex, age, device (Hertel, Luedde, CT) and examiner: use wide, population-conditioned priors.
- **Canthal tilt** ↔️ depends on reference line (FH, true horizontal, bipupillary) and en definition; "positive tilt / hunter eyes" is fashion, not anatomy 🧠.
- **East Asian upper lid** ↔️: septal fusion vs missing skin insertions; single-lid and epicanthus prevalence vary widely 🧠.
- **Intercanthal norms** ↔️ span 31–41 mm across samples [17][18][19]; a pooled review argues White-based standards must change [20] 📄.
- **Canons:** "eyes at half head height" vs a textbook line through the lid bottoms [37] 📄; "five eyes across" [38] 📄, yet the orbital canon fit only 30.6% of African American women [35] 📄 and intercanthal width was below nose width in most of 1,050 Turkish adults [36] 📄.
- **Globe placement**: superolateral is now sourced [54][55]; the sign of the corneal-tangent rule remains ↔️.
- **Ptosis thresholds** ↔️: MRD1 ≤2 mm, <2.5 mm, or >1–2 mm side difference.
- **Myths** 🧠: "big eyes = big eyeballs"; "eyes are full-size at birth" (the globe grows from ~16–17 to ~24 mm; only the cornea nears adult size by 2–3 y, `anatomy/child-growth.md`); "the crease is the fold"; "the tear trough is pigment".
- **Open:** (Hertel norms now sourced, see Verification log) this library has no source yet for canthal depth, crease height by population, tarsal size, Hertel norms or lash geometry.
