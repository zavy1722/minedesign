# Nose anatomy and morphology

> The external nose has three tiers: a bony upper third (paired nasal bones), a cartilaginous middle third (upper lateral cartilages fused to the dorsal septum) and a mobile lower third (alar cartilages, fibrofatty alae, columella). The skin over it is thinnest at rhinion (≈3 mm) and thick at nasion and over a sebaceous tip. Only the upper third follows the skull closely; tip, alae and columella are weakly predictable from bone, so the photos must supply them. Width reads in frontal views. Projection, rotation and dorsal profile read almost only in profile, ¾ and basal views, and camera distance, head pitch and lighting distort the nose more than any other feature. Surgical "ideal" angles and ratios (nasofrontal, nasolabial, nasofacial, Goode) are good measurement frames and warnings, not population priors.

## Key facts

**Skeleton and cartilage**
- The nasal bones form the bony bridge and meet the frontal bone at nasion, the midline junction of the frontonasal and internasal sutures [5][6][7] ✅. They are ≈21 mm long in Korean 3D-CT (n = 75) [15] 📄; 20–25 mm is a usable adult range 🧠.
- The upper lateral cartilages (ULCs) tuck several mm under the caudal nasal bones at the **keystone** (rhinion) and fuse with the dorsal septum into a T-shaped middle vault 🧠. A dorsal hump straddles rhinion: bone above, cartilage below 🧠.
- Each **lower lateral (alar) cartilage** has a medial, an intermediate (domal) and a lateral crus. The domes make the tip, and the medial crura plus the membranous septum make the columella. The lateral crus runs up and away from the alar rim, so the rim and alar lobule are fibrofatty with no cartilage 🧠. The ULC–LLC overlap (the **scroll**) can show as a supra-alar crease 🧠.
- The **septum** (quadrangular cartilage, perpendicular plate of the ethmoid, vomer, maxillary crest) props the dorsum. Its anterior septal angle supports the supratip, and the caudal septum sits behind the columella 🧠. Deviation is common and moves tip and columella 0.5–3 mm off the midline (`facial-asymmetry.md`) 🧠.
- The anterior nasal spine lies at the inferior midline margin of the piriform aperture [8] 📄 and supports the columella base 🧠.
- **The aperture does not predict the nose** ✅:
  - Piriform width grows proportionally more than nasal breadth (CBCT, n = 96) [9].
  - Only 26 of 76 nose–aperture relations were significant (Korean 3D-CT) [16].
  - Several profile prediction rules performed poorly [10], and nose shape correlates weakly with bone (RV < 0.4) [11].
  - Regressions improve when sex and age are added [17].

**Surface landmarks** (see `craniometric-landmarks.md`)
- **n′ (soft-tissue nasion):** the deepest point of the frontonasal concavity in cephalometry, or the skin point over bony n in anthropometry. The two can differ by several mm 🧠 ↔️.
- **prn (pronasale):** the most anterior, most protrusive midpoint of the tip [1][2] ✅.
- **sn (subnasale):** where the columella meets the upper lip. Sources construct it differently: a point on the nasolabial contour, the deepest point, or the lower columella border at the philtrum [1][2][3] ↔️. The philtrum runs from sn to labiale superius [37] 📄.
- **al (alare):** the most lateral point of the alar contour [2][4] ✅. Do not confuse it with ac (alar curvature point, the deepest point of the alar–facial groove) or sbal (subalare, where the alar base meets the lip) 🧠. Alar flare makes al–al exceed ac–ac by about 2–6 mm 🧠.
- **Tip features** 🧠:
  - paired **tip-defining points** (dome light reflexes), a few mm apart, merging into one highlight in bulbous or thick-skinned tips;
  - the **supratip break** just above them;
  - the **columella–lobule junction** (c′, the "double break") and the infratip lobule below.
- **Basal view** 🧠: an isosceles triangle, with the columella about ⅔ and the lobule about ⅓ of its height. Nostrils are teardrops, more vertical in narrow noses and rounder and more horizontal in wide ones.
- **Alar–columellar relationship in profile:** 2–4 mm of columella shows below the alar rim. A line along the nostril's long axis sits 1–2 mm from both the rim and the columella (Gunter) 🧠. A "hanging" columella (>4 mm) and a retracted ala are common individual traits, not errors.

**Soft tissue and muscles**
- **Cover depth.** Midline cover is ≈3.0 mm at rhinion and ≈6.0 mm at nasion in pooled data [12] 📄. Nasion depth rises with BMI, from 5.57 to 8.25 mm across BMI groups [13]; together with [12] this gives ≈6 mm, more with BMI ✅. Men are thicker at nasion and rhinion [38] and over the nose [14] ✅. Supratip and tip carry roughly 2–4 mm in thin skin and ≥4–6 mm in thick, sebaceous skin, which blurs the domes into a bulbous lobule 🧠.
- **Nasalis** flares the nostrils and makes vertical "bunny lines" in a snarl [24][25] ✅.
- **LLSAN** (levator labii superioris alaeque nasi) inserts into the ala and upper lip and pulls the ala up and out [26][27] ✅.
- **Procerus** arises at the junction of the lower nasal bone and ULC and makes horizontal nasal-root lines [28][29][30][31] ✅.
- **Depressor septi nasi** pulls the tip down and shortens the lip on smiling 🧠.
- **FACS:** AU9 (nose wrinkler), AU38 (nostril dilator), AU39 (nostril compressor) 🧠.

**Variation**
- **Sex.** Half-width (alare to midline) is 17.34 mm in men vs 15.39 mm in women (297 adults) [18] 📄. en–en/al–al is 0.89 for men and 1.01 for women in 3D data from southern Spain [19] 📄, so men's noses are wider than their intercanthal distance and women's about equal to it. Men also average a deeper radix, a more acute nasofrontal angle, a lower nasolabial angle and more humps; women more often show a supratip break and more tip rotation 🧠.
- **The orbito-nasal canon (al–al = en–en) fails in many people** ✅. Intercanthal width was shorter than nose width in most of 1,050 young adults [21], and the canon was not validated in Saudi students [22][23].
- **Population averages differ, with large overlap.** Malay 3D nose width is 39.6 mm (M) and 36.7 mm (F) [20] 📄 ↔️ (definition unconfirmed). Expert patterns 🧠 ↔️:
  - **East Asian:** lower radix and dorsum, less tip projection, shorter columella, wider base, thicker skin.
  - **African descent:** widest base (al–al/en–en ≈ 1.15–1.3, `facial-proportions-canons.md`), most alar flare, low dorsum, short columella, more acute nasolabial angle.
  - **European:** narrowest, with the highest radix and dorsum, thinner skin and more humps.
- **Heritability and climate.** Nose shape is strongly heritable (GWAS loci: Adhikari et al. 2016), and nostril width tracks climate (Zaidi et al. 2017) 🧠 ↔️. The adaptive explanation is debated.
- **Age** brings tip ptosis and lost rotation, a few mm of lengthening, columella retraction, a closing nasolabial angle and a set-back alar base (`aging-face.md`) 🧠. Children's noses are short, upturned and low-bridged, and projection matures at puberty (`child-growth.md`) 🧠.

**How the nose reads in photos**
- **Distance, not focal length, sets perspective** [34][35] ✅ (practitioner sources). The tip is the nearest facial point, so it is enlarged most. The reported selfie effect is a ≈30% wider nasal base at 12 in than at 5 ft (Ward et al. 2018) 🧠 ↔️, against ~10–12% from geometry alone (`reference-camera-setup.md`).
- **Pitch.** prn sits ~25–30 mm in front of n′ and the alar base, so pitch moves it about 0.5 mm per degree relative to them. A camera 10° low makes the nose look shorter and upturned and shows the nostrils 🧠.
- **Lighting.** Dorsal aesthetic lines (brow to tip-defining points), tip highlights, the supratip shadow and the alar-groove shadow are lighting cues, not edges 🧠. Detected prn drifts toward the specular highlight (`landmark-detection.md`) 🧠.
- **Head-pose pipelines lean on the nose tip.** It is one of six PnP points in Bengal-HP, which uses focal ≈ image width as an uncalibrated approximation [36] 📄.
- **dlib-68** puts the nose at indices 27–35 [32][33] ✅. 27–30 run down the bridge to the tip, 31–35 cross the nostril base, and 33 ≈ sn 🧠. Neither dlib nor MediaPipe marks anatomical al; their "alar" points sit near the alar base or crease 🧠 ↔️.

## Applying this to photo→3D face sculpting in Blender

1. **Shoot for the nose.** Take frontal, both true profiles, both ¾ views and one basal ("worm's-eye", chin up 30–45°) view. Use ≥1.5 m and 85–135 mm-equivalent, with the camera at nose height. Keep the face neutral and breathing quietly: no flare, and no smile, because depressor septi drops the tip. Remove glasses well beforehand, since pad marks dent the dorsum. Add one diffuse and one side-lit frame 🧠.
2. **Solve cameras before measuring** (`reference-camera-setup.md`). Never trace a nose from a <1 m photo in an orthographic view. With selfies only, fix distance from a metric cue (iris ≈11.7 mm) and expect al–al errors of ≥5–10% 🧠.
3. **Level to Frankfort** before reading rotation; nasolabial and nasofrontal angles are interior angles and survive roll, not pitch-induced foreshortening in ¾ views 🧠.
4. **Sculpt in order of observability** 🧠:
   1. radix height and depth, and the nasofrontal angle;
   2. the bony dorsum n′→rhinion, tight to the profile (with a skull layer, keep skin 1.5–4 mm over the nasal bones);
   3. the cartilaginous dorsum: hump or supratip break;
   4. tip projection, with a Goode check as a warning only;
   5. rotation, through the nasolabial angle and 2–4 mm of columellar show;
   6. alar crease position (ac 10–15 mm behind sn) and the alar rim arc;
   7. al–al and ac–ac in the frontal camera;
   8. the tip lobule: two domes, tip-defining point spacing, the infratip lobule;
   9. nostrils and columella from the basal view;
   10. real asymmetry: tip deviation and nostril differences.
5. **Measure in-scene.** Place empties at g′, n′, prn, c′, sn, ls, pg′, al and ac. A short script prints the angles and ratios from Table 2 for comparison with the same quantities clicked on the solved profile photo. Also overlay Workbench flat-black silhouettes on the profile photo.
6. **Topology.**
   - Alar-crease loops should continue into the nasolabial fold.
   - Nostrils need concentric loops around a real vestibule 8–12 mm deep, since basal and ¾ views see inside.
   - The columella needs ≥3 loops across it, and the domes need extra density.
   - Keep the alar rim a rolled, cartilage-free edge, not a crease.
7. **Lighting check.** Light a matcap or Eevee render from the photo's key direction and compare the dorsal lines, the tip-defining points and the supratip shadow. Frontal nose likeness is judged by these.
8. **What goes wrong** 🧠:
   - width inflated by selfies, or narrowed by idealisation or a European template;
   - projection too low from frontal-only 3DMM fits;
   - a single pointed tip instead of two domes under a skin envelope;
   - a shallow or too-high alar crease, or an alar base too far forward;
   - nostrils over-visible because the camera was low;
   - deviation symmetrised away and humps smoothed out;
   - nose taken from a smiling photo.

## Algorithmic implications

- **Nose parameter block** (defaults: young adult, population-agnostic means; widen σ when population is unknown) 🧠:

|---|---|---|---|
| n′ depth vs cornea plane (mm) | 13 / 12 | 2.5 | 6–20 |
| n–sn (mm) | 54.5 / 50.5 | 3.3 / 3.1 | ±4σ |
| n–prn (mm) | 50 / 45.5 | 3.5 | ±4σ |
| sn–prn (mm) | 20 / 18.5 | 2.0 | 12–28 |
| al–al (mm) | 34.9 / 31.4 (unknown population: 36 / 33, σ 4) | 2.1 / 2.0 | 26–50 |
| al–al − ac–ac (mm) | 4 | 1.5 | 0–9 |
| Nasolabial angle (°) | 100 / 105 | 9 | 70–135 |
| Nasofrontal angle (°) | 125 / 130 | 7 | 100–155 |
| Dorsal hump, signed (mm, + convex) | 1.0 / 0.0 | 1.5 | −4…+6 |
| Supratip break depth (mm) | 0.5 / 1.0 | 0.7 | 0–3 |
| Columellar show (mm) | 3 | 1.2 | 0–7 |
| Tip lateral deviation (mm) | 0 | 1.2 (Laplace) | ±5 |
| Nostril L/R asymmetry (mm) | 0 | 0.8 | ±3 |

- **One triangle, one prior.** Goode, Crumley and the nasofacial angle are one geometric relation. Encode the nasofacial angle (or Goode) once; stacking them triple-counts. Treat aesthetic ideals as UI warnings (weight ≤0.1 of the population prior), never as targets 🧠.
- **Observation model.** Use the library's landmark σ: prn 2.5%, sn 1.5% of image IOD (`landmark-detection.md`) 🧠.
  - Allow a prn bias of 1–2 mm toward the estimated specular highlight.
  - Map detector "alar" points to ac or sbal, not al. Take al from an edge term on the frontal alar contour.
- **Silhouette first.** In true profiles, fit the n′→prn→c′→sn midline contour with a chamfer term in % IOD, with tangent sliding. ¾ silhouettes constrain dorsal width and alar-groove depth. Use midline points for profile ratios: off-profile yaw shifts ac by x·sin ψ (Table 4) while barely shortening midline depth 🧠.
- **Depth–distance coupling.** In frontal-only data, projection is observable only through perspective, so camera distance and nasal projection trade off. Fix distance by multi-view or a metric cue, report projection σ, and double the projection prior σ's influence (rely on the prior) when no profile exists 🧠.
- **Hard constraints** (hinge penalties) 🧠:
  - skin ≥1.5 mm over the nasal bones (`soft-tissue-thickness.md`);
  - columellar show within 0–7 mm, with the columella below the alar rim;
  - prn in front of sn and ac, and ac 8–16 mm behind sn;
  - non-degenerate nostrils, with no rim self-intersection.
- **Symmetry.** Apply a soft bilateral prior, plus explicit midline-deviation parameters for the tip, columella and dorsum (C- or S-curve). Never snap prn to x = 0 🧠.
- **Template warping.** Give the template stable vertex IDs for n′, skin rhinion, supratip, the tip-defining points (L/R), prn, c′, sn, al, ac and sbal (L/R) and the nostril apex (L/R). Warp with ARAP or biharmonic handles, with high rigidity on the vestibule loops to stop nostril collapse 🧠.
- **Expression gating.** If AU12, AU9 or AU38 is detected, down-weight the nose observations from that photo. A smile drops the tip and widens the alae 🧠.
- **Evaluation.** Report per-feature errors (al–al, n–prn and sn–prn in mm; nasolabial and nasofrontal angles in degrees), not one RMS 🧠.

## Disagreements, myths & open questions

- **Ideal ≠ normal** ↔️. Rhinoplasty ideals come from small, mostly European, "attractive" samples. Healthy noses often fall outside them (Leong & White 2006 🧠), and canon studies fail broadly [19][21][22] ✅.
- **The projection ratios disagree because their baselines differ** ↔️. Goode measures from the alar crease (0.55–0.60) and Byrd from the alar–cheek junction (0.67).
- **Nasolabial angle norms** span 90–120° partly through definition: columella tangent vs sn–c′ line, lip tangent vs sn–ls 🧠 ↔️.
- **Subnasale and n′** are defined inconsistently [1][2][3] ↔️ 🧠.
- **Skull→nose rules are weak** ✅ [10][11][16]. The two-tangent and "3 × ANS" rules are contested (`forensic-reconstruction.md`) ↔️.
- **The size of the selfie effect** (≈30% clinical vs ~10–12% geometric) is unresolved ↔️.
- **Myths** 🧠:
  - "The nose grows throughout life": mostly soft-tissue droop and elongation, with debated magnitude ↔️.
  - "Nostrils should never show from the front": this depends on rotation and camera height.
  - "The brightest point is the tip."
- **Open:**
  - sourced population norms for nasal angles;
  - nose-specific error figures for 3DMM and regression methods;
  - verified MediaPipe alar indices;
  - nostril-shape norms;
  - basal-view datasets.
