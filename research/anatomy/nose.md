# Nose anatomy and morphology

> The external nose has three tiers: bony (paired nasal bones), cartilaginous (upper lateral cartilages fused to the dorsal septum) and mobile (alar cartilages, fibrofatty alae, columella), under skin thinnest at rhinion (≈3 mm) and thick at nasion and over a sebaceous tip. Only the upper third follows the skull; tip, alae and columella are weakly predictable from bone, so photos must supply them. Width reads in frontal views; projection, rotation and dorsal profile read almost only in profile, ¾ and basal views, and camera distance, pitch and lighting distort the nose more than any other feature. Surgical "ideal" angles and ratios (nasofrontal, nasolabial, nasofacial, Goode) are measurement frames and warnings, not population priors.

## Key facts

**Skeleton and cartilage**
- Nasal bones form the bony bridge and meet the frontal bone at nasion, the midline junction of the frontonasal and internasal sutures [5][6][7] ✅. They are ≈21 mm long in Korean 3D-CT (n = 75) [15] 📄; 20–25 mm is a usable adult range 🧠.
- Upper lateral cartilages (ULCs) tuck several mm under the nasal bones at the **keystone** (rhinion) and fuse with the dorsal septum into a T-shaped middle vault. A dorsal hump straddles rhinion: bone above, cartilage below 🧠.
- Each **lower lateral (alar) cartilage** has medial, intermediate (domal) and lateral crura. Domes make the tip; medial crura plus membranous septum make the columella. The lateral crus runs up and away from the alar rim, so the rim and alar lobule are fibrofatty, cartilage-free tissue 🧠. The ULC–LLC overlap (**scroll**) can show as a supra-alar crease 🧠.
- The **septum** (quadrangular cartilage, ethmoid perpendicular plate, vomer, maxillary crest) props the dorsum; its anterior angle supports the supratip 🧠. Deviation is common and moves tip and columella 0.5–3 mm off the midline (`facial-asymmetry.md`) 🧠.
- The anterior nasal spine lies at the inferior midline margin of the piriform aperture [8] 📄 and supports the columella base 🧠.
- **The aperture does not predict the nose** ✅: piriform width grows proportionally more than nasal breadth (CBCT, n = 96) [9]; only 26 of 76 nose–aperture relations were significant [16]; several profile rules performed poorly [10]; nose shape correlates weakly with bone (RV < 0.4) [11]; regressions improve with sex and age [17].

**Surface landmarks** (see `craniometric-landmarks.md`)
- **n′:** deepest point of the frontonasal concavity (cephalometry) or skin over bony n (anthropometry); they can differ by several mm 🧠 ↔️.
- **prn:** most anterior, protrusive midpoint of the tip [1][2] ✅.
- **sn:** columella meets upper lip, but constructions differ (nasolabial-contour point, deepest point, lower columella border at the philtrum) [1][2][3] ↔️. The philtrum runs from sn to labiale superius [37] 📄.
- **al:** most lateral point of the alar contour [2][4] ✅. Not ac (alar curvature point, deepest in the alar–facial groove) or sbal (alar base meets lip) 🧠; alar flare makes al–al exceed ac–ac by ~2–6 mm 🧠.
- **Tip** 🧠: paired **tip-defining points** (dome reflexes, a few mm apart, fused into one highlight in bulbous or thick-skinned tips); the **supratip break** above them; the **columella–lobule junction** (c′, "double break") and infratip lobule below. The basal view is an isosceles triangle, columella ≈ ⅔ and lobule ≈ ⅓ of its height; nostrils are teardrops, more vertical in narrow noses, rounder and more horizontal in wide ones.
- **Alar–columellar relationship:** in profile 2–4 mm of columella shows below the alar rim; the nostril's long axis sits 1–2 mm from both (Gunter) 🧠. A hanging columella (>4 mm) or retracted ala is an individual trait, not an error.

**Soft tissue and muscles**
- Cover is ≈3.0 mm at rhinion and ≈6.0 mm at nasion (pooled) [12] 📄; nasion rises with BMI, 5.57 → 8.25 mm across BMI groups [13], so ≈6 mm, more with BMI ✅. Men are thicker at nasion, rhinion and over the nose [14][38] ✅. Supratip and tip carry ~2–4 mm in thin skin and ≥4–6 mm in thick sebaceous skin, which blurs the domes 🧠.
- Nasalis flares the nostrils and makes vertical "bunny lines" [24][25] ✅. LLSAN inserts into ala and upper lip, pulling the ala up and out [26][27] ✅. Procerus arises at the lower nasal bone/ULC junction and makes horizontal nasal-root lines [28][29][30][31] ✅. Depressor septi nasi pulls the tip down and shortens the lip on smiling 🧠. FACS: AU9 nose wrinkler, AU38 nostril dilator, AU39 compressor 🧠.

**Variation**
- **Sex:** alare-to-midline 17.34 mm (M) vs 15.39 mm (F), 297 adults [18] 📄; en–en/al–al 0.89 (M) vs 1.01 (F) in southern-Spain 3D data [19] 📄, so men's noses exceed their intercanthal width and women's roughly match it. Men average a deeper radix, more acute nasofrontal and nasolabial angles and more humps; women more supratip break and rotation 🧠.
- **The orbito-nasal canon (al–al = en–en) fails in many people:** intercanthal width was shorter than nose width in most of 1,050 young adults [21] and the canon was not validated in Saudi students [22][23] ✅.
- **Populations** differ on average with large overlap. Malay 3D nose width is 39.6 (M) / 36.7 (F) mm [20] 📄 ↔️ (definition unconfirmed). Expert patterns 🧠 ↔️: East Asian — lower radix and dorsum, less projection, shorter columella, wider base, thicker skin; African descent — widest base (al–al/en–en ≈ 1.15–1.3, `facial-proportions-canons.md`), most flare, low dorsum, short columella, more acute nasolabial angle; European — narrowest, highest radix and dorsum, thinner skin, more humps. Nose shape is strongly heritable (GWAS, Adhikari et al. 2016) and nostril width tracks climate (Zaidi et al. 2017) 🧠 ↔️.
- **Age:** tip ptosis and lost rotation, a few mm of lengthening, columella retraction, a closing nasolabial angle (`aging-face.md`) 🧠. Children: short, upturned, low-bridged; projection matures at puberty (`child-growth.md`) 🧠.

**Reading the nose in photos**
- Distance, not focal length, sets perspective [34][35] ✅ (practitioner sources). The tip is the nearest point and is enlarged most. The reported selfie effect is ≈30% wider nasal base at 12 in than 5 ft (Ward et al. 2018) [40] 📄 ↔️, against ~10–12% from geometry alone (`reference-camera-setup.md`).
- prn sits ~25–30 mm in front of n′ and the alar base, so pitch moves it ≈0.5 mm per degree against them; a camera 10° low shortens and up-tilts the nose and shows the nostrils 🧠.
- Dorsal aesthetic lines (brow to tip-defining points), tip highlights, supratip and alar-groove shadows are lighting cues, not edges; detected prn drifts toward the highlight (`landmark-detection.md`) 🧠.
- Head-pose pipelines lean on the nose tip, e.g. one of six PnP points with focal ≈ image width as an uncalibrated approximation [36] 📄.
- dlib-68 nose = indices 27–35 [32][33] ✅: 27–30 bridge to tip, 31–35 nostril base, 33 ≈ sn 🧠. Neither dlib nor MediaPipe marks anatomical al; their "alar" points sit near the alar base 🧠 ↔️.

## Applying this to photo→3D face sculpting in Blender

1. **Shoot for the nose:** frontal, both true profiles, both ¾ and one basal ("worm's-eye", chin up 30–45°) view; ≥1.5 m, 85–135 mm-equivalent, camera at nose height; neutral face, quiet breathing (no flare), no smile (depressor septi drops the tip); glasses off well beforehand (pad marks dent the dorsum); one diffuse and one side-lit frame 🧠.
2. **Solve cameras before measuring** (`reference-camera-setup.md`); never trace a nose from a <1 m photo in an orthographic view. With selfies only, fix distance from a metric cue (iris ≈11.7 mm) and expect al–al errors of ≥5–10% 🧠.
3. **Level to Frankfort first.** Interior angles (nasolabial, nasofrontal) survive roll but not yaw off true profile 🧠.
4. **Sculpt in order of observability** 🧠: radix height/depth and nasofrontal angle → bony dorsum n′→rhinion tight to the profile (with a skull layer, skin 1.5–4 mm over the nasal bones) → cartilaginous dorsum (hump or supratip break) → tip projection (Goode as a warning only) → rotation (nasolabial angle, 2–4 mm columellar show) → alar crease (ac 10–15 mm behind sn) and rim arc → al–al and ac–ac in the frontal camera → tip lobule (two domes, infratip lobule) → nostrils and columella from the basal view → real asymmetry.
5. **Measure in-scene:** empties at g′, n′, prn, c′, sn, ls, pg′, al, ac; a short script prints Table 2 quantities for comparison with the same points clicked on the solved profile photo; overlay Workbench flat-black silhouettes 🧠.
6. **Topology:** alar-crease loops continuing into the nasolabial fold; concentric nostril loops around a real vestibule 8–12 mm deep (basal and ¾ views see inside); ≥3 loops across the columella; extra density over the domes; the alar rim a rolled cartilage-free edge, not a crease 🧠.
7. **Lighting check:** a matcap or Eevee render lit from the photo's key direction must reproduce the dorsal lines, tip-defining points and supratip shadow, by which frontal nose likeness is judged 🧠.
8. **What goes wrong** 🧠: width inflated by selfies or narrowed by idealisation or a European template; projection too low from frontal-only 3DMM fits; one pointed tip instead of two domes under a skin envelope; a shallow or high alar crease, or an alar base too far forward; nostrils over-visible from a low camera; deviation symmetrised and humps smoothed away; a nose taken from a smiling photo.

- **One triangle, one prior.** Encode the nasofacial angle (or Goode) once; stacking Goode, Crumley and nasofacial triple-counts. Aesthetic ideals are UI warnings (weight ≤ 0.1 of the population prior), never targets 🧠.
- **Observation model:** prn σ 2.5% and sn σ 1.5% of image IOD (`landmark-detection.md`); allow a 1–2 mm prn bias toward the estimated highlight; map detector "alar" points to ac/sbal and take al from an edge term on the frontal alar contour 🧠.
- **Silhouette first:** in true profiles fit the n′→prn→c′→sn contour with a sliding chamfer term (% IOD); ¾ silhouettes constrain dorsal width and alar-groove depth. Build profile ratios from midline points, because off-profile yaw shifts ac by x·sin ψ (Table 4) while barely shortening midline depth 🧠.
- **Depth–distance coupling:** with frontal-only data, projection is observable only through perspective, so camera distance and projection trade off. Fix distance by multi-view or a metric cue, let the projection prior dominate when no profile exists, and report projection σ 🧠.
- **Hard constraints** (hinge penalties) 🧠: skin ≥ 1.5 mm over nasal bones (`soft-tissue-thickness.md`); columella below the alar rim, show 0–7 mm; prn ahead of sn and ac; ac 8–16 mm behind sn; no nostril-rim self-intersection.
- **Symmetry:** a soft bilateral prior plus explicit midline-deviation parameters for tip, columella and dorsum (C/S curve); never snap prn to x = 0 🧠.
- **Template warping:** stable vertex IDs for n′, skin rhinion, supratip, tip-defining points L/R, prn, c′, sn, al/ac/sbal L/R and nostril apex L/R; ARAP or biharmonic handles, high rigidity on vestibule loops so nostrils do not collapse 🧠.
- **Expression gating:** if AU12, AU9 or AU38 is detected, down-weight that photo's nose observations; a smile drops the tip and widens the alae 🧠.
- **Evaluation:** per-feature errors (al–al, n–prn, sn–prn in mm; nasolabial and nasofrontal in degrees), not one RMS 🧠.

## Disagreements, myths & open questions

- **Ideal ≠ normal** ↔️: rhinoplasty ideals come from small, mostly European "attractive" samples; healthy noses often fall outside them (Leong & White 2006 🧠), and canon tests fail broadly [19][21][22] ✅.
- **Projection ratios disagree by baseline** ↔️: Goode from the alar crease (0.55–0.60) vs Byrd from the alar–cheek junction (0.67).
- **Nasolabial norms** span 90–120° partly through definition (columella tangent vs sn–c′ line; lip tangent vs sn–ls) 🧠 ↔️. **Subnasale and n′** are defined inconsistently [1][2][3] ↔️.
- **Skull→nose rules are weak** [10][11][16] ✅; the two-tangent and "3 × ANS" rules are contested (`forensic-reconstruction.md`) ↔️.
- **Selfie-effect size** (≈30% clinical vs ~10–12% geometric) is unresolved ↔️.
- **Myths** 🧠: "the nose grows throughout life" (mostly soft-tissue droop and elongation; magnitude debated ↔️); "nostrils should never show from the front" (depends on rotation and camera height); "the brightest point is the tip".
- **Open:** sourced population norms for nasal angles; nose-specific error figures for 3DMM and regression methods; verified MediaPipe alar indices; nostril-shape norms; basal-view datasets.
