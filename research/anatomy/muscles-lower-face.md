# Muscles of the lower face, lips and chin

> The lower face is driven by a thin, interwoven muscle system rather than discrete bellies: orbicularis oris (a composite of interlacing quadrants and strata, not a true sphincter), two lower-lip/commissure depressors (depressor anguli oris, DAO; depressor labii inferioris, DLI), the cone-shaped mentalis, and the sheet-like platysma, all converging on the modiolus at the mouth corner. For likeness work these muscles show up mainly as surface signatures: the mentolabial sulcus, the marionette fold (shaped by DAO's skin insertion plus the mandibular retaining ligament), chin dimpling from mentalis, and the commissure's resting position. The only hard numbers available are cadaveric muscle widths (DLI about 20 mm, DAO about 14 mm) and innervation percentages from one surgical cohort. Both come from a single source each. Several basic attachments (DAO, DLI, mentalis insertions) and the nature of the modiolus itself are contested.

## Key facts

- **Orbicularis oris (OO) is not a true sphincter.** It is described as four independent quadrants (upper/lower, left/right) that interlace to give a roughly circular action, with many strata of fibres running in different directions [1][4]. ✅ (Note: [1] and [2] are two PMC records with the same title, so they are counted as one source.)
- **OO is composite.** It has a larger peripheral part and a smaller marginal part (the marginal part lies under the vermilion). In dissection, the upper and lower halves each have an inner marginal part and an outer labial part, and the perioral muscles as a whole lie in three layers [3]. ⚠️
- **Much of OO is borrowed from neighbouring muscles.** Most peripheral fibres come from the surrounding facial muscles, with buccinator named as the main contributor in one source. The marginal part arises from the sides of the modiolus [3][5]. ⚠️ (the buccinator emphasis rests on one source)
- **Fibre layer sets lip shape.** Strong contraction of the superficial fibres with the diagonal fibres relaxed protrudes (pouts) the lips. Contraction of the deep and oblique fibres presses the lips against the teeth [3]. ⚠️ A biomechanical model links OO activation and jaw posture to lip shape [31]; its quantitative results were not extracted.
- **DAO:** arises from the mental tubercle and the oblique line of the mandible and inserts at the modiolus [6]. Sources disagree on what else it joins: skin at the mouth angle plus OO, risorius and levator anguli oris [6], or continuity downward with platysma and the cervical fascia [20]. ↔️
- **DLI:** arises from the oblique line between the symphysis and the mental foramen and depresses the lower lip [7]. Its insertion is given as blending with OO [7] or as the skin and submucosa of the lower lip [3]. ↔️
- **Mentalis:** a cone-shaped muscle from the incisive fossa of the mandible that inserts into the chin skin. It raises the chin skin and elevates the lower lip [8]. Another source says its fibres insert into the dermis, which is why contraction dimples the overlying skin [21]. ⚠️
- **Measured lower-lip displacement (1978 stimulation study):** activating mentalis moved the lower-lip midline superiorly and anteriorly; activating DLI moved it inferiorly and posteriorly [13]. ⚠️
- **Muscle widths in cadavers:** DLI 20 ± 4 mm and DAO 14 ± 3 mm. The authors recommend extending myectomy laterally to cover the full width [11][32]. ⚠️
- **Lower-lip innervation (Plast Reconstr Surg, 45 synkinesis patients):** 95.5% of DLI branches were classed as "cervical" and 68.8% were pure DLI branches. DAO and mentalis had mixed innervation. In a sub-analysis (n = 24), marginal mandibular neurectomy did not worsen lower-lip position [12]. ⚠️ "Cervical" conflicts with the textbook teaching that the marginal mandibular branch supplies DLI, so read the full text before relying on it.
- **Commissure vectors:** zygomaticus major pulls the mouth corner superolaterally and backward; levator anguli oris shifts that vector upward; DAO is the main depressor of the corner [14]. ⚠️
- **The modiolus is where the mouth-corner muscles converge.** A 3D-reconstruction dissection study names OO, buccinator, zygomaticus major, levator anguli oris and DAO as its core components, and reports that labial OO and zygomaticus major bundles cross obliquely and interweave tightly inside it [15]. ⚠️ A micro-CT iodine-staining study also built 3D models of the corner-of-mouth muscles [28]; numbers were not extracted.
- **What the modiolus actually is remains disputed.** Sources describe it as a fibromuscular node, as a mere cluster of convergence sites (fetal histology [16]), or as a structure with its own ligament (2025 cadaver study [17]). ↔️ The modiolus is reported to be better defined in humans than in other primates [18], and a separate paper argues that DAO is uniquely human [19]. ⚠️
- **Marionette fold mechanics:** the upper part of the fold is formed by DAO's skin insertion, and contraction of DAO, platysma and mentalis contributes [9][3]. The mandibular retaining ligament anchors DAO, platysma and DLI to the mandible and ties down the fold's lower end [22]. Ageing adds bone resorption, gravity, and loss of collagen and elastin [10]. ✅ (the fold has several causes)
- **Marionette severity is graded.** A validated photonumeric scale exists [10], but its grade definitions were not extracted. "Type IV" folds are reported to resist filler and botulinum toxin [26]. ⚠️
- **Platysma:** arises from the fascia over the pectoral and deltoid regions and inserts mainly on the lower border of the mandible. Its posterior fibres cross the mandibular angle into buccinator and the modiolus. The facial part is supplied mainly by the marginal mandibular branch [23][20]. ✅ A 2017 review found that existing classifications use arbitrary standards and that thickness and age-related change remain unstudied [24]. ⚠️
- **Chin clefts and dimples come in two kinds.** A **cleft chin** is skeletal: the two halves of the mandible fail to fuse fully at the lower midline, and the trait is heritable with variable penetrance [25]. **Dynamic dimpling** (the "cobblestone" chin) comes from mentalis contraction or overactivity pulling on its skin insertions [21]. McGill states that chin dimples "have nothing to do with muscles" [25], which holds for clefts but not for dynamic dimpling. ↔️ (the two sources describe different phenomena)

## Applying this to photo→3D face sculpting in Blender

1. **Make sure the reference shows a neutral lower face before measuring anything.** Mentalis lifts and pushes the lower lip forward [13] and dimples the chin skin [21]. A subject straining to close the lips shows a flattened mentolabial sulcus and an "orange-peel" chin. If you sculpt from that frame, both get baked into the neutral mesh. Reject frames with a textured chin, or move those features into a shape key. (This is an inference from [13][21], not a tested rule.)
2. **Classify the chin groove before sculpting it.** If a vertical midline groove is present at rest, symmetric, and still visible with the face relaxed, treat it as a skeletal cleft [25]: put it in the bone/base layer and keep it on every expression shape. If it appears only when the chin is tensed, treat it as mentalis dimpling and put it in a mentalis shape key.
3. **The marionette fold has two parts.** The upper segment runs down from the commissure and modiolus and comes from DAO's skin insertion [9][3]. The lower segment is tied to the mandible by the retaining ligament [22]. Sculpt a fold that is crisper near the commissure and anchored at a fixed point near the mandible border, and do not let the jowl slide across that anchor. Depth depends on age [10], so take it from the subject's photo rather than a template.
4. **Treat the modiolus as a pole, not a smooth curve.** Five or more muscles meet there [15]. In topology, the concentric OO loops should end in a pole at each mouth corner, with edge flow running out along DAO (inferolaterally toward the mental tubercle), zygomaticus major (superolaterally), and the platysma/buccinator direction (posterolaterally). When the corner falls between edge loops, smiles and frowns crease in the wrong place.
5. **Use the measured widths to size deformation regions.** For a DAO shape key or weight map, start the band at about 14 mm wide where the muscle meets the mandible, and use about 20 mm for DLI [11]. Scale these to the subject (the values are cadaveric, with an unknown and probably small n). DLI lies medial to DAO (its origin is between the symphysis and the mental foramen [7]), so the two masks should overlap only slightly.
6. **Model the two OO layers separately.** The marginal part, under the vermilion, sets the lip roll and the vermilion border; the peripheral part sets the skin lip [3]. Pouting comes from the superficial fibres and lip compression from the deep fibres [3], so build two shape keys rather than one "OO" key.
7. **Common failures:** a mentolabial sulcus made too deep because the lower lip was everted in the photo; marionette lines baked in from a frowning frame; perfectly mirrored commissures (real faces are asymmetric, and innervation of DAO and mentalis is mixed [12]); platysma bands added to young subjects; wide-angle lens distortion inflating chin and lip projection (see the camera and lens notes elsewhere in the library).

## Algorithmic implications

- **Landmarks:** define each modiolus as a derived landmark tied to the commissure. Do not hard-code an offset in mm, because no sourced value was found; learn the offset per subject or leave it as a free parameter. Make it a high-weight anchor for both the OO ring and the radial muscle vectors.
- **Expression priors:** encode Table 3 as signed unit vectors on control points (lower-lip midline: mentalis +up/+forward, DLI −down/−back; commissure: zygomaticus major up-out-back, levator anguli oris up, DAO down). Use them as priors when separating expression from identity, so that the solver blames a raised, protruded lower lip on mentalis activation rather than on identity shape.
- **Neutral-state gate:** before fitting identity, score likely mentalis activation (chin texture energy, a flattened sulcus) and likely DAO activation (commissure below its neutral estimate). Down-weight or reject those frames.
- **Soft-tissue tethering:** add a regularizer that limits vertical displacement of skin near the mandibular retaining ligament [22]. Model the marionette fold as a crease curve from modiolus to ligament anchor, with depth as an age-related parameter. Map it to the photonumeric scale [10] once its grades have been extracted.
- **Separate parameters for static and dynamic chin features:** use a skeletal `chin_cleft_depth` (bone layer, persists across expressions) and a `mentalis_dimple` blendshape (dynamic). They should never share a parameter.
- **Coupled sheets:** whether DAO and platysma are continuous is contested [6][20]. Expose a `dao_platysma_coupling` weight between 0 and 1 rather than hard-separating the two regions.
- **Width priors:** Gaussian priors on region widths (DLI μ = 20, σ = 4 mm; DAO μ = 14, σ = 3 mm [11]), normalized by a face-width measure. Flag them as single-source and weakly weighted.
- **Asymmetry:** fit left and right independently, using symmetry as a soft prior only.

## Disagreements, myths & refuted claims

- **The OO "sphincter" myth:** OO is described as interlacing quadrants and strata, not a single ring [1][4]. A rig with a single radial "pucker" will not reproduce pout and press as separate actions.
- **DAO insertion:** skin, OO, risorius and levator anguli oris [6] versus continuity with platysma and the cervical fascia [20]. These may be complementary rather than contradictory; this was not resolved.
- **DLI insertion:** OO [7] versus lower-lip skin and submucosa [3].
- **Mentalis insertion:** chin skin [8] versus dermis [21]. These are close to compatible.
- **The modiolus:** a fibromuscular node, a convergence cluster [16], or a structure with its own ligament [17]. The fetal-histology result may not carry over to adults.
- **Chin dimples and muscles:** McGill [25] says they are unrelated; mentalis sources [21] say otherwise. Resolution: clefts are skeletal, while dynamic dimpling is muscular.
- **DLI "cervical" innervation (95.5%) [12]:** at odds with the usual marginal-mandibular teaching. This is unconfirmed, not refuted.
- **Harvest corrections:** [1] and [2] share a title and should not count as two independent sources. The McGill URL belongs to its Office for Science and Society, not an "Office of Sustainability" as the harvest labelled it. The Wikipedia URL [21] is reproduced as it appeared in the results (en.wikipedia.com).
- **Refuted:** none. With no searches available, nothing could be refuted.

## Open questions

- What are the population norms for mentolabial sulcus depth and angle, and the modiolus position in mm relative to the commissure? These are the most important missing numbers for a fitting engine.
- Perioral muscle and platysma thickness from ultrasound or MRI, and how they change with age [24].
- What did the DAO ultrasound study [27] find for DAO coordinates and morphology, and what are the grade definitions in the marionette scale [10]?
- What sample size underlies the 20 ± 4 mm and 14 ± 3 mm widths [11], and in what year was the study published? Is the J Craniofac Surg article the same study as the DiVA thesis chapter [32]?
- What did the 2024 Surgical and Radiologic Anatomy perioral study [30] and the corner-mouth-lift paper [29] report? Neither was opened.
- Does the evidence support or criticize botulinum toxin for DAO and mentalis as a treatment for marionette lines and dimpling? This is relevant only as a proxy for which muscle drives which fold. It was not searched.
