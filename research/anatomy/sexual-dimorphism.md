# Sexual dimorphism of the face and skull

> Adult men's faces average about 5–9% larger than women's on most linear dimensions (Cohen's d ≈ 1.3–1.8 for face height, nose width, bizygomatic and jaw width), but differ by only d ≈ 0–0.5 on eye-region measures, and eyeball size does not differ. Every single measure overlaps: at d = 1.5 the two distributions share about 45% of their area. Beyond size, men have a projecting glabella and medial brow ridge, a sloping forehead, a larger nose, a taller lower face and chin, bigger masseters and thicker midline soft tissue, while women have a vertical forehead with frontal eminences, relatively larger-looking orbits, more lateral-cheek fat and lips that are fuller relative to the face. These moderate differences add up to ≈90–97% whole-face separability, so a fitter should treat sex as one continuous latent axis with overlapping priors, never as a template switch or a hard constraint. ## Key facts

A partial verification pass ran on 2026-11-20 (see the log at the end). The harvested facts come from search snippets, not full texts.

**Size, overlap, separability**
- Men are larger on nearly every linear facial dimension [1][2][4][5][6] ✅. Face height n–gn differs by 8–10 mm, d ≈ 1.4–1.8 (Table 1) [1][2][3] ✅. Male SDs are larger in mm, but CVs are similar 🧠.
- **Largest d:** face height, lower-face and chin height, nose width and height, zy–zy and head size 🧠. **Smallest d:** en–en (0.7–2.3 mm [1][4][5] ✅; d ≈ 0.3 [1]), the eye fissure and vermilion heights 🧠.
- **Allometry.** Much of the dimorphism is size, but a size-free residue remains at the brow and glabella, nose, chin and jaw angle (Rosas & Bastir 2002) 🧠. A female face is not a scaled-down male face.
- **Separability.** Many moderate differences combine. Whole-face 3D shape gives a Mahalanobis D ≈ 3–3.5, or ≈93–96% correct classification 🧠. Humans sex hair-hidden photos at ≈95% (Bruce et al. 1993) 🧠. Skulls sex at ≈80–90% from visual traits (Walker 2008), and the mandible alone at ≈75–85% 🧠.
- **Population.** Absolute values and the degree of dimorphism vary by population, so a robust female can score "male" on another population's standard 🧠 ↔️. Forehead height varies most between ethnic groups [10] 📄, and the classic canons ignore sex and ethnicity [34] 📄.

**Upper third**
- **Brow and forehead** 🧠. Men have a larger glabella and medial superciliary arches (with a bigger frontal sinus), a blunt, rounded orbital margin, a forehead sloping back and a supraglabellar dip. Women have a near-vertical forehead with frontal eminences, a sharp, thin margin and one continuous convex profile. Forensic standards grade these traits 1–5 (Buikstra & Ubelaker 1994) 🧠. See `anatomy/forehead-brow-temple-hairline.md`.
- Forehead soft tissue is thicker in men at all ages and in most regions (MRI) [15] 📄. It adds to the bony difference 🧠.
- **Eyebrows.** Male brows sit lower (on the rim) and are flatter and thicker. Female brows sit higher with a lateral arch, so the brow-to-lid distance is larger. This is a strong perceptual sex cue (Brown & Perrett 1993) 🧠, but grooming makes it hair, not bone 🧠.
- **Temple.** Temporal fossa volume is similar in both sexes [28] 📄. The temporalis is thicker in men: MRI (n = 360, aged 35–84, except over 70) [25] and ultrasound [26] ✅. The temple difference is therefore muscle 🧠.

**Eyes, nose, midface**
- **Eyes.** Eyeball diameters do not differ by sex [29] 📄, and iris diameter differs by tenths of a mm at most 🧠. Female orbits are relatively larger and rounder within a smaller face, so "bigger eyes" is a proportion effect 🧠. p–p and ex–ex have d ≈ 0.8–0.9 🧠.
- **Nose size.** The nose is larger in men in every dimension (al–al d ≈ 1.7, NAW 🧠). Alare–midline is 17.34 vs 15.39 mm (n = 297) [6] 📄. In southern Spain (3D), en–en/al–al is 0.89 (M) vs 1.01 (F), and ch–ch/en–en is 1.59 vs 1.52 [7] 📄. Men's noses are wider than their intercanthal distance.
- **Nose profile.** Men tend to have a higher radix, a straight or convex dorsum and less tip rotation. Women tend to have a slightly concave dorsum and a nasolabial angle about 5–15° more obtuse 🧠 ↔️. Published "ideal" angles are aesthetic targets ↔️.
- **Cheek.** zy–zy is larger in men (d ≈ 1.4–1.6), with a more robust zygoma and arch 🧠. Women carry more fat at the lateral orbit, inferior malar area and gonion, and the FSTT sex sign flips by region [18] ↔️. "High cheekbones" in women are fat plus a narrower lower face, not a higher zygoma 🧠.

**Mouth, jaw, chin**
- **Mouth.** ch–ch (d ≈ 0.9) and sn–sto (d ≈ 1.0–1.3) are larger in men. Vermilion heights barely differ (d ≈ 0.3–0.5), so female lips are fuller only relative to the lower face 🧠. Resting upper-incisor display is ≈3.4 mm in women and ≈1.9 mm in men (Vig & Brundo 1978) 🧠.
- **Midline soft tissue** is thicker in men: mid-philtrum 16.41 vs 13.95 mm (Iranian MRI) [16], most landmarks (Korean CBCT) [17], and nasal and perioral regions (Thai) [18] ✅.
- **Mandible.** It is larger in men, with a taller ramus and symphysis, a wider bigonial span and more everted gonia 🧠. The mental protuberance is larger, and chin and symphysis shape differ (CT) [11] 📄.
- **Gonial angle.** Reported sex differences are small and inconsistent: no significant difference in a 250-subject panoramic study (127.08° F vs 127.27° M) [43], women 1.6° larger on cephalographs (122.7° vs 121.1°) [42], and one digital study 7.8° larger in women (130.8° vs 123.0°). The earlier "men ~2–6° smaller" is contested, range ≈0–8° with direction not consistent ↔️. The "90° M / 110° F" pair conflicts with the adult ≈120° [12] and the 120.17° of young male mandibles [13] ✅. It is rejected.
- **Jaw taper.** The bony go–go/zy–zy ratio barely differs (≈0.71 vs 0.70) 🧠 ↔️. The tapered female lower face comes from smaller masseters, a narrower, rounder chin and fat distribution 🧠.
  - Masseter volume is ≈35 vs 25 mL (older Japanese, MRI) [23][24] 📄, and masseter thickness is greater in men, relaxed and contracted [27] 📄.
  - Pogonion soft tissue is ≈12.3 vs 11.0 mm (Azerbaijan CT) [14] 📄.

**Ear, neck, skin, life course**
- **Ears.** Ear length is larger in men ([30]: 62.9 vs 60.9 mm, d ≈ 0.45; [33]) ✅. Width is inconsistent [31] ↔️. Absolute length and width discriminate sex better than indices [31][32] 📄.
- **Larynx.** The laminae meet at ≈90° in men and ≈120° in women 🧠 (`anatomy/jaw-chin-neck.md`).
- **Skin.** Mid-cheek dermis is 1.877 vs 1.580 mm, with equal epidermis [21] 📄. This affects shading and pores more than shape 🧠.
- **BMI vs sex.** BMI is associated with FSTT more strongly than sex is [37] 📄. Sex effects were significant in 1,174 Chinese adults [19] but limited in Greek CT [20] ↔️. Sex-stratified data are a minority: 30 of 84 articles in a 2023 review [35] 📄. A 424-adult Han CBCT study models sex, age and BMI together [36] 📄.
- **Puberty.** Dimorphism is small before puberty and diverges after peak height velocity (~11–12 y F, ~13–14 y M). The male mandible, nose and brow keep growing into the late teens or early 20s 🧠 (`anatomy/child-growth.md`).
- **Hormone therapy.** In adults it shifts soft tissue (fat, masseter bulk, skin, beard) over months to years, but not bone. Bony brow, jaw and nose change only with surgery 🧠 ↔️.
- **Ageing.** The male temporalis advantage disappears after ~70 [25] 📄, and low masseter volume tracks sarcopenia [24] 📄. Muscle-driven dimorphism therefore shrinks with age, while bony dimorphism persists 🧠.

## Applying this to photo→3D face sculpting in Blender

1. **Record, don't infer.** Store custom properties on the head: `sex_prior` (male, female, unspecified or a numeric s), `age`, `population`, `bmi`, and optionally `hrt_years` and `facial_surgery` (with consent). Never set these from a classifier run on the photos 🧠.
2. **Set scale sex-neutrally.** Use a metric reference or iris diameter (eyeball size is sex-neutral [29]). If you must assume an IPD, use the sex-specific mean (≈64 M, ≈61.5 F mm, SD ≈3.5). The wrong-sex mean gives a ~3–4% scale error, about 4–5 mm on face height 🧠.
3. **Split size from shape on the base mesh.** Drive a uniform scale with a `size` property and a separate `SexAxis` shape key built from Procrustes-aligned (size-removed) male and female mean templates. Map the key so that s = −1 is the female mean and +1 the male mean, and allow −1.5 to +1.5. Set it from `sex_prior`, then let the fit move it 🧠.
4. **Measure the dimorphic signals from solved cameras, not raw photos.**
   - Profile: glabella projection ahead of soft nasion, the supraglabellar dip, forehead slope, nasofrontal and nasolabial angles, dorsum line, chin height and projection.
   - Frontal: al–al/en–en, ch–ch/en–en, brow-to-lid distance, chin width against mouth width, and jaw silhouette width at gonion level against zy–zy.
   - 3/4: malar apex and the mandibular border.
   Compute z-scores against same-sex, same-population rows of Table 1 🧠.
5. **Triage outliers.** For |z| > 2.5, first rule out photo problems: lens distance (a close camera inflates the nose and narrows the face), head pitch (chin-down hides the gonial angle and shortens the lower face), expression, beard, makeup and lighting. A value that survives is a likeness feature: protect it and never pull it toward the sex mean 🧠.
6. **Sculpt in order of dimorphic weight.** Brow and glabella mass, then ramus and jaw angle, chin box, nose size and dorsum, and finally soft tissue (malar fat, lips, temples). Keep the male brow medial, and keep a supraglabellar dip when the profile shows one 🧠.
7. **Beards** extend the apparent jaw and chin and fill the mentolabial sulcus. Take the jawline from beard-free cues (ear-to-angle line, cheek plane break, profile above the beard) plus bone priors and a masseter estimate 🧠.
8. **Makeup** moves shading edges (contour), brows (pencil) and vermilion borders (overlining). Trust silhouettes over shading, and take brow-ridge projection from the profile only 🧠.
9. **Lighting.** Top light deepens brow and under-cheek shadows (reads masculine); flat front light hides the brow (reads feminine). Judge brow and malar form under a neutral matcap with a profile overlay 🧠.
10. **Global sex check.** Temporarily move `SexAxis` by ±0.3 and compare silhouette overlays in all views. If one direction clearly fits better everywhere, the global sex setting was off; fix it, lock it, then sculpt locally 🧠.
11. **What goes wrong:**
   - Stylised base meshes and character-creator presets exaggerate dimorphism (tiny jaw, small nose, big eyes on female presets), which beautifies away likeness.
   - Male or NAW norms are applied to everyone.
   - Facial-feminisation or "ideal" targets are used as norms.
   - Brow and jaw types are mixed incoherently. A male brow with no supraglabellar break reads juvenile or female, but real people do mix traits, so trust measurements over coherence 🧠.

## Algorithmic implications

- **Model.** Use X = k·R(μ̄ + (s/2)·Δ_sex + Σ c_i·φ_i) + t. Here μ̄ is the sex-midpoint mean shape, Δ_sex is the size-free M − F mean difference, k is size and the φ_i are PCs of the **sex-centred** (within-sex) covariance, so the PCs do not re-encode sex 🧠.
- **Sex prior.** With means at s = ±1, the within-sex SD along the axis is 2/D. For whole-face D ≈ 3 that is ≈0.65 🧠.
  - Declared sex: s ~ N(±1, 0.65²).
  - Unspecified: a two-component mixture 0.5·N(−1, 0.65²) + 0.5·N(+1, 0.65²), or the single N(0, 1.2²) for a convex solver.
  - Custom or under hormone therapy: user-chosen s₀ with σ ≈ 1.0.
  - Inflate σ_s by ×1.3 when population is unknown or for sparse landmark sets (D ≈ 2) 🧠.
- **Size prior.** log k ~ N(log k̄ ± 0.035, 0.04²) by sex. The M/F log size gap is ≈0.06–0.08 (n–gn 0.082; zy–zy ≈0.06), and within-sex CV is 3–5% 🧠. When metric scale comes from the camera, fix k and let s carry shape only.
- **Landmark sigmas** (`face3d.solve.bundle.Priors.sigma`, head frame). Conditioning on sex mainly shifts **means**, by up to ±0.9 pooled SD for d ≈ 1.8. It shrinks SDs by only 1/√(1 + d²/4): ≈20% for d = 1.5 and nothing for en–en. Suggested within-sex sigmas: bony midline (g, n, rhinion, pogonion) 2.5–4 mm; nose tip and alae 3–4 mm; soft lateral points (soft zygion, soft gonion, cheek apex) 5–8 mm. Keep the current 8 mm default when sex is unspecified 🧠.
- **FSTT coupling.** Use t(x) = t̄(x)·exp(γ(x)·s/2 + β_bmi(x)·(BMI − 22)). γ ≈ +0.10 to +0.18 at philtrum, lips and pogonion ([16]: +18%; [14]: +12%) ✅, about +0.05 at forehead and nasion, and −0.05 to −0.10 at malar, lateral orbit and gonion ↔️. Cap |γ| ≤ 0.2. BMI terms should outweigh sex [37] 📄. This matches the s = ±½ convention used in `anatomy/soft-tissue-thickness.md`.
- **Which features move s.** Weight by d. Strong: lower-face height, nose width and height, brow and glabella projection, zy–zy, chin height and width, masseter bulge. Weak: en–en, ex–en, eye height, vermilion heights, ear width. Exclude fWHR, beard-region silhouettes and brow hair 🧠.
- **Constraints** are sex-agnostic hinges only, for example soft glabella at least 1 mm ahead of soft nasion. Never use hard sex-specific bounds 🧠.
- **Age gate.** Multiply Δ_sex by w(a) = logistic((a − a_PHV)/1.5 y) as in `anatomy/child-growth.md` 🧠.
- **Diagnostics.** Report posterior s and its σ. Warn, without erroring, when |s − s₀| > 2σ_s: the cause is usually a lens or pitch error, a beard or makeup, or a wrong prior. Output per-region residuals against both sex-conditional means 🧠.
- **Where to get Δ_sex.**
  - 3DFN sex-specific summary landmarks and growth curves [8][9] 📄 (European-ancestry US sample 🧠).
  - Regressing a 3DMM's shape coefficients on sex labels. The Basel Face Model 2009 shipped attribute directions including gender, and FLAME releases have included male, female and generic variants [39] 📄. The BFM 2009 licence is non-commercial research only [40] 📄; the BFM 2009 gender attribute directions remain 🧠. Licences, versions and access terms are time-sensitive: verify before use.

## Disagreements, myths & open questions

- **fWHR** ↔️ [41]. Weston et al. (2007) reported skull fWHR dimorphism. Meta-analyses (Geniole et al. 2015; Kramer 2017) found a tiny or absent sex difference. A 2018 commentary [41] disputes Kramer's reading and says skull-based dimorphism replicates Geniole; the d ≈ 0.1 figure is unconfirmed. Do not use fWHR as a sex cue or a prior.
- **Gonial angle** ↔️. The 90°/110° pair is wrong [12][13]. The real difference is small and inconsistent in sign [42][43].
- **"Men are thicker everywhere."** One compiled review cites a study finding exactly that [22] 📄, but Thai data contradict it laterally [18] ↔️. It holds at the midline and perioral sites [16][17] ✅.
- **"Women have bigger eyes."** Eyeballs match [29]. Orbits and openings look larger only relative to a smaller face and higher brows 🧠.
- **"High female cheekbones."** These come from fat and a narrower lower face, not from a higher zygoma 🧠.
- **"Square jaw = male."** The bony jaw-to-face ratio barely differs. Squareness is mostly masseter, chin shape and leanness 🧠 ↔️.
- **Neoteny** ↔️. Female faces are partly "juvenile" in proportion (smaller nose and jaw, relatively larger eyes), but this is not a general rule.
- **Secular gracilisation** ↔️. Whether skull dimorphism has fallen over recent centuries is debated.
- **Open questions:**
  - Dense, population-specific 3D M−F difference maps with within-sex SDs.
  - The size of the size-free shape residue per region.
  - Quantified soft-tissue change under hormone therapy.
  - Nasolabial and nasofrontal angle norms by sex measured in the same frame.
  - Whether population differences in dimorphism exceed sampling error.

- fWHR not meaningfully dimorphic (Kramer 2017; Geniole 2015, d ≈ 0.1): contested, badge stays ↔️. A commentary says Kramer's meta-analysis calls fWHR's dimorphism into question, but that the skull-based result replicates Geniole's dimorphism and the face-only effect is trend-level. The d ≈ 0.1 value was not confirmed. Source: https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5949324/
- Gonial angle differs 2–6° by sex (men smaller): contested, badge stays ↔️. Studies show no significant difference (127.08° F vs 127.27° M), women 1.6° larger, or women 7.8° larger; the "men smaller" direction is not supported. The 90°/110° pair stays rejected. Sources: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11031626/ , https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6981352/
