# Psychology of face recognition and likeness

> People recognise identity holistically. The eye region carries the most weight, and the brows count at least as much as the eyes; hair and outline dominate for unfamiliar viewers, and shape and pigmentation contribute roughly equally. Identity is coded as a deviation from an internal average, so caricature helps recognition, while shrinking the deviation (statistical priors, symmetrising, smoothing) is the commonest reason an accurate-looking head reads as "a relative". Familiar viewers tolerate global scale and stretch but not errors in internal feature shapes and relations. Unfamiliar viewers, the artist included, are poor judges. So: prioritise lids, brows, lips and the sitter's most distinctive measurements, audit prior shrinkage, and have people who know the sitter judge multi-view renders with eyes, brows and hair in place. ## Key facts

**Processing**
- **Holistic processing.** 🧠
  - Inversion hurts faces far more than objects (Yin 1969).
  - Aligned halves of two faces fuse into a new identity (composite effect; Young, Hellawell & Hay 1987).
  - Features are recognised better inside their own face (part–whole effect; Tanaka & Farah 1993).
  - Inverted eyes and mouth go unnoticed in an upside-down face (Thatcher illusion; Thompson 1980).
- **"Configural" means three things:** first-order layout, holistic binding, and second-order metric spacing (Maurer, Le Grand & Mondloch 2002). 🧠
- **Spacing versus feature shape is contested.** ↔️ McKone & Yovel (2009) found feature shape is also processed holistically. 2D inter-feature distances vary little between people and a lot across photos of one person, so they do not identify faces alone (Taschereau-Dumouchel et al. 2010; Burton et al. 2016). 🧠 Likeness means correct feature shapes in correct *3D* relations, not ratio-matching on one photo.
- **Familiar recognition survives large uniform stretches**, but not stretching of one half (Hole et al. 2002; Sandford & Burton 2014). 🧠 Recognisable is not the same as a good likeness.
- **Spatial band.** Identification relies most on about 8–16 cycles per face width (Costen, Parker & Craw 1996; Näsänen 1999). ↔️ Familiar faces survive very coarse images (Harmon 1973; Sinha et al. 2006). 🧠 On a 140 mm face that band is 9–18 mm form: orbit depth, lid and brow masses, nose and lip volumes, cheek planes. Pores do not fall in it. 🧠

**Regions**
- **Eyes.** Bubbles maps for identity centre on the eyes (Gosselin & Schyns 2001; Schyns, Bonnar & Gosselin 2002). The optimal first fixation is just below the eyes (Peterson & Eckstein 2012), and East Asian viewers fixate more centrally (Blais et al. 2008). 🧠
- **Brows ≥ eyes.** Removing the brows from celebrity photos hurt recognition more than removing the eyes (Sadr, Jarudi & Sinha 2003). 🧠 A few mm of brow height or tilt shifts perceived mood and sex. 🧠
- **Internal and external features.** Familiar faces are recognised mainly from internal features; unfamiliar faces as much from hair and outline (Ellis, Shepherd & Davies 1979). Hair can override the internal features (Sinha & Poggio 1996). 🧠
- **"Critical features."** ↔️ Abudarham & Yovel (2016) reported high sensitivity to lip thickness, eye shape, eyebrow thickness, eye colour and hair colour, and low sensitivity to eye distance, mouth size and face proportion. 🧠 The list is recalled, from rated 2D features, and conflicts with spacing studies.
- **Upper face over lower** for identity; the mouth matters more for expression; ears little. 🧠

**Shape, surface, image**
- **Shape and pigmentation contribute comparably** (O'Toole, Vetter & Blanz 1999; Russell & Sinha 2007). ↔️ Clay loses brow density, lashes, lip-colour boundary and iris contrast. 🧠
- **Lighting from below and contrast reversal** impair recognition (Johnston, Hill & Carman 1992). Any lighting change hurts unfamiliar matching. 🧠
- **Camera distance** changes configuration enough to impair identity matching (Noyes & Jenkins 2017); see `blender/reference-camera-setup.md`. 🧠
- **Viewpoint.** The three-quarter-view advantage is inconsistent (Liu & Chaudhuri 2002). ↔️ Unfamiliar recognition is view-dependent. 🧠
- **Realism raises the stakes:** distortions tolerated in stylised faces disturb on realistic ones (Seyama & Nagayama 2007). 🧠

**Face space and caricature**
- **Norm-based coding.** Faces are coded relative to an average (Valentine 1991), and distinctive faces are recognised better. Adapting to an anti-face shifts perception along the line through the mean (Leopold et al. 2001). 🧠
- **Caricature effect.** Caricatures are identified as well or better and faster; anti-caricatures worse (Brennan 1985; Rhodes, Brennan & Carey 1987; Benson & Perrett 1991; Lee, Byatt & Rhodes 2000; in 3D, O'Toole et al. 1997). 🧠 Best-likeness ratings peak at slight positive exaggeration. ↔️
- **Averaging flatters and erases.** Average faces are attractive (Langlois & Roggman 1990) and less identifiable. A regularised fit can look better and less like the person. 🧠
- **Adaptation.** Viewing distorted faces shifts the perceived norm (Webster & MacLin 1999); the artist adapts to the sculpt's errors. 🧠

**Observers**
- **Unfamiliar matching fails often:** about 10–30% errors with good images (Bruce et al. 1999; Burton, White & McNeill 2010). Passport officers are no better (White et al. 2014). ↔️
- **One photo is a sample.** Sorting 40 photos of two strangers, viewers made about 7–8 identities; familiar viewers made about 2 (Jenkins et al. 2011). 🧠
- **Other-race effect.** Own-race faces get more hits and fewer false alarms (Meissner & Brigham 2001), and other-race faces get less holistic processing. Algorithms show demographic differentials too (NIST IR 8280). 🧠 Artists and priors carry their own population's average.
- **Individual differences** run from prosopagnosia (about 2–2.5% ↔️) to super-recognisers (Russell, Duchaine & Nakayama 2009); screen judges with the CFMT or GFMT. 🧠
- **The sitter is biased:** people prefer their mirror image (Mita, Dermer & Knight 1977) and accept enhanced morphs as themselves (Epley & Whitchurch 2008). 🧠
- **Neutral structure reads as emotion** (Said, Sebe & Todorov 2009). A mouth-corner or brow error gets reported as "looks sad, not him". 🧠

### C. MAP shrinkage of the sitter's deviation from the mean (exact arithmetic)

The fit keeps k = σp²/(σp²+σd²) of the true deviation (σp prior SD, σd data error); 1−k is anti-caricature.

| σd (mm) → | 0.5 | 1 | 2 | 4 | 8 | 16 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| k, σp = 8 mm (engine default) | 0.996 | 0.985 | 0.941 | 0.800 | 0.500 | 0.200 |
| k, σp = 4 mm (≈ population SD) | 0.985 | 0.941 | 0.800 | 0.500 | 0.200 | 0.059 |

Typical σd values are 🧠 estimates:
- Lateral position, 3 or more views: 0.5–1 mm.
- Depth from two views at least 30° apart: about 2× the lateral error.
- Depth from a frontal view only: 10–20 mm.

A frontal-only fit keeps roughly 20–40% of the sitter's projection deviation.

## Applying this to photo→3D face sculpting in Blender

1. **Use many photos.** Take 10–30 images from several sessions within one period of the sitter's life, the measurement set from 1.5 m or more. Take each feature's median over at least 3 photos. 🧠
2. **Measure in Table B order** and normalise by IPD. Compute z-scores against sex- and ancestry-matched norms. Put the 3–5 largest |z| on a protect list: they are the caricature directions. Never "correct" them toward a canon. 🧠
3. **Get the thumbnail right first.** Render at 64–128 px across the face and compare with photos downsampled the same way. Start no pores until the thumbnail reads as the person. 🧠
4. **Add identity pigment before judging:**
   - eyeballs with iris and gaze (`blender/eyes-modeling-blender.md`);
   - painted brow shape and density;
   - the vermilion boundary;
   - a hair-cap mesh with the correct hairline.

   Blank-eyed, bald clay reads as unlike. 🧠
5. **Review lighting.** Key light above and 30–45° to the side, never from below. Use a matte grey matcap. Desaturate the photos (Hue Saturation Value node, Saturation 0) so clay is compared with clay. 🧠
6. **Discipline.**
   - Camera-matched overlays for every solved view, including 3/4 and profile.
   - Flip the render and photo together.
   - Roll the camera 180° to view upside down: this exposes contour shapes but hides configural errors, so check both.
   - Break every 20–30 minutes against adaptation. 🧠
7. **Caricature shape key.**
   - Basis = the template warped to the population-mean landmarks; "Identity" = the fit. Set the slider range −0.5 to 1.5.
   - If familiar viewers prefer about 1.1, a region is over-shrunk (Table C): fix it and export at 1.0. 🧠
8. **Judges.**
   - People who know the sitter, ideally from the sitter's own population.
   - Blind lineup: the likeness plus 5 foils (same hair cap, lighting, camera). Chance is 17%; aim for 80% or more (design target).
   - Show the sitter both the true and the mirrored render. 🧠
9. **Symptom → cause.** 🧠
   - **"A relative":** shrinkage, symmetrising, or softened protect-list features.
   - **"Eyes wrong":** lid peak, lid show versus crease, lower-lid height, tilt, depth, gaze.
   - **"Sad or angry":** mouth corners, inner-brow tilt.
   - **"Wrong age":** hooding, nasolabial fold, jowl, lip volume, or photos from another period.
   - **"Narrow face, big nose":** selfie-distance photos.
   - **"Front fine, 3/4 wrong":** the prior filled in depth.

## Algorithmic implications

- **Keep three weights separate.** 🧠
  - Noise goes in the data term (`ViewObs.sigma_px`, `weights`).
  - Population spread goes in the prior (`Priors.sigma`).
  - Perceptual weight (Table B) drives only evaluation, user guidance and requests for more views. Putting it in the data term would bias the solve.
- **Shrinkage audit.** Take k_i = 1 − σ²_post,i/σ²_prior,i from diag((JᵀJ)⁻¹) after LM in `face3d/solve/bundle.py`. Show a heatmap, and flag k < 0.8 on rank 1–5 regions by requesting another view. 🧠
- **Priors.** 🧠
  - Replace the scalar `sigma = 8.0` with per-landmark, per-axis arrays of 1.0–1.5 × the population SD.
  - Use a Student-t prior (ν ≈ 4) so features with |z| > 2 survive.
  - Use sex- and ancestry-matched means, never a default European mean.
- **Optional distinctiveness restore.** ↔️
  - Per region, X_out = μ + α(X_fit − μ), default α = 1.
  - An "unbiased" mode sets α = 1/k only where k ≥ 0.5, capped at 1.5. Elsewhere, flag the region rather than amplify noise.
  - Whether MAP or unbiased estimates look more like the person is unknown.
- **Likeness metric.** 🧠
  1. Umeyama alignment (`procrustes.py::umeyama`) on en, ex, n, glabella and sn.
  2. Low-pass both surfaces to wavelengths of 9 mm or more, then take the region-weighted RMS (Table B).
  3. Report three things separately: high-frequency "detail/age" residuals, global scale and aspect ratio.
- **Distinctiveness.** 🧠
  - Features: en–en, ex–ex, fissure height, canthal tilt, brow height at the head, peak and tail, brow–lid distance, al–al, n–sn, philtrum, vermilion heights, ch–ch, sto–me, go–go/zy–zy and n–me.
  - Normalise by IPD; D = √(Σz²/n).
  - The top-k |z| features need 2 or more views at least 30° apart.
- **Expression weights.** In non-neutral views, scale confidence by 0.2–0.3 for raised brows, for the mouth in smiles, and for the lower lid in Duchenne smiles. 🧠
- **Sessions.** Per-session cameras, shared identity, robust loss on per-session residuals. 🧠
- **Landmarks.** Add brow peak, upper-lid peak, lower-lid low point, crease height and the lower vermilion border to `synthetic.py` and the detector map. 🧠
- **Tests.** 🧠
  - With 3 or more views at least 30° apart: |dev_fit|/|dev_true| of 0.9–1.1 and a cosine of 0.95 or more on rank 1–5 landmarks.
  - A frontal-only fit must flag depth shrinkage.
- **Embedding scores** on clay renders are a tertiary check (`reconstruction/identity-embeddings.md`). 🧠

## Disagreements, myths & open questions

- **Spacing versus shape** ↔️: never implement "configural accuracy" as 2D ratio-matching.
- **Myth: "the eyes are 90% of a likeness."** Brows, configuration, hair (for strangers) and pigment all count. 🧠
- **Contested:** the critical-feature list ↔️, the size of the caricature effect in photoreal 3D ↔️, and the three-quarter-view advantage ↔️.
- **Myth: the sitter's approval proves accuracy** (mirror and self-enhancement bias). 🧠
- **Open questions:**
  - The 3D just-noticeable displacement for features of familiar faces (Haig 1984 tested 2D only).
  - Whether DCNN embeddings track human likeness of clay renders.
  - Whether MAP or unbiased estimates read as more like the person.
