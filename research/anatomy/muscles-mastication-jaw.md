# Masseter, temporalis and the jaw line

> Lower-face width, the apparent jaw angle and temple fullness depend on two chewing muscles laid over bone: the masseter on the ramus and the temporalis in the temporal fossa, each under fat, fascia and (for the masseter) the parotid gland. Relaxed adult masseter thickness is roughly 10-15 mm per side by ultrasound and rises about 27-30% on clenching. It is greater in men, in low-angle (short) faces and, on average, in bruxers. The adult temporalis is larger in men and shrinks with age. Botulinum toxin cuts masseter bulk by about 25-30% for months, but whether it changes bone is contested. For photo-to-3D the core problem is identifiability: a frontal photo confounds bone, muscle, gland and fat, so a fitter needs separate layers, a per-photo clench state and profile or 3/4 contour evidence.

## Key facts

**Anatomy that shapes the surface**
- **Masseter attachments.** The superficial part runs from the zygomatic process of the maxilla and the anterior two-thirds of the lower border of the zygomatic arch, down and back to the angle and the lower lateral ramus. The deep part is more vertical, from the posterior and deep arch to the upper ramus. The anterior border runs from under the zygoma body to the lower border of the mandible just behind the facial-artery (antegonial) notch, where the facial pulse can be felt. 🧠
- **What covers the masseter.** The parotid gland covers the posterior third, from in front of the tragus down to the angle. The parotid duct crosses the muscle about a finger's breadth below the arch. The buccal fat pad and the buccal hollow lie in front of the anterior border, and platysma covers the lowest part. Masseteric ligaments run along the anterior border, and the jowl forms in front of them. 🧠
- **Temporalis attachments.** It arises from the floor of the temporal fossa up to the *inferior* temporal line, and from the deep temporal fascia. That fascia attaches to the *superior* temporal line. A harvested summary says loosely that the muscle "originates" at the superior line [44]. 🧠 The tendon passes deep to the zygomatic arch and inserts on the coronoid process and anterior ramus. 🧠
- **Temple layers.** From the surface: skin, subcutaneous fat, temporoparietal fascia (continuous with the SMAS), the deep temporal fascia enclosing the superficial temporal fat pad, the deep temporal fat pad, temporalis, then bone [44] 🧠. Hollowing involves muscle atrophy or retraction [45] and fat [44]. ✅ The temporal line shows as a ridge between the forehead and temple planes in lean or older faces. 🧠

**Masseter size**
- **Thickness.** A systematic review gives male relaxed thickness as 10-15 mm and contracted as 14-19 mm, with study means of 12.0-17.2 mm. Probe, measurement site, sex and population all shift these values [1]. Primary studies fall inside the range [2][3]. ✅
- **Sex.** Men are thicker both relaxed and contracted [2][18][48]. ✅ Korean young adults measured 9.8 ± 1.3 (F) and 11.3 ± 1.2 mm (M) relaxed, and 12.4 ± 1.4 and 14.7 ± 1.4 mm clenched, which is +27% and +30% (derived) [2]. 📄
- **Vertical facial pattern.** Relaxed thickness was 13.94 mm in hypodivergent faces, 12.53 mm in normodivergent and 11.13 mm in hyperdivergent [3]. 📄 The direction is confirmed elsewhere [4][5][8]. ✅
- **Width, angle and occlusion.** Thickness correlates positively with bigonial and bizygomatic width and negatively with anterior face height and mandibular length (Raadsheer, cited secondhand) [8]. 📄 It correlates inversely with gonial angle and positively with ramus height [5]. 📄 It is greater in men and with normal occlusion [49]. 📄 All of this evidence is cross-sectional.
- **Volume.** In the Bunkyo older-adult MRI cohort, volume was about 35 mL (M) and 25 mL (F) (summary). Whether that is per side is unclear. Low volume tracks sarcopenia and osteoporosis risk [24][25]. 📄 (both papers come from the same cohort)
- **Age and growth.** Volume rises about 199% from age 0 to 17, with a left-side bias [26]. 📄 Thickness at 55+ is lower than at 18-35 [50]. 📄 Masticatory muscle declines with age [21][50]. ✅
- **Adaptation to the skeleton.** After orthognathic correction, volume and cross-sectional area on the non-deviated side fell 13.5% and 16.4% [27]. Volume also changes after setback [28]. ✅ Masseter asymmetry therefore usually goes with skeletal asymmetry. 🧠 Strong masseters go with a laterally flared, everted angle and a roughened masseteric tuberosity. 🧠

**Bruxism, hypertrophy, botulinum toxin**
- **Bruxism.** Clenching is associated with masseter (and sometimes temporalis) enlargement and a "square" lower face [9][10][34][36]. ✅ Individual values overlap with controls, and in one sample there was no difference (12.91 against 13.07 mm, summary) [11]. ↔️
- **A wide lower third is bone plus muscle.** It is attributed mainly to angle morphology plus masseter hypertrophy [6][34]. ✅ Hypertrophy can be one-sided. Parotid enlargement mimics it but does not harden on clenching. 🧠
- **Toxin reduces bulk.** Thickness, volume and lower-face width all fall [6][13][14][15][16][39][47]. ✅ Reported effects are about −25% thickness [6] and −30% volume on CT [15]. 📄 A meta-analysis of 5 RCTs (536 participants) found short-term benefit with high heterogeneity [39]. An earlier review found no trials [40]. 📄
- **Time course and bone.** The effect peaks at about 3 months [15][41]. ✅ Duration is reported as about 6 months [15] against 6-12 months [41]. ↔️ Bone effects are contested: see Table 4 [7][37][38]. ↔️
- **Function and side effects.** Repeated doses reduce contraction and masticatory performance [6]. 📄 Side effects include fatigue and a limited or asymmetric smile [42]. 📄 There is no consensus dose, and the use is off-label [34][42]. ✅ Surgical debulking and angle reduction also exist [8][35]. ✅

**Temporalis size**
- **Sex.** Adults: larger in men [18][20][21][30]. ✅ The difference ranges from about 32% (derived from [21]) to about 40% [20]. ↔️ In children aged 5-15 there is no sex effect [19]. 📄
- **Age.** In Taiwanese adults, male volume peaked at 36.9 mL (age 20-29) and fell 38%. Female volume peaked at 27.9 mL (age 30-39) and fell 60% [21]. 📄 Thickness tracks weight and BMI [30]. 📄
- **Thickness depends on method.** Ultrasound gives 8.8 ± 1.1 mm at rest in one reference cohort against about 6.4 and 8.1 mm in others [29]. ↔️ MRI in 360 adults gave 3.69-16.90 mm [30]. 📄 CT and MRI agree (ICC 0.894), but MRI protocols differ [32]. 📄 Temporalis thickness is used as a sarcopenia marker [31][32]. ✅
- **Clenching.** No significant upright thickening on clenching, unlike the masseter. The lower temporalis differs by sex, the upper does not [18]. 📄
- **Variation and surgery.** Some people have only the deep layer [22]. 📄 Hollowing after pterional craniotomy is common, at 87-100% in one summary [23], but incidence varies with technique and definition. 🧠 ↔️ Fat, filler, PEEK and flap repairs exist, and none is standard [45]. 📄

**What each view shows** 🧠
- **Frontal.** The masseter sets the outline below the ear lobe. Hypertrophy turns a tapered lower face square, with a convexity behind the buccal hollow.
- **3/4.** The anterior border is a soft vertical edge, which sharpens on clenching.
- **Profile.** The angle silhouette is mostly bone. A big masseter rounds the angle and adds fullness above and behind it, but barely moves the posterior ramus line.
- **Temple.** It flickers on clenching in lean subjects and is more concave in older, lean or female faces.

## Applying this to photo→3D face sculpting in Blender

1. **Capture.** Take a frontal, both 3/4 views (yaw 30-50°) and both profiles at 1.5 m or more, with an 85-135 mm equivalent lens. Use a rest pose of "lips together, teeth apart". Then take a deliberate *clenched* frontal and 3/4 pair from the same camera positions. The rest-to-clench difference shows the masseter's anterior border and belly, and the temple flicker shows the temporalis. Record any jaw-slimming injections, because bulk changes 25-30% over months [6][15]. Never mix photos from different treatment windows.
2. **Perspective.** In a 40 cm selfie the jaw plane is shrunk about 20% relative to the nose. Judge width only in camera-solved views. 🧠
3. **Profile first, for bone.** Construct soft-tissue gonion where the tangent to the posterior ramus contour meets the tangent to the lower border. Block in ramus height, gonial angle (Table 5) and the antegonial notch. A long, steep-plane face with a big flaring jaw is atypical [3][4][5]. Rule out a clench, parotid or fat before accepting it.
4. **3/4 views, for muscle.** Trace the anterior masseter border with a Bezier guide from under the zygoma body to the antegonial notch. Fit the far-side jaw silhouette, which passes over the belly.
5. **Frontal last.** Silhouette width at jaw level = bone + 2 × (masseter + fat), plus parotid. Whatever the profile-fitted bone leaves unexplained goes to the soft layers.
6. **Layers in metric.** Use Metric units at scale 1.0, so 3 mm = 0.003. Add vertex groups `masseter_L/R` (peak over the lower-middle ramus, fading at the anterior border, the arch and just under the angle), `temporalis_L/R`, `parotid_L/R`, `buccal_hollow_L/R` and `jowl_L/R`. Drive shape keys, or Normal-direction Displace modifiers, from custom properties in millimetres (`t_m_L`, `clench`, `temple_fill_L`). Multires has no native sculpt layers, so keep layered edits in shape keys. 🧠
7. **Check with light.** Overlay the matched-camera background photo at 50%. Then light with a grazing key from high front-side: the anterior masseter edge, buccal hollow, temporal ridge and temple concavity should line up with the photo's shading.

**What goes wrong:**
- Widening the mandible to match a muscular frontal face, which gives the wrong profile angle.
- Mixing clenched with relaxed photos, or pre- with post-toxin photos.
- Reading parotid swelling or fat as muscle. 🧠
- Using East-Asian or young-female norms for everyone.
- Symmetrising a one-sided post-surgical temple hollow [23][45].
- Treating 68-point or MediaPipe jaw points as gonion. They slide along the visible contour as the head turns. 🧠

## Algorithmic implications

- **Layered model.** Move each vertex along the bone-surface normal: x′ = x + n·[t_m(1+κc)·w_m + s_f·w_f + p·w_p + d_t·w_t], with smooth weights w. Fit the skeleton separately: bony bigonial width, θ_go, ramus height and SN-MP. A single "jaw width" blendshape cannot reproduce how bone and muscle differ across views. Weight maps could be learned from automatic MRI/CT segmentations, for which 2011 overlaps were 82.6% for masseter and 78.8% for temporalis [43], or from deep-learning and 3D Slicer pipelines [20][33].
- **Masseter prior (relaxed, per side).** The Korean sample gives F N(9.8, 1.3²) and M N(11.3, 1.2²) mm [2]. Cross-population defaults are F N(10, 1.8²), M N(12, 2.0²) and unknown-sex N(11, 2.3²). The widening is a design choice anchored on [1][3]. Bounds 6-20 mm.
- **Clench.** c ∈ [0, 1] is set per image: Beta(1, 4) for rest captures, fixed at 1 for the clench shot. κ ~ N(0.28, 0.05²), clipped to 0.15-0.40 [2] (derived). Temporalis κ_t ≈ 0-0.1 [18][29].
- **Couplings.** Shift the mean of t_m by about −0.14 mm per degree of SN-MP above 32°. This is derived from the 1.4 mm step between groups about 10° apart [3] 🧠. Use weak covariances with θ_go (negative) and ramus height (positive) [5], ρ ≈ 0.2-0.3 and tunable 🧠.
- **Skeleton and fat.** θ_go ~ N(122°, 7²), bounds 105-145°. Bony go-go M N(97, 6²) mm, F N(90, 5²) mm. s_f ~ N(6, 2.5²) mm, scaled by BMI. Temple offset d_t ~ N(13, 3²) mm, shrinking about 0.6%/yr (M) and 1%/yr (F) after 30, derived from [21] 🧠.
- **Symmetry.** t_m,L − t_m,R ~ Laplace(0, 0.8 mm). Allow large one-sided temple deficits only when the user flags them. 🧠
- **Identifiability.** The frontal silhouette gives one equation, W_sil = W_bone + 2(t_m + s_f) + parotid. Profile pins θ_go and the ramus. The far-side 3/4 silhouette pins the belly. A clench pair gives Δ = κ·t_m directly. Use occluding-contour residuals with correspondences recomputed every iteration ("landmark marching"), never fixed jaw landmarks. Report the posterior covariance of the bone/muscle split. 🧠
- **Unknown-subject defaults.** c = 0, a normodivergent skeleton, t_m = 11-12.5 mm [2][3], symmetric.

## Disagreements, myths & open questions

- **"Botox narrows the jaw" against "no width change".** Soft tissue shrinks [13][14][15][16][39], while skeletal width holds at 6 and 12 months [7][38]. One small study does find bone loss at the angle [37]. ↔️ Most of the conflict comes from comparing soft tissue with bone at different times. Bone remodelling is still open.
- **"A big masseter means a bruxer".** True for group means [9][10][36], but individual values overlap [11]. Infer neither from the other.
- **Does muscle shape the angle?** The functional-matrix view and growth data suggest it does during growth 🧠, but the adult evidence is only correlational [3][5][8]. ↔️
- **Temporalis numbers.** Ultrasound values disagree [29], and the measurement sites differ between ultrasound, MRI and CT [30][32]. Do not transfer values between methods.
- **"Chewing gum sharpens the jawline".** A thicker masseter *widens* the lower face. Jawline definition depends mostly on submandibular and jowl fat and on bone. 🧠
- **Possible inconsistency in [21].** The reported declines give about 22.9 mL (M) against 11.2 mL (F) in the oldest group, which is not obviously "convergence". Check the full text.
- **Dropped as unconfirmed.** The 9.17 ± 0.40 against 10.38 ± 0.27 mm values in [12] (group unknown), and the craniotomy ratios and Thai volumes once attributed to [23].
- **Open questions.**
  - Whether the [21] and [24] volumes are per side.
  - Population norms by sex, age and ethnicity measured with one protocol.
  - Soft-tissue depth over the jaw and temple by BMI.
  - 3D stereophotogrammetry that separates muscle from bone at the jaw line; [46] uses the method for TMD.
  - The roles of the parotid and jowl, and of mandibular aging (Shaw) 🧠.
