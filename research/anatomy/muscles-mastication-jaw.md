# Masseter, temporalis and the jaw line

> Lower-face width, the apparent jaw angle and temple fullness depend on two chewing muscles laid over bone: the masseter on the mandibular ramus and the temporalis in the temporal fossa, each covered by fat, fascia and (for the masseter) the parotid gland. By ultrasound, relaxed adult masseter thickness is roughly 10-15 mm per side and rises about 27-30% on clenching. It is thicker in men, in low-angle (short) faces and, on average, in bruxers. The adult temporalis is larger in men and shrinks with age. Botulinum toxin cuts masseter bulk by about 25-30% for several months, but whether it also changes bone is contested. For photo-to-3D work the central problem is identifiability: a frontal photo confounds bone width, muscle, gland and fat, so a fitter needs separate parameters for each, a per-photo clench state, and profile or 3/4 contour evidence to tell them apart.

## Key facts

**Anatomy that drives the surface**
- **Masseter.** The superficial part runs from the zygomatic process of the maxilla and the anterior two-thirds of the lower border of the zygomatic arch, down and back to the angle and the lower lateral ramus. The deep part runs from the posterior and deep arch, more vertically, to the upper ramus. The anterior border runs from under the zygoma body to the lower border of the mandible just behind the facial-artery (antegonial) notch, where the facial pulse can be felt. 🧠
- **What covers the masseter.** The parotid gland sits over its posterior third, from in front of the tragus down to the angle. The parotid duct crosses the muscle about a finger's breadth below the arch and turns inward at the anterior border. The buccal fat pad and buccal hollow lie in front of the anterior border. Platysma covers the lowest part. Masseteric cutaneous ligaments run along the anterior border, and the jowl forms just in front of them, above the mandibular border. 🧠
- **Temporalis.** It arises from the floor of the temporal fossa up to the *inferior* temporal line and from the deep surface of the deep temporal fascia. That fascia attaches to the *superior* temporal line. The fibres converge into a tendon that passes deep to the zygomatic arch and inserts on the coronoid process and anterior ramus. Anterior fibres run nearly vertically and posterior fibres nearly horizontally. 🧠 One harvested summary loosely says the muscle "originates" at the superior temporal line [44]; the textbook origin is the inferior line. 🧠
- **The temple is not just muscle.** The layers from the surface inward are skin, subcutaneous fat, temporoparietal fascia (continuous with the SMAS), the deep temporal fascia with the superficial temporal fat pad inside it, the deep temporal fat pad (part of the buccal fat system), temporalis, then bone [44] 🧠. Temple hollowing has been linked both to temporalis atrophy or retraction [45] and to the fat compartments [44]. ✅ The upper boundary of the temple (the temporal line) shows as a visible ridge between the forehead and temple planes in lean or older faces. 🧠

**Masseter size and what predicts it**
- **Thickness.** A systematic review gives male relaxed thickness as 10-15 mm and contracted thickness as 14-19 mm, with means across studies of 12.0-17.2 mm. It gives no universal range, because probe, measurement site, sex and population all shift the values [1]. The primary studies agree with this range [2][3]. ✅
- **Sex.** Men have thicker masseters in both relaxed and contracted states [2][18][48]. ✅ In young Korean adults, relaxed thickness was 9.8 ± 1.3 mm in women and 11.3 ± 1.2 mm in men, and clenched thickness was 12.4 ± 1.4 and 14.7 ± 1.4 mm [2]. 📄 That is +27% and +30% on clenching (derived). 📄
- **Vertical facial pattern.** Mean relaxed thickness was 13.94 mm in hypodivergent (low-angle, short-face) subjects, 12.53 mm in normodivergent and 11.13 mm in hyperdivergent (long-face) subjects [3]. 📄 The direction is confirmed elsewhere [4][5][8]. ✅ Masseter thickness correlates positively with bigonial and bizygomatic width and negatively with anterior face height and mandibular length (Raadsheer, cited secondhand) [8]. 📄 It also correlates inversely with gonial angle and positively with ramus height [5]. 📄 It is greater in men and with normal occlusion, and smaller with more severe malocclusion [49]. 📄 All of this evidence is cross-sectional.
- **Volume.** In the Bunkyo older-adult MRI cohort, mean volume was about 35 mL in men and 25 mL in women, and low volume tracked sarcopenia and osteoporosis risk [24][25]. 📄 These figures come from a search summary, the two papers share one cohort, and it is unclear whether the volumes are per side.
- **Growth and age.** Masseter volume rose by about 199% from age 0 to 17, with a left-side bias [26]. 📄 Relaxed thickness was lower at 55+ than at 18-35 [50]. 📄 Masticatory muscle declines with age [21][50]. ✅
- **The muscle adapts to the skeleton.** After orthognathic correction of asymmetry, masseter volume and cross-sectional area on the non-deviated side fell by 13.5% and 16.4% [27], and volume also changes after mandibular setback [28]. ✅ Masseter asymmetry therefore usually goes with skeletal asymmetry. 🧠
- **Bony signature of a strong masseter.** Strong masseters tend to go with a laterally flared, everted gonial angle and a roughened masseteric tuberosity. 🧠

**Bruxism, hypertrophy and botulinum toxin**
- **Bruxism.** Chronic clenching is associated with larger masseters, and sometimes larger temporalis muscles, and with a "square" lower face [9][10][34][36]. ✅ All patients in one severe-bruxism cohort had hypertrophy [36]. 📄 Individual values overlap heavily with controls, and one TMD-myalgia sample showed no difference (12.91 against 13.07 mm, summary) [11]. ↔️
- **"Square face" is bone plus muscle.** A wide lower third is attributed mainly to mandibular angle morphology plus masseter hypertrophy [6][34]. ✅ Benign masseteric hypertrophy can be one-sided. 🧠 Parotid enlargement (from alcohol, bulimia, Sjögren's and other causes) mimics it, but unlike the masseter it does not harden on clenching. 🧠
- **Botulinum toxin reduces bulk.** Masseter thickness and volume and lower-face width fall after injection [6][13][14][15][16][39][47]. ✅ The reported size of the effect is about −25% in thickness [6] and about −30% in volume on CT [15]. 📄 A meta-analysis of 5 RCTs (536 participants) found short-term benefit with high heterogeneity [39]. An earlier systematic review found no trials at all [40]. 📄
- **Time course.** Onset is at about 2 weeks and the effect peaks around 3 months [15][41]. ✅ Duration is reported as recovery by about 6 months [15] against 6-12 months [41]. ↔️ Ultrasound shows the lowest thickness at 1 month while CT thinning continues to 3 months [14]. 📄
- **Bone effects.** A CBCT study (n = 20) found bone-volume loss at the angle after repeated injections [37]. A 12-month placebo-controlled CT trial (150 against 37) found no change in bigonial width, cortical thickness, flare or gonial angle [38]. A 6-month study (n = 10) also found no change in bigonial width or angle [7]. ↔️
- **Function and side effects.** Repeated low-dose injections reduced contraction and masticatory performance [6]. 📄 Reported side effects include masticatory fatigue, a limited or asymmetric smile (possibly from risorius spread) [42]. 📄 There is no consensus dose, and the use is off-label in most jurisdictions [34][42]. ✅ Surgical debulking and angle reduction also exist [8][35]. ✅

**Temporalis size**
- **Sex.** Adults: larger in men [18][20][21][30]. ✅ The size of the difference ranges from about 32% (peak ratio, derived from [21]) to about 40% [20]. ↔️ In children aged 5-15 there is no sex effect, though volume grows with age [19]. 📄
- **Age and body size.** In Taiwanese adults, male volume peaked at 36.9 mL at age 20-29 and fell 38% by the oldest group. Female volume peaked at 27.9 mL at 30-39 and fell 60% [21]. 📄 Thickness tracks body weight and BMI, and men have higher values except over age 70 [30]. 📄
- **Thickness depends on method.** Healthy-adult ultrasound gives 8.8 ± 1.1 mm at rest and 9.8 ± 1.2 mm contracted [29]. Other cohorts give about 6.4/7.6 mm and 8.1/9.8 mm [29]. ↔️ A Japanese MRI cohort aged 35-84 (n = 360) ranged from 3.69 to 16.90 mm [30]. 📄 CT and MRI agree closely (ICC 0.894), but MRI landmarking protocols differ [32]. 📄 Temporal muscle thickness is used as a sarcopenia marker [31][32]. ✅
- **Clenching and regional differences.** Upright, the temporalis showed no significant thickening on clenching, unlike the masseter. The lower temporalis differs by sex but the upper does not [18]. 📄
- **Variation and surgery.** Some people have only the deep layer [22]. 📄 Temporal hollowing after pterional craniotomy is common. One summary gives 87-100% [23], but incidence depends on technique and on how hollowing is defined 🧠. ↔️ Repair options include fat, fillers, PEEK implants and flaps, and none is standard [45]. 📄

**Visible in views** 🧠
- **Frontal view.** The masseter sets the lower-face outline below the ear lobe. Hypertrophy turns an oval or tapered lower face into a square or "inverted trapezoid" with a soft convexity behind the buccal hollow.
- **3/4 view.** The anterior border shows as a soft vertical edge, with the buccal hollow in front of it. On clenching the edge sharpens and the belly bulges.
- **Profile.** The angle silhouette comes mostly from bone. A big masseter rounds the angle and adds fullness above and behind it, but barely moves the posterior ramus line.
- **Temples.** A clench "flickers" the temple in lean subjects. The skin is more often concave in older, lean or female faces.

## Applying this to photo→3D face sculpting in Blender

1. **Capture protocol (consenting subject).** Take frontal, both 3/4 views (yaw 30-50°) and both profiles at 1.5 m or more, using an 85-135 mm full-frame-equivalent lens. Use a rest pose of "lips together, teeth apart". Then take a deliberate *clenched* frontal and 3/4 pair from the same camera positions. The rest-to-clench difference isolates the masseter, showing its anterior border and belly bulge, and any temple flicker shows the temporalis. Record any jaw-slimming injections and their dates: bulk changes by 25-30% across months [6][15]. Tag photos from different treatment windows and do not mix them.
2. **Correct perspective before judging width.** In a 40 cm selfie the jaw plane is shrunk about 20% relative to the nose. Use only camera-solved views for width, never eyeballed ones. 🧠
3. **Profile first, for bone.** Construct soft-tissue gonion from the tangent to the posterior ramus contour (below the ear lobe) and the tangent to the lower border, and measure the angle between them. Then block in the mandible: ramus height, gonial angle (Table 5), and the depth of the antegonial notch. A long, steep-plane face with a big flaring jaw is atypical [3][4][5], so check for clenching, parotid or fat before accepting it.
4. **3/4 views, for the muscle.** Trace the anterior masseter border as a Bezier guide curve from under the zygoma body to the antegonial notch. Fit the far-side jaw silhouette, which passes over the masseter belly.
5. **Frontal view last.** The silhouette width at mouth-corner to gonion level is bone plus 2 × (masseter + fat), plus the parotid posteriorly. Whatever the profile-fitted bone does not explain goes to the muscle and fat layers.
6. **Build it in layers in metric units.** Set the scene to Metric with unit scale 1.0, so 3 mm is 0.003 m. Use these vertex groups on the template: `masseter_L/R` (peak over the lower-middle ramus, fading to zero at the anterior border, the arch and just under the angle), `temporalis_L/R` (bounded by the temporal line, the lateral orbital rim and the arch), `parotid_L/R`, `buccal_hollow_L/R` and `jowl_L/R`. Drive each layer with shape keys or a Displace modifier set to Normal direction with a vertex group, and drive those from custom properties held in millimetres (`t_m_L`, `clench`, `temple_fill_L`). Multires has no native sculpt layers, so keep layer edits in shape keys. 🧠
7. **Check with light.** Match the camera to the photo with the background image at 50% opacity. Then use a grazing key light from high front-side: the anterior masseter edge, the buccal hollow, the temporal ridge and the temple concavity should line up with the shading in the photo.

**What goes wrong**
- Widening the mandible to match a muscular frontal face. The profile angle comes out wrong, and the relaxed face looks wrong too.
- Mixing clenched and relaxed photos, or photos from before and after toxin.
- Reading parotid swelling or fat as muscle. Clenching hardens and lifts only the masseter. 🧠
- Using East-Asian or young-female norms for everyone. Widen the prior.
- Making the temple symmetrical when it is not. A deep one-sided hollow may be post-surgical [23][45].
- Treating 68-point or MediaPipe jaw landmarks as gonion. They slide along the visible contour as the head turns. 🧠

## Algorithmic implications

- **Layered parameterisation.** Each template vertex moves along the bone-surface normal: x′ = x + n·[t_m(1 + κc)·w_m + s_f·w_f + p·w_p + d_t·w_t]. Here w are smooth vertex weights; t_m is masseter thickness, s_f the fat offset, p the parotid offset and d_t the temple offset. The skeleton is fitted separately: bony bigonial width, gonial angle θ_go, ramus height and mandibular plane angle. A single "jaw width" blendshape cannot reproduce how bone and muscle differ in profile and 3/4. Masseter cross-sections on MRI or CT can be segmented automatically (≈ 82.6% masseter, 78.8% temporalis overlap with ground truth in 2011 [43]), and temporal thickness can be measured by deep learning [33] or semi-automatically in 3D Slicer [20]. These could build data-driven weight maps. 🧠
- **Masseter prior (relaxed, per side).** Korean-sample values are F N(9.8, 1.3²) and M N(11.3, 1.2²) mm [2]. Cross-population defaults are F N(10, 1.8²), M N(12, 2.0²), and N(11, 2.3²) when sex is unknown. The widening is a design choice anchored on [1][3]. Hard bounds are 6-20 mm.
- **Clench.** c ∈ [0, 1] per image, with prior Beta(1, 4) for rest-pose captures and fixed at 1 for the deliberate clench shot. Clench gain κ ~ N(0.28, 0.05²), clipped to 0.15-0.40 [2] (derived). The temporalis term gets κ_t ≈ 0 to 0.1 [18][29].
- **Couplings.** Shift the mean of t_m by about −0.14 mm per degree of SN-MP above 32°. This is derived from the roughly 1.4 mm step between groups about 10° apart [3] and is 🧠 (derived). Add a weak negative covariance with θ_go and a positive one with ramus height [5], ρ ≈ −0.2 to −0.3 and tunable 🧠.
- **Skeleton priors.** θ_go ~ N(122°, 7²) with bounds of 105-145°. Bony bigonial width M N(97, 6²) mm, F N(90, 5²) mm. 🧠 (verify)
- **Fat and temple.** s_f ~ N(6, 2.5²) mm, scaled with BMI. d_t (total temple soft tissue) ~ N(13, 3²) mm. Shrink d_t by about 0.6%/yr after 30 in men and about 1%/yr in women, derived from [21] (Taiwanese data; 🧠 extrapolation). Thickness rises with weight [30].
- **Symmetry.** t_m,L − t_m,R ~ Laplace(0, 0.8 mm). Allow larger one-sided temple deficits only when the user flags surgery or other history. 🧠
- **Identifiability.** The frontal silhouette gives a single equation, W_sil = W_bone + 2(t_m + s_f) + parotid. Profile constrains θ_go and the ramus, which have thin soft-tissue cover. The far-side 3/4 silhouette constrains the masseter belly. A clench pair gives Δ = κ·t_m directly, so t_m ≈ Δ/κ. Use occluding-contour residuals with correspondences recomputed every iteration ("landmark marching"), not fixed jaw landmarks. Report the posterior covariance of the bone/muscle split, not just a point estimate.
- **Defaults when nothing is known.** Rest pose, c = 0, a normodivergent skeleton, t_m = 11-12.5 mm [2][3], symmetric.

## Disagreements, myths & open questions

- **"Botox narrows the jaw" against "no width change".** Soft tissue shrinks for months [13][14][15][16][39]. Skeletal width is unchanged at 6 and 12 months [7][38], but one small study reports bone loss at the angle [37]. ↔️ Most of the apparent conflict is soft tissue against bone, plus timing. Bone remodelling remains open.
- **Duration.** About 6 months [15] against 6-12 months [41]. ↔️
- **"A big masseter means a bruxer".** This holds for group means [9][10][36], but individual values overlap [11]. Do not infer either one from the other.
- **Does muscle shape the angle?** The functional-matrix view (Moss) and growth data favour some muscle influence on gonial form during growth 🧠, but adult evidence is only correlational [3][5][8]. ↔️
- **Temporalis thickness.** Ultrasound values disagree (6.4-8.8 mm at rest) [29], and MRI, CT and ultrasound use different sites [30][32]. Values cannot be transferred between methods. ↔️
- **Temporalis origin.** The muscle originates at the inferior temporal line, while the fascia attaches at the superior line 🧠, despite a looser harvested phrasing [44].
- **"Chewing gum sharpens the jawline".** Training can thicken the masseter, which *widens* the lower face. A defined jawline depends mostly on submandibular and jowl fat and on bone. 🧠
- **Possible inconsistency in [21].** The reported declines give about 22.9 mL (men) against 11.2 mL (women) at the oldest ages, which does not look like "convergence". It may refer to rates; check the full text.
- **Dropped as unconfirmed.** The 9.17 ± 0.40 against 10.38 ± 0.27 mm values in [12] (group assignment unknown), and the post-craniotomy thickness ratios and Thai volumes once attributed to [23].
- **Open questions.**
  - Whether the [21] and [24] volumes are per side.
  - Population norms by sex, age and ethnicity measured with one shared protocol.
  - Soft-tissue depth over the gonion, ramus and temple by BMI.
  - Correlation coefficients between masseter size and gonial angle.
  - 3D stereophotogrammetry that separates muscle from bone at the jaw line; [46] shows the method is used in TMD.
  - The roles of the parotid and jowl in the aging jaw line, including ramus shortening and angle opening with age (Shaw) 🧠.
