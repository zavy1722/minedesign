# SMAS and facial retaining ligaments

> Facial soft tissue is a five-layer laminate: skin, subcutaneous fat divided into compartments, the superficial musculoaponeurotic system (SMAS, continuous with platysma below and temporoparietal fascia above), a sub-SMAS plane of glide spaces, and deep fascia or periosteum. Retaining ligaments cross the laminate from bone or deep fascia to the dermis along the edges of the glide spaces, giving the face fixed lines (orbital rim, zygomatic body, anterior masseter border, anterior mandible) with mobile masses between them. Tear trough, lid–cheek junction, midcheek groove, jowl and prejowl sulcus appear where a tethered line meets a mass that bulges, sags or deflates. The nasolabial and marionette folds are partly muscle-insertion and fat-boundary folds, not pure ligament grooves. A fitter should therefore treat ligament lines as low-variance anchors and the spaces between them as high-variance regions that respond to gravity, expression and age.

## Key facts

**Layers**
- From the surface: (1) skin; (2) subcutaneous fat, split into compartments by septa; (3) the musculoaponeurotic layer (SMAS, continuous with platysma, temporoparietal fascia and galea/frontalis); (4) a loose plane holding the glide spaces, the deep fat, the ligaments' passage and the facial-nerve branches; (5) deep fascia or periosteum 🧠 (Mendelson's layered model; Stuzin 1992).
- The SMAS (Mitz and Peyronie 1976) is a fibromuscular sheet that links the mimetic muscles and carries their pull to the skin through septa 🧠. Ghassemi's type 1, lateral to the nasolabial fold, is septa around fat lobules adherent to parotid fascia; type 2, in the fold and lip, is a dense collagen–elastin–muscle mesh inserting into dermis 🧠. It is classically described as thick and fixed over the parotid and thin and fragmented over the anterior cheek, but one cadaver CT/MRI study found the opposite gradient (0.76 ± 0.43 mm near the parotid, 1.69 ± 0.52 mm over the cheek, 2.41 ± 0.05 mm at the nasolabial fold) [26] ↔️; continuation medial to the fold is also ↔️.
- Temple: the temporoparietal fascia is the SMAS of the temple. Beneath it lie the deep temporal fascia around the superficial temporal fat pad, then the deep temporal fat pad between the deep fascia and temporalis, which sits in its own fascial envelope [20] 📄.
- Mimetic muscle depth (Freilinger 1987), superficial to deep: (1) DAO, zygomaticus minor, orbicularis oculi; (2) DLI, risorius, platysma, zygomaticus major, LLSAN; (3) orbicularis oris, LLS; (4) mentalis, levator anguli oris, buccinator. Nerves enter layers 1–3 from their deep surface 🧠.
- Platysma, the lower-face SMAS, covers the lower third of masseter; its *pars labialis* crosses the mandible into the lower-lip dermis [4] 📄.
- The orbital part of orbicularis oculi spreads beyond the rim onto cheek and temple; the palpebral part is preseptal plus pretarsal [14] 📄. The tear trough lies where the two parts meet 🧠.

**Ligament classes**
- True (osteocutaneous) ligaments run from periosteum to dermis: zygomatic (McGregor's patch), mandibular, orbicularis retaining (ORL) and tear trough. The upper face has similar adhesions: the temporal ligamentous adhesion, the temporal septa and lateral orbital thickening. False (fasciocutaneous) ligaments run from deep fascia to dermis: masseteric cutaneous, parotid-cutaneous and platysma-auricular 🧠 (Furnas 1989; Stuzin 1992).
- Ligaments fan out through fat as retinacula cutis and line the glide spaces (upper temporal, prezygomatic, premasseter). Together they form a roughly vertical line between the fixed lateral face (parotid, masseter, temporal fascia) and the mobile anterior face, where expression and ageing act 🧠.

**Periorbit**
- The ORL (orbitomalar ligament, Kikkawa 1996) arises just outside the orbital rim and inserts through orbicularis at the lid–cheek junction. It is short and tight medially and longer and laxer laterally, so the lateral junction descends more and malar bags form there 🧠.
- The tear trough ligament (Wong, Hsieh and Mendelson 2012) is a true osteocutaneous ligament arising from the maxilla at the medial infraorbital rim, between the palpebral and orbital parts of orbicularis, and continuous laterally with the ORL [24] 📄. Its exact span (about the medial canthus to the mid-pupil) was not confirmed in verification 🧠. The skin over it has almost no fat 🧠.
- The infraorbital rim is maxilla medially and zygoma laterally [15][22] ✅. The infraorbital foramen lies ≈5–8.5 mm below the rim [22][23] ✅, a bony check below the ligament line.
- The prezygomatic space is a triangle over the zygomatic body. Orbital orbicularis is its roof and SOOF its floor; the ORL bounds it above and the zygomatic ligaments below. Oedema or fat collecting there, plus ORL laxity, gives the malar mound or festoon 🧠 (Mendelson 2002).
- The lid–cheek "Y" has three limbs: the tear trough (tear trough ligament), the palpebromalar groove (ORL), and the midcheek groove (the "Indian band"), which runs inferolaterally along the zygomaticocutaneous septum between medial and middle cheek fat 🧠 (Haddock 2009; Mendelson and Jacobson 2008).

**Midface**
- The zygomatic ligaments are strongest at the junction of the zygomatic body and arch, beside the origins of zygomaticus major and minor, with the zygomatic nerve branches just below. They pin the malar skin and form the lower edge of the malar mound 🧠. The main ligament is often quoted as ≈4.5 cm in front of the tragus ↔️ (unverified; a 2025-26 search found no tragus-referenced measurement, and a review says the patch's extent is not definitively described [29]).
- The nasolabial fold (NLF) is a 3D curvature at the medial edge of the malar fat, made by the cutaneous insertions of the lip elevators and zygomaticus major [9] 📄. Which muscle dominates is disputed: levator labii and zygomaticus minor [10] vs zygomaticus major plus the elevators [9]. It is also distinct from nasolabial wrinkles [11] ↔️. It is a fat-boundary fold, not an osteocutaneous ligament line 🧠.
- Superficial fat compartments (Rohrich and Pessa 2007): nasolabial; medial, middle and lateral-temporal cheek; three orbital; forehead; jowl. Deep: medial/lateral SOOF, deep medial cheek, buccal, ROOF. Septa between them show as lines when neighbouring volumes change unevenly 🧠.
- Loss of the deep medial cheek fat removes support under both the tear trough and the NLF, deepening them without any ligament moving 🧠 (Rohrich, Pessa and Ristow 2008; Gierloff 2012).

**Lower face**
- Masseteric cutaneous ligaments run along the anterior masseter border. The upper ones are strong and merge with the zygomatic ligaments; the lower ones weaken with age, and the jowl forms in front of them over the premasseter space, a glide plane under platysma/SMAS 🧠 (Mendelson 2008).
- Jowling reflects loose platysma adherence to the mandible behind the mandibular ligament, not in front of it [3] 📄. The jowl is a local excess of skin and subcutaneous fat [1] 📄 ([1] and [2] are probably one paper), bounded below by a "mandibular septum" 🧠 (Reece 2008).
- The mandibular ligament is classically a true ligament on the anterior third of the mandible, ≈1 cm above the border (Furnas); a 10-cadaver study measured an osteocutaneous mandibular ligament 9.1 ± 3.1 mm above the inferior border and 51.1 ± 4.5 mm lateral to the midline [25] 📄. Minelli 2022 (49 cadaver heads) found the true ligament only below platysma, as the combined attachment of the lip depressors and platysma, with no osteocutaneous structure at the jowl's front edge; its mean extent was 63.1 mm (men) / 59.3 mm (women) from the midline, matching maximal jowl fullness rather than the front edge [1] ↔️. The prejowl sulcus lies there in either case and deepens with anterior mandibular bone loss 🧠.
- Depressor pull (DAO, platysma, buccinator) accentuates the marionette fold with age [5] 📄. The modiolus moves down and inward with age (ultrasound, 45 young vs 45 older Asian adults) [6] 📄. 2025 cadaver studies describe a "ligament of the modiolus" [8] and a modiolus-to-dermis "false ligament" [7] ↔️ (small studies).
- The parotid-cutaneous and platysma-auricular ligaments fix the preauricular skin, so the lateral face barely moves 🧠.

**Ageing and thickness**
- Four ageing models compete: (a) ligament laxity and descent over the spaces (Furnas, Stuzin, Mendelson); (b) fat deflation and redistribution (Rohrich and Pessa; Lambros 2007, whose serial photographs found lid–cheek junction height fairly stable); (c) skeletal remodelling: orbital aperture widening, maxillary and pyriform retrusion, mandibular resorption (Shaw and Kahn 2007; Mendelson and Wong 2012); (d) muscle shortening and tone [12][13] ↔️. All contribute; the weights are disputed.
- The lateral brow falls first: it lies outside the temporal adhesion, where frontalis gives no lift 🧠 (Knize 1996).
- Cheek skin is ≈1–2 mm thick: mid-cheek dermis 1.88 mm in men vs 1.58 mm in women [16], and high-frequency ultrasound medians of 1.1–1.6 mm across facial regions [17] ✅.
- A CT study of 1174 Chinese adults (18–90 years, 33 landmarks) separated skin, fat and muscle layers; sex and age significantly changed the fat and muscle layers [18] 📄. Its values have not yet been extracted for a layered prior.
- Posture changes measured soft-tissue thickness, and supine or cadaver values are distorted [19] 📄. The tissue that shifts is the tissue over the spaces 🧠.

## Applying this to photo→3D face sculpting in Blender

1. **Capture.** Upright, Frankfort horizontal, relaxed (chin-down compresses the jowl, chin-up stretches it). Add one broad smile: the cheek piles against the ORL and NLF and reveals the fixed lines. Shoot 3/4 and a slightly low profile so NLF, malar mound and jowl reach the silhouette. Avoid top light. Ask about fillers, threads, lifts and weight change 🧠.
2. **Annotate.** On every view, draw polylines for the tear trough, palpebromalar, midcheek, NLF, marionette, prejowl, masseteric (anterior masseter border) and temporal lines. A real tether stays put across views and in the smile; a lighting shadow moves 🧠.
3. **Topology and Face Sets.** Run edge loops along the ORL ring, the NLF, the marionette–jaw line and the masseteric line, with a vertex row at the mandibular ligament. Make one Face Set per fat compartment and per ligament strip so Mask by Face Set isolates fixed vs mobile tissue 🧠.
4. **Sculpt order.** Bone → ligament anchors (bone + thin cover) → compartment volumes → grooves, which should emerge where anchored tissue meets bulging tissue. Use Crease only for the skin-fold component; a carved V reads as a scar 🧠.
5. **Age with anatomy.** Mask the ligament strips, move the masses inferomedially (midface) or inferiorly (jowl) with Elastic Deform or Grab, and deflate the deep medial cheek and SOOF. To de-age, refill deep compartments rather than filling grooves. Store each as a shape key so age is a slider 🧠.
6. **Expressions.** In AU6/AU12 the cheek rises and stops at the ORL (lower-lid bulge, crow's feet) and bunches against the NLF, while the lateral face stays still 🧠.
7. **Shading check.** Compare tear trough, NLF and jowl shadows under the photo's key light. Never fake a tear trough with darker albedo: pigment is a material issue 🧠.
8. **Failures:** top-lit grooves; smile frames used for identity; mirrored grooves; chin-down jowls on young subjects; a symmetric "U" tear trough (should be tight medially, lax laterally); a jowl merged with the cheek; supine CT priors on upright photos [19].

## Algorithmic implications

- **Template annotation.** Store named ligament paths (`lig_tear_trough_L/R`, `lig_orl`, `lig_zygomatic`, `lig_masseteric`, `lig_mandibular`, `lig_temporal`) and fold curves (`fold_nlf`, `fold_marionette`). Origins are bony, so define them on the bone layer so skeletal parameters carry them; the insertion is bone + local thickness along the normal 🧠.
- **Variance by tether.** In the two-layer model skin = bone + t(x)·n̂, set σ_t(x) = σ_lig + (σ_space − σ_lig)·m(x), with σ_lig ≈ 1.0 mm and σ_space ≈ 3.5 mm (heuristics to calibrate) 🧠.
- **Mobility field m.** Solve a Laplace equation with the existing cotan Laplacian: Dirichlet m = 0 on the ligament strips, the lateral face and the bony midline, and m = 1 at the centroids of the prezygomatic, jowl, nasolabial and malar compartments 🧠.
- **Stiffness.** `laplacian_deform` takes per-constraint weights but uses uniform smoothness. Add per-vertex row weights (W^½L): ligament strips ×3–5, spaces ×0.5–0.7, lips ×0.3. Make strips 3–6 mm wide (1–2 rings at 2–3 mm edges) 🧠.
- **Age as its own basis.** `laxity` ∈ [0, 1] gives d(x) = laxity·D·m(x)·ĝ(x), with ĝ inferomedial in the midface and inferior at the jowl and D ≈ 5 mm (uncalibrated). Add `deep_cheek_volume` (±3 mm along the normal) and `orbital_fat_prolapse`; with known age use laxity ~ N(f(age), 0.2). Jowls and troughs then cost no identity modes 🧠.
- **Placement priors** (head frame mm, +x = subject's left, +y = up; all 🧠 with wide σ):
  - Tear trough: medial end 3–6 mm below endocanthion (σ 2); lateral end at pupil x, 14–18 mm below the pupil centre (σ 3).
  - Lid–cheek junction: 10–13 mm below the lower-lid margin in young adults, increasing with age ↔️ (Lambros; the direction is disputed [27][28], the 10–13 mm figure unconfirmed).
  - Mandibular ligament: arc fraction 0.25 ± 0.07 from menton to gonion, ≈10 mm above the border.
  - Masseteric line meets the jaw at arc fraction 0.6–0.75 (facial-artery notch).
  - NLF: from 2–5 mm lateral of the alar-facial groove to 8–15 mm lateral of cheilion.
  - Zygomatic ligament: place it on the fitted bone.
- **Fold features.** Match luminance valleys (Hessian/ridge filter) to projected fold curves by chamfer distance, at low weight and never in top-lit views. A fold on a profile or 3/4 occluding contour is geometry; frontal shading is not 🧠.
- **Grades.** Expose `tear_trough_grade` 0–3 and `nlf_grade` / `marionette_grade` 0–4 (photonumeric-scale style). Calibrate depth per grade on scans; ≈1 mm per grade is only a placeholder 🧠.
- **Gating and symmetry.** Down-weight identity frames with AU6/AU12 above trace. Fit the two sides independently with a soft symmetry prior (σ ≈ 1.5 mm on groove positions) 🧠.
- **Domain shift.** CT and cadaver priors are supine [19]: down-weight them where m > 0.5 or apply an upright correction 🧠.

## Disagreements, myths & open questions

- **Is the SMAS a discrete layer?** Mitz–Peyronie and Ghassemi say yes, with regional types. Jost and Levet (1984) read the parotid "SMAS" as parotid fascia, and Gardetto's 2003 plastination study questioned a discrete sheet ↔️. Model it as a layer with regionally varying stiffness.
- **Mandibular ligament:** osteocutaneous (Furnas) vs sub-platysmal with no anterior jowl boundary [1] ↔️; [3] puts jowling behind it.
- **NLF cause:** [9] vs [10] vs [11] ↔️. Myth: the NLF is a ligament line or a wrinkle. It is a boundary fold, present from youth, that deepens with age.
- **Myth: ageing is gravity.** Photo series, deep-fat CT and skeletal CT support deflation and bone change alongside descent ↔️.
- **Myth: carve the grooves.** Volume on each side of the tether matters more than the cut.
- **Recalled numbers to check:** zygomatic ligament ≈4.5 cm from tragus; youthful lower-lid height ≈11–12 mm. (SMAS thickness checked in verification: ≈0.6–2.4 mm, gradient disputed [26].)
- **Open:** regional SMAS thickness from MRI/ultrasound; any population dataset of 3D ligament positions (none known); the layer values in [18]; whether [7] and [8] describe one structure; the role of muscle tone [12][13]; the deep-fascia review [21] (surfaced, not read).
