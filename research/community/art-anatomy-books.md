# Classic art-anatomy references for heads

> The classic head references fall into three families. Construction canons (Loomis, the Reilly method, Bridgman) give a fast, idealized scaffold. Plane models (the Asaro head, Reilly's rhythm lines) explain how form turns into light. Anatomical atlases (Peck, Goldfinger, Scott Spencer, Uldis Zarins) cover the skull, muscle and fat that produce those forms, and Zarins and Faigin add expression. The canons describe one idealized adult head with no variance, and most real faces fail most of them [5][7][8]. For photo→3D likeness, use the books three ways: as a block-in initializer, as the vocabulary and region map for checks and UI, and as weak priors only where the photos cannot see. Never use them as targets.

## Key facts

**Loomis (construction canon)**
- The cranium is a ball with a centerline. Where the centerline crosses the brow line is the key construction point [4] 📄. The sides of the ball are sliced flat (the side planes), and the face hangs below the ball 🧠
- The eye line sits at half the total head height, which is one quarter of the way down the middle third [1] 📄. One textbook puts it through the lid bottoms instead, and Proko notes the conflict [1] ↔️
- The mouth line sits in the top third of the lower third [2] 📄. The head is about five eye-widths across, with one eye-width between the eyes. The ears sit in the middle third [4] 📄
- Thirds and halves compress with perspective and viewing angle [1] 📄. Individual heads depart from the average [4] 📄
- In the "ideal" adult, the head is 3½ units tall: crown→hairline ½, then hairline→brow, brow→nose base and nose base→chin at 1 each. This puts the eye line exactly at half height, consistent with [1] 🧠
- *Drawing the Head and Hands* (1956) is the head book. *Figure Drawing for All It's Worth* (1943) and *Fun with a Pencil* (1939) round out the set, and Titan Books reprints them in facsimile 🧠. Proko lessons teach Loomis construction [1][2] 📄, plus Asaro and Reilly material 🧠

**Reilly method**
- Frank J. Reilly (1906–1967) taught at the Art Students League of New York and was himself a Bridgman student. He published no book, so the method comes down through students such as Jack Faragasso and through class notes, and versions differ 🧠 ↔️
- The "Reilly abstraction" lays named rhythm lines over a ball-and-plane head: the brow arc, the eye-socket ovals, a cheek line from the outer brow around the muzzle toward the chin, the mouth barrel and the jaw. The lines encode plane boundaries and rhythm, not measurements 🧠

**Asaro head**
- John Asaro, a Reilly student, made the planar head now sold as a cast teaching tool in several variants (male, female and others). Its flat facets make every value change under a single light read as a plane change 🧠 ↔️
- The facet layout is an artistic simplification, not an anatomical partition, and other planar heads differ 🧠. It is a copyrighted commercial sculpture, so do not ship a copy 🧠 ↔️

**Bridgman**
- George B. Bridgman (1865–1943) taught at the Art Students League for about four decades. His books are *Constructive Anatomy* (1920), *Life Drawing* (1924), *Heads, Features and Faces* (1932) and *The Human Machine* (1939), all available as Dover reprints 🧠
- His method is wedged, interlocking blocks: a cranial box, a face wedge, and the neck driving into the skull. It is strong on mass and movement, loose on accuracy, and best read after a precise atlas 🧠

**Peck · Spencer · Zarins**
- Stephen Rogers Peck, *Atlas of Human Anatomy for the Artist* (Oxford University Press, 1951; still in print). It gives clear plates of the skull and facial muscles, proportion charts and an expression section, and is the usual "first accurate atlas" 🧠
- Scott Spencer (Gnomon instructor, film creature sculptor) wrote *ZBrush Character Creation* (2008; 2nd ed. 2011) and *ZBrush Digital Sculpting Human Anatomy* (Sybex/Wiley, 2010). He teaches inside-out head sculpting: skull landmarks, then muscles, then fat pads and skin. It is the reference closest to a Blender sculpt workflow 🧠
- Uldis Zarins and Sandis Kondrats wrote *Anatomy for Sculptors* (2014), which reduces muscle and fat to sculptable forms and is the book 3D artists cite most. Zarins's *Anatomy of Facial Expression* (≈2017, Anatomy Next/Exonicus) goes muscle by muscle: attachments, direction of pull, the skin deformation and wrinkles each muscle produces (roughly perpendicular to the pull), and how they combine into emotions. It is illustrated in a 3D-render style 🧠 ↔️ (year, co-authors and publisher need checking)

**Also widely recommended for heads**
- Goldfinger, *Human Anatomy for Artists* (1991), often called the most accurate art atlas. Faigin, *The Artist's Complete Guide to Facial Expression* (1990). Winslow, *Classic Human Anatomy* (2009). For likeness: Lanteri's modelling volumes (caliper measurement of the sitter) and Rubino, *The Portrait in Clay* (1997) 🧠

**What anthropometry says about the canons**
- The neoclassical canons fail in most measured subjects: Saudi [5], African American women [7] and Turkish adults (n = 1,050) [8] ✅
- The orbital canon (en–en = en–ex) performs best of the set, yet only 30.6% of African American women met it [7]. It was also the most validated canon in Saudi Class III adults [9] ✅
- In 168 Saudi adults the thirds were unequal (p < 0.0001) [5] 📄. Forehead height varies most between ethnic groups [10] 📄
- Farkas's norms come from 2,326 North American White subjects, so canon "averages" carry that population [18] 📄. White-based intercanthal standards miss variation by age, sex and ethnicity [17] 📄
- There is no evidence for using the golden ratio in facial planning [15]. Marquardt's phi mask fits non-European faces poorly [16] ✅

## Applying this to photo→3D face sculpting in Blender

1. **Solve the camera before judging proportions.** A ratio read off a raw photo mixes head pitch, lens and identity [1]. Arm's-length selfies inflate the nose and shrink the ears. Measure only on the fitted mesh, in a Frankfort-horizontal frame 🧠
2. **Block in like Loomis, then let the photos win.** Click vertex, glabella, nasion, subnasale, menton, tragion, opisthocranion and both zygia across the front and profile photos. Fit the cranial sphere, side planes, face plane and jaw wedge, then warp the template to the photo landmarks with canon weights near zero 🧠
3. **Report a measurement panel.** Measure en–ex, en–en, ex–ex, al–al, ch–ch, n–sn, sn–sto, sto–me, n–me, zy–zy, go–go, sa–sba and the depth from tragion to nasion. Show each as subject value, canon value and population z. A large |z| is a likeness feature: preserve it, never "correct" it 🧠
4. **Check the planes the Asaro/Reilly way.** Light the model with a single key matched to the photo's key light (Workbench with one studio light, or EEVEE with one area light). Compare where the terminator falls on the cheek's front-to-side turn, the brow, the temple and the mouth barrel. Plane errors show in shading before they show in landmark error 🧠
5. **Profile items a frontal photo cannot constrain:**
   - nasion depth
   - nasal tip projection
   - lips against the Ricketts E-line (usually a few mm behind it; varies by ethnicity and age)
   - chin projection
   - gonial angle
   - ear set-back and tilt
   - occiput

   🧠 ↔️
6. **Eyes.** The corneal apex sits about 12–20 mm in front of the lateral orbital rim (Hertel norms vary by population). The lateral canthus sits further back than the medial and slightly higher. Eyes that are too far forward or too level are a common tell 🧠
7. **Frequent failures:**
   - "Loomis-ized", averaged, symmetric faces
   - an eye line placed by rule
   - mouth corners stretched under the pupils
   - ears set between brow and nose when the subject's sit lower or are longer
   - a flat face front with no mouth barrel
   - the hairline used as an anchor
   - a smile baked into identity (it widens ch–ch and lifts the cheeks)

   🧠

## Algorithmic implications

- **Prior records.** Store each canon as `{name, landmarks_A, landmarks_B, stated_ratio, pop_mean, pop_sd, population, n, source_urls, status, weight}`. The art books supply only `stated_ratio`. `pop_mean` and `pop_sd` must come from anthropometry. The loader refuses a non-zero `weight` while `status` is `knowledge` 🧠
- **Energy.** `E_canon = Σ w_i((r_i − μ_i)/σ_i)²` with `w_i = w0·(1 − coverage_i)` and `w0 ≈ 0.05–0.1` relative to the landmark term. In a frontal-only fit the depth priors (profile square, nasal projection, ear set-back) do the work. Never apply canons as hard constraints 🧠
- **σ sizing.** Linear facial dimensions have a coefficient of variation of about 4–8% [11][12][13]. For a ratio, CV ≈ √(c₁² + c₂²), less a correlation term, which gives σ ≈ 8–12% of μ (Table 3). Use 0.02 for the adult normalized eye line. Widen σ by about 1.5× when sex or population is unknown 🧠
- **Gating.** Turn off the vertical canons for children. Tag every prior with its population. Never give trichion any weight 🧠
- **Loomis initializer.** Parameters are cranial radius R, side-plane offset (≈0.7–0.8 R), brow height, unit length, jaw width, chin projection and pitch, solved from 6–8 clicks. Hand off to template warping or a PCA shape model, which captures the co-variation canons ignore and should replace them where available 🧠
- **Plane regions.** Author your own ~20–30 named vertex groups (forehead front and sides, temple, brow ridge, orbit, lids, infraorbital, zygomatic front and side, nasal dorsum, sides, tip and alae, maxillary barrel, lips, mental, mandibular side and ramus). Use them for per-region regularization, single-light shading residuals and named sculpt masks. Do not copy the Asaro facets 🧠
- **Expression layer.** Map muscle → FACS AU → shape key, following the Zarins and Faigin tables. Hold all AUs at 0 during the identity fit. Flag residuals that look like expression and ask the user for a neutral photo 🧠
- **Deviation report.** Rank the |z| values and name them in art vocabulary ("wide-set eyes", "long lower third"). That turns the books into an explanation layer instead of a target 🧠

## Disagreements, myths & open questions

- **Eye line.** Placed at eye centres or at the lid bottoms [1] ↔️. Measured adults fall at half height or slightly below 🧠
- **Thirds and fifths.** These are teaching devices. Measured thirds are unequal [5], and "five eye-widths" depends on whether width means zy–zy or eu–eu ↔️
- **Mouth width.** Is it under the pupils, under the medial limbus, or about 1.5 eye-gaps? The data say narrower than the interpupillary distance [14] ↔️
- **Golden ratio and phi mask.** Unsupported as a target [15][16] ✅
- **Bridgman for beginners.** Practitioners split on whether he inspires or misleads 🧠 ↔️. Planar models help lighting and form, but likeness comes from measurement (Lanteri, Rubino) 🧠
- **Open questions:**
  - the exact Reilly line set
  - the current Asaro variants and their licence
  - the publication details of Zarins's expression book
  - the US renewal status of Loomis
  - measured values for Table 2 from ANSUR II, Farkas or 3DFN
  - any study comparing Asaro planes with the normals of scanned mean faces
