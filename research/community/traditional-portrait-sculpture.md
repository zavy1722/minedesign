# Traditional portrait sculpture methods

> Traditional clay portraiture gets likeness from two checks used together: measurement (calipers, dividers, plumb line and triangulation from a few bony landmarks, above all the ear holes, nasion, nose tip and chin) and contour (sitter and clay turned together, their silhouettes matched from 8–16 directions plus above and below: Rodin's "profiles"). Planes of the head (Asaro, Reilly) are for judging form under one light, not for measuring likeness, and the work goes big to small, all round at once, with features last. Nearly every studio move has an exact digital counterpart: calipers become distance constraints in mm, profile templates become silhouette residuals in solved perspective cameras, pointing becomes sparse correspondences plus interpolation, and Willème's photosculpture is a visual hull. What does not carry over is the live, re-posable sitter, so depth, crown and occiput fall to priors unless photos or caliper readings supply them. No harvest or draft existed, so every claim is expert knowledge awaiting verification (see also `community/likeness-mistakes.md`, `anatomy/anthropometric-norms.md`, `blender/sculpting-likeness-workflow.md`).

## Key facts

**The life sitting**
- Sitter and clay stand side by side on turntables, eye levels matched, so the eye moves between them at the same distance and angle, under one constant dominant light (north window or a single high side key) 🧠
- Poses last about 20–30 min; a life-size head takes a few to 20+ sittings ↔️. Expression drifts within a pose (jaw drops, lids lower, lips tense), so sculptors talk to the sitter and model a "living rest", never a held smile 🧠
- Armature: base, vertical pipe and head peg with wooden "butterflies"; the neck leans forward, not vertical. Water clay is hollowed and fired or moulded; oil clay (plastilina types) never dries and goes to a mould 🧠

**Measuring**
- Tools: bow calipers, large sliding or spreading calipers, proportional dividers, plumb line, level, depth pointer. Measure between bony, palpable points that expression does not move: vertex, glabella, nasion, nose tip, nose base, chin, ear holes (tragus notch), outer eye corners, cheekbones, jaw angles, occiput, pit of the neck. Mouth corners, lip line and hairline come last and are trusted least 🧠
- **The ear hole is the hub.** Three known distances fix a point in space, so the classic set runs from each ear hole to nasion, nose tip, nose base, chin, outer eye corner and mouth corner, left and right separately 🧠. Frontal photos cannot give these face depths 🧠
- Order: head height, length, breadth and ear-to-ear; then nasion–chin; then the ear-hole triangles; then feature widths 🧠
- Through hair, calipers press to the scalp. The skull is modelled first and hair added as mass on top 🧠
- Lanteri (*Modelling*, three volumes, early 1900s) and the academies measure systematically. Many teachers insist on "block in by eye, check with calipers", because heads built from numbers alone come out accurate but lifeless ↔️

**Contour ("profiles")**
- Rodin (conversations with Paul Gsell, *L'Art*, 1911) describes working by successive profiles, turning model and clay together, and treating every surface as the end of a volume, a lesson he credited to his early teacher Constant 🧠
- Practice: rotate both stands in equal steps (8 × 45°, refined to 16 × 22.5°), compare each silhouette against a plain ground, correct, rotate again 🧠. Then look from above (cranial plan, forehead curve, nose against cheeks, ear set-back) and from below (jaw, chin, neck). Both profiles are checked; they differ 🧠
- A silhouette is an occluding contour that slides across the surface as the view turns. In true lateral view the forehead, nose, lips and chin outline lies near the midline; the brow, cheek and jaw outlines do not 🧠

**Working from photographs**
- For posthumous or remote commissions, sculptors scale a profile photo to life size from one known measurement, trace it and cut a negative template (card, foamboard, acrylic) to offer to the clay; front and top templates likewise 🧠
- They ask for long-lens photos from several metres at eye level (front, both profiles, both 3/4s, back, top) plus hat size or a few caliper readings (`community/reference-photography-protocol.md`), and photograph the clay from the same position and lens to overlay or flick between 🧠
- Templates traced from close photos are wrong: perspective enlarges near features (nose tip, lips), and a few degrees of head turn change the outline 🧠

**Historical precursors (direct ancestors of the algorithms)**
- Alberti, *De Statua* (15th c.): the *exempeda* divides body height into 6 feet, 60 *unciae* and 600 *minuta*. The *finitorium* (a rotating arm over a graduated disc, with plumb lines) records points in cylindrical coordinates so a figure can be copied 🧠
- The pointing machine (late 18th–19th c.; inventor disputed) registers model and block on three fixed points, transfers hundreds to thousands of depth points, and the carver cuts between them; Canova's plasters still carry the marks 🧠 ↔️. Dividers, pantographs and Collas's machine (1830s) scaled copies 🧠
- Houdon took a life mask and caliper measurements of Washington at Mount Vernon (1785); Volk masked Lincoln in 1860 🧠. Masks fix bony structure but distort soft tissue: the subject lies down, eyes shut, and plaster weight compresses nose and brows 🧠
- Willème's photosculpture (1860s): about 24 cameras at 15° steps; each silhouette was traced by pantograph to cut the clay, then hand-finished. It was a visual hull 🧠 ↔️
- Forensic reconstruction builds clay to tissue-depth pegs glued on a skull, the same "pins to a measured depth" logic as a portraitist's reference pegs (`anatomy/forensic-reconstruction.md`) 🧠

**Planes**
- The Asaro head (John Asaro, Reilly tradition) is a cast planar head sold in several variants; under one light every value change reads as a plane change. The facets are artistic, not anatomical, and it is in copyright 🧠 ↔️
- Vocabulary: forehead front and sides, brow ridge, upper and lower orbit, cheek front and side, mouth barrel over the dental arch, nose bridge, sides, tip, wings and underside, chin box, jaw side and underside 🧠. The front-to-side turn runs from the outer brow over the cheekbone toward the jaw angle and sets how wide or flat the face reads 🧠
- Planes judge form and light; they are not a measuring system and do not carry likeness alone 🧠

**How masters check likeness**
- Big to small, all round at once; no feature is finished before masses and contours agree 🧠
- Step back 3–5 m, squint, half-light, mirror, overnight rest, greyscale photos of the clay, someone who knows the sitter; compare under the sitter's single light, then raking and moving light 🧠
- **Monochrome compensation.** Clay and marble lose the colour of brows, lashes, lips and iris. Bernini (Chantelou's diary, Paris, 1665) said marble sometimes needs what is not in the model, such as a hollow where the skin around the eye is dark 🧠 ↔️. Incised irises, drilled pupils, deeper sockets and crisper lid and lip edges serve the same end 🧠
- Bernini sketched Louis XIV while the king moved about his business, not in a fixed pose, to catch a living likeness 🧠

## Applying this to photo→3D face sculpting in Blender

1. **Rebuild the studio before sculpting.** Work at real scale (1 unit = 1 m), with the head in a Frankfort-horizontal, midline-centred frame and each photo as the background of its own solved perspective camera (`blender/reference-camera-setup.md`); each photo is the sitter frozen at one yaw and pitch. Treat a photo as an orthographic template only if shot from ≥3 m, where nose-versus-ear magnification falls to a few percent 🧠
2. **Take a five-minute caliper session if the person consents.** Use a 200–300 mm spreading or sliding caliper: t–t, t–n, t–sn and t–gn on both sides, n–gn, ex–ex and sn–prn. One reading fixes scale better than any prior, and the tragion triangles fix face depth, which frontal photos cannot 🧠
3. **Block in as the clay would.** Pose and scale first, then cranium, face block, jaw and neck masses against silhouettes and Tier A landmarks, then features. Do not touch eyelids or lips until the outer contours agree in every available camera 🧠
4. **Turn the contours.** Render flat silhouettes (Workbench: Flat lighting, Single colour black, white world) through each solved camera and compare them with the photo's mask region by region (forehead, nose, lips, chin, neck, occiput, hair). Views with no photo are prior-driven; do not sculpt them by guesswork 🧠
5. **Make profile templates the right way.** Draw the profile with Grease Pencil in the solved profile camera, then compare it with the model's projected occluding contour, not with its midline vertices. A 2–3° yaw error moves the nose and chin outline off the midline 🧠
6. **Triangulate from the ear holes.** Place empties at t_L and t_R and display the distances to n, prn, sn, sto, pg, ex and ch for each side. Left/right differences are the sitter's asymmetry, so keep them (`anatomy/facial-asymmetry.md`) 🧠
7. **Check planes under one light.** Use a single area light matched to the photo's key direction. Compare where the shadow edge falls on the cheekbone corner, temple, brow and mouth barrel, then rake the light to find lumps. Matcaps are for form, never for likeness 🧠
8. **Remember the monochrome effect.** A grey model lacks brow, lash, lip and iris colour, so it reads less like the person than the photo. Never distort shape for this if the output is textured; for an untextured print or cast, put compensation (incised iris and pupil, crisper lid and vermilion edges) on its own shape key 🧠
9. **Scale the output.** For fired clay or bronze, apply Table 3 and confirm the factor with the kiln or foundry 🧠
10. **What goes wrong:**
    - orthographic templates traced from close or slightly turned photos
    - measuring through hair, or anchoring on the hairline or lip line
    - finishing features before the masses agree
    - judging likeness only in matcap or only from the front
    - checking only one profile
    - treating the silhouette as the midline
    - a life mask, supine scan or smiling photo used as neutral shape
    - over-measuring into a dead, averaged head, or under-measuring until proportions drift

    🧠

## Algorithmic implications

- **Coarse-to-fine schedule, as the studio does it.** Stage 0: similarity pose and scale. Stage 1: masses (cranial ellipsoid, face block, jaw, neck; 10–20 parameters) fitted to silhouettes and Tier A landmarks. Stage 2: a low-order shape basis or planar proxy (≈100–300 faces). Stage 3: local features. Stage 4: detail. Advance a stage only when the mean silhouette chamfer is under about 2–3 mm in every available view 🧠
- **Landmark tiers.** Tier A, bony and stable (n, t, ex, en, prn, sn, pg, gn, zy, go): caliper σ 1–2 mm. Tier B, soft or expression-dependent (ch, sto, ls, li, al): 2–4 mm. Tier C, hair-dependent (v, eu, op, tr): excluded from equalities and used only as one-sided bounds 🧠
- **Caliper term.** `E_cal = Σ ((‖X_a − X_b‖ − d_ab − o_ab)/σ_ab)²`. Caliper data overrides population priors 🧠. `o_ab` corrects pressed readings (zy–zy, go–go, eu–eu) to the uncompressed surface; start at +2 to +5 mm for the pair ↔️
- **Silhouettes are the profile templates.** Occluding-contour correspondences must be recomputed every iteration, and weighted per view and per region. Store an explicit list of view directions with data; never let a missing view count as agreement 🧠
- **Visual-hull bound (Willème).** The head must lie inside every silhouette cone, so apply a hinge penalty only to model points outside a mask. A hair mask bounds the scalp from outside; it is not an equality 🧠
- **Pointing = registration + sparse warp + smooth fill.** Do a similarity or Procrustes alignment on ≥3 Tier A points (the three fixed points), then a TPS/RBF warp of the template from landmark and contour correspondences, then a Laplacian or bending regulariser ("carve between the points") 🧠
- **Depth priors from the ear triangles.** With frontal photos only, apply t–n, t–sn and t–gn priors (Table 1) with σ ≈ 6–8 mm, tightening to 1.5–2 mm when caliper data exist 🧠 ↔️
- **Coverage report.** Classify each Table 2 view as observed, inferred or prior-only. Default to 8 yaw views plus above; if a turntable video exists, sample every 22.5° 🧠
- **Caliper sheet UI.** Per pair: subject, model, Δ; flag Δ > 2 mm (Tier A) or > 3 mm (Tier B). It is the sculptor's measurement sheet, auditable by a human 🧠
- **Keep compensation out of identity.** Monochrome and expression layers are optional shape keys held at 0 during the fit; store `output_scale` and `material_shrink` in export metadata 🧠

## Disagreements, myths & open questions

- **Measure heavily or by eye?** Lanteri-style academies measure a lot; intuitive modellers measure only to check ↔️. The engine takes the second view: photos drive, priors and calipers check 🧠
- **Sight-size in sculpture.** Some ateliers adapt it, placing clay and sitter side by side and viewing them from a fixed spot; others reject it for 3D work ↔️
- **Where to start:** profile first, front first or all round from the start. Teachers differ ↔️
- **Expression:** a neutral rest or a lightly animated face. For identity fitting, use the neutral; for display, add expression as a layer ↔️
- **"Life-size looks small"** and the over-life convention: widely repeated, with factors that vary ↔️
- **Myth: the planes give the likeness.** They explain light, while likeness comes from proportion and contour 🧠
- **Myth: a profile photo is a profile template.** Not without distance and yaw control 🧠
- **Myth: a life mask is the true face.** Soft tissue is displaced and the eyes are closed 🧠
- **Historical details to verify:** the pointing machine's inventor, Willème's camera count, what Houdon measured, Bernini's exact wording, Rodin's credit to Constant ↔️
- **Open questions:** caliper measurement error in non-expert hands; compression offsets of pressed landmarks against 3D surfaces; Farkas values for the t-depths; any study scoring sculptors' clay accuracy against scans; useful view count beyond 16; current Asaro variants and licence
