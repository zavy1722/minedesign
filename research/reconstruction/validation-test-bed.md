# Ground-truth test bed and threshold calibration for the fitting engine

> The engine's accuracy can only be measured against heads whose shape is known independently, and its σ values, gates and thresholds can only be tuned on images that behave like users' phone photos. A four-tier test bed covers both: numpy synthetic scenes in CI; Blender renders of CC0/CC-BY scans pushed through a simulated phone pipeline and MediaPipe; a 3D-printed CC0 head photographed with real phones; and consenting sitters with a reference scan plus same-session photos. Licensed research sets (NoW validation, FaceScape, REALY, Stirling/ESRC) serve private benchmarking only. A coverage check run for this note on the repo's own bundle adjuster (Table 5) shows its posterior σ is honest for shape when σ_px is right, too pessimistic where the 8 mm prior dominates, 2–4× overconfident for absolute scale, and badly overconfident once landmark-definition bias exists. ## Key facts

**Ground truth you can actually get**
- Smithsonian Open Access (Feb 2020) put ≈2.8 M 2D/3D items under CC0; its 3D portal holds scans of Lincoln's life masks (Volk 1860, Mills 1865), shown since the 2013 "Smithsonian X 3D" launch. Rights are flagged per item. 🧠 CC0 download of each mask ↔️
- Life masks are face-only plaster casts: eyes closed, tissue loaded by plaster, no hair, no matching photographs. Good for nose, lips, chin and forehead; useless for lids, ears and cranium. 🧠
- Sketchfab uploaders pick CC0, BY, BY-SA, BY-ND, BY-NC, BY-NC-SA or BY-NC-ND. CC0 and BY suit fixtures; ND blocks publishing renders; NC assets stay out of a repo others use commercially. Sketchfab's store moved into Epic's Fab (launched Oct 2024), which mixes Fab licences with CC listings. 🧠 Current state ↔️
- CC 4.0 licences expressly do not license publicity, privacy or personality rights, and CC0 cannot waive the sitter's. A CC scan of a living person is not consent and remains personal data under GDPR (`ethics/consent-likeness-law.md`). 🧠
- 3D Scan Store (Ten24) and Triplegangers sell model-released scans and have offered free samples; as I recall their EULAs allow use in projects, forbid redistributing the scan, and Triplegangers forbids scraping and AI training. Keep samples private, never in git. ↔️
- NoW (MPI account, non-commercial): the 20-subject validation split ships neutral 3dMD scans with iPhone photos (neutral, expression, occlusion, selfie), the only public set pairing phone photos with a scan; the 80-subject test split is scored by submission. 🧠
- FaceScape (signed NC agreement, 847 of 938 subjects released, `face-scan-datasets.md`) gives calibrated DSLR multi-view images with meshes; REALY (≈100 Headspace scans, application) and Stirling/ESRC (Di3D, research request) give region-wise and in-the-wild protocols. Publish aggregate numbers only; never ship data, renders, detections or priors derived from them. 🧠 Whether a maintainer's evaluation is "non-commercial research" ↔️ (ask the licensor)
- **Inverse crime** (Kaipio & Somersalo): data generated by the solver's own model flatter it. `face3d.synthetic.random_subject` is a smooth polynomial warp of the prior's template (1.9 mm per-coordinate spread after similarity alignment, 4.4 % scale SD, measured here), so its errors are lower bounds. 🧠

**Synthetic photos**
- Phones: main ≈23–28 mm-equivalent, teles ≈48–125 mm, front ≈22–30 mm; at selfie distance the nose is magnified ≈1.3–1.5× relative to the ear plane (Table 3). 🧠
- The ISP is not "pinhole + noise": raw shot/read noise, demosaic, multi-frame denoise (smears lashes and lip lines), local tone mapping, sharpening halos, JPEG/HEIC. Simulate on linear renders after Brooks et al. 2019 ("unprocessing") and the Foi et al. 2008 Poisson–Gaussian model. 🧠
- Main cameras correct distortion in-pipeline to a small residual; some wide front and ultrawide pipelines apply face-preserving unwarping (Shih, Lai & Liang 2019), which no global camera model represents. 🧠 Which phones, when ↔️
- Blender traps: 35 mm-equivalence is diagonal-based (a 4:3 frame's equivalent width is 34.6 mm, not 36); `sensor_fit = AUTO` applies the sensor to the longer side, silently changing focal in portrait renders; cameras look down −Z, +Y up. `face3d/geometry/camera.py` handles all three; the round-trip test below proves it. 🧠
- MediaPipe Face Landmarker: 478 points regressed from a ≈256 px crop, so error scales with face size, not megapixels (the rationale for `AUTO_SIGMA_FRACTION_OF_DIAG`); 3–4.5 % IOD NME class, reliable to ≈±45° yaw, no visibility flags, far-side oval hallucinated (`landmark-detection.md`). ↔️
- **Domain gap:** I know no published figure for MediaPipe on renders of scans. Photoreal synthetic faces with hair, eyes and clothing train detectors to real-data accuracy (Wood et al. 2021), so good renders can be close; bare-scan or plaster renders with missing eyes degrade lids, brows and lips. Measure it (Applying, step 3). ↔️

**Reference capture**
- 3dMD/Di3D: nominal ≈0.2 mm, live repeat ≈0.2–0.5 mm (verified in `benchmarks-metrics.md`). Handheld structured light (Artec, EinScan, Revopoint): ≈0.05–0.1 mm claimed on rigid targets, but 20–90 s sweeps let a face move, ≈0.3–1 mm. TrueDepth apps ≈0.5–2 mm, face only. Rear LiDAR is centimetre-class. Single-camera walk-around photogrammetry of a person: 1–3 mm motion error. ↔️
- Two scans ten minutes apart give the floor; differences below ≈1.5× it are unresolvable. 🧠

**Statistics**
- Test σ in a gauge-free frame. Perspective photos fix a head only up to similarity; the prior chooses one, so raw-frame z mixes in unobservable error (RMS 1.7–3.2 here). Similarity-align, then project the covariance with P = I − G(GᵀG)⁻¹Gᵀ, G the 3N×7 similarity tangent. 🧠
- Correctly specified, the aligned 5-view coverage is 0.69/0.96 at 1σ/2σ with NEES/dof 0.98: the Gauss–Newton covariance is honest, and NEES/dof scales as (true/declared σ_px)², so it calibrates σ_px directly. 🧠
- The subject (and session) is the unit of independence: bootstrap over subjects, compare settings with common random numbers and paired tests. 🧠
- Without a scan, two photo sessions give test–retest precision, and Bland–Altman agreement of caliper distances gives bias and limits of agreement. 🧠

**How open projects evaluate**
- DECA and MICA score through NoW's separate evaluator and hidden-test submission; MICA adds a metric track. eos (Apache-2.0 C++ with Python bindings) ships unit tests and wheels; its accuracy lives in papers. Metrical Tracker and VHAP (FLAME trackers) are judged by landmark/photometric residuals and held-out-camera views on multi-view sets such as NeRSemble. None runs accuracy regressions in CI. 🧠 Licences, CI status ↔️

## Applying this to photo→3D face sculpting in Blender

1. **Tiers answer different questions.** Tier 1 (numpy scenes): is the solver mathematically right? Tier 2 (renders): which σ_px and gates suit MediaPipe under phone optics? Tier 3 (printed head): what do real lenses and ISPs add with geometry known exactly? Tier 4 (sitters): what does a living, moving person add? Freeze a tier's tuning before moving up. 🧠
2. **Render recipe.** `blender -b -P render_set.py`; Cycles, clean render plus OpenImageDenoise, written as linear EXR. Export K, the OpenCV-convention pose, projected GT landmarks with `BVHTree` ray-cast visibility, an object-index silhouette and depth. Add noise, ISP and JPEG in numpy afterwards, so one render yields many degradations. Give scans eyeballs (iris, wet cornea), brows and lashes; MediaPipe reads texture. 🧠
3. **Measure the domain gap.** Solve the camera on a real tier-3 or tier-4 photo, render the scan from that camera, run MediaPipe on both, and report per-landmark paired differences in % IOD. Regions where the gap exceeds ≈0.5 % IOD are tuned on real data only. 🧠
4. **Print-in-the-loop.** Print a CC0 head at 1:1, paint it matte skin tone with printed iris decals, re-scan the print (prints warp), and photograph it with real phones: real optics, no motion, no consent issue. 🧠
5. **Sitter protocol.** Signed consent covering scans, photos, retention, deletion and whether aggregate results are published. Then, all within 30 minutes:
   - swim cap, no glasses, makeup unchanged;
   - two reference scans;
   - caliper zy–zy, eu–eu, g–op and n–gn;
   - ChArUco calibration of every phone lens at locked focus;
   - photos: 9 yaws −90…+90° at 1–1.5 m on main and 2× lenses, three arm's-length selfies, two lighting setups, both orientations. Portrait mode, beauty filters and front-camera mirroring off; originals kept with EXIF.
   Two annotators mark scan landmarks twice each, for inter- and intra-observer error. 🧠
6. **Report.**
   - per-region signed scan-to-mesh after landmark-only, NoW-style and REALY-style alignment (`benchmarks-metrics.md`);
   - leave-one-view-out reprojection: re-solve without view k, pose k by PnP on a stable subset (canthi, nasion, subnasale), score the remaining landmarks and the silhouette in % IOD;
   - scale error, test–retest RMS, Bland–Altman on the caliper distances.
   Ablate by oracle substitution (true 3D landmarks → template-warp floor; true 2D projections → solver floor; detections → full pipeline) to see where error enters. 🧠
7. **What goes wrong:** unit or axis mismatches; mirrored selfies; HEIC conversion dropping EXIF; phones switching a "2×" shot to a crop of the main sensor; scan hair and closed eyes scored as error; tuning and testing on the same people. 🧠

## Algorithmic implications

- **`face3d/eval/coverage.py`**: `gauge_project(X, C)`, `z_scores`, `nees` and a reliability curve (empirical vs nominal coverage at α = 0.1…0.9). This requires `bundle_adjust` to return the full 3N×3N point covariance, not only `point_sigma_mm`. Strengthen `test_point_uncertainty_reflects_coverage` to assert aligned coverage 0.6–0.76 at 1σ and NEES/dof 0.8–1.25 over ≥30 seeded trials. 🧠
- **Add a scale parameter** with a log-normal prior (σ ≈ 0.045 until verified) and keep iid landmark σ for shape. An iid isotropic prior implies ≈σ/(√N·r̄) scale freedom (≈2 % at 8 mm, 32 points), half the real spread, so absolute size is overconfident (Table 5). A low-rank (PCA) covariance from licensed scans is the long-term fix. 🧠
- **σ_def floor:** add per-landmark definition-bias variance (0.5–2 mm, landmark-specific) to the posterior covariance, or model it as a per-landmark 3D offset shared across views. Otherwise σ shrinks as 1/√views while the bias stays, and Table 5 shows z blowing up. 🧠
- **σ_px**: after each fit, publish the reduced χ² (2·cost/(m − n)). Calibrate `AUTO_SIGMA_FRACTION_OF_DIAG` so its median over tier-2/4 sets is ≈1, with per-landmark multipliers (inner anchors ≈0.5–0.7×, oval ≈1.5–2×, `landmark-detection.md`). Inflate the covariance by max(1, χ²_red) when reporting. 🧠
- **Gates from quantiles, not taste:** landmark outlier at 3.03σ (1 % false alarm, 2D); view rejected when reduced χ² exceeds its 99 % bound (Table 4); a final IRLS round with a redescending loss (Geman–McClure or Tukey) or hard rejection, because Huber leaves gross outliers bounded but non-zero influence (Table 5, last row). 🧠
- **Units:** express pixel thresholds (`max_dist_px`) in IOD or `sigma_px` units, as `huber_sigmas` already is. 🧠
- **Tuning order:** σ ratios set the MAP estimate, so tune ratios for accuracy (median S2M, configuration RMS); then set the global σ scale for coverage. Screen ~12 parameters with Morris elementary effects (≈20 trajectories, 260 runs); run Sobol' indices on the top 4–5; validate leave-one-subject-out on real sets. 🧠
- **CI tiers:**
  - Tier 1, every push, numpy only: fixed seeds; assert medians against stored baselines ± 3 MAD over 30 seeds, with paired comparisons to limit flakiness.
  - Tier 2, nightly: cached detection JSON from CC0 renders committed with licence and attribution files.
  - Tiers 3–4, before each release on a private machine: aggregate metrics only.
  Store the protocol version, licence hashes and the engine git SHA with every result. 🧠

## Disagreements, myths & open questions

- **Myth: "more views always shrink error bars honestly."** Only if landmark semantics match the anatomy; bias makes more views more confidently wrong. 🧠
- **Myth: "a CC0 scan is free to use as a face."** Copyright yes; personality and data-protection rights no. ↔️ (jurisdiction-dependent)
- **Renders vs reality** ↔️: synthetic-only training succeeds (Wood et al.), yet off-the-shelf detectors on bare scans fail; the gap is per-region and unmeasured publicly.
- **Prior σ** ↔️: 8 mm protects unusual faces but over-covers where photos are weak; 2–4 mm population values risk pulling distinctive features to the mean (`face-perception-likeness.md`).
- **Whole-face vs region alignment** ↔️ (`benchmarks-metrics.md`).
- **Open:** a published MediaPipe-on-render error; current Smithsonian, Sketchfab/Fab and vendor terms; whether NoW validation images carry usable EXIF; head-size CV to use as scale prior; a public phone-photo + scan set under a permissive licence.
